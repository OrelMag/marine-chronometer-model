# What we'd ask a Model 21 owner or the NAWCC for

**Since 14 October 2026 none of these holds up the design** (OPEN-QUESTIONS.md, "Closed by design"): each is settled by a design value the checks pass and a bench test that makes the made part right. The answers would make the model truer to the original Model 21, not make a chronometer built to it run better; item 3 (the free run-down) is now also a bench test any maker runs on their own balance.

A post to send, for the readings no video, photograph or the 1948 manual settles (OPEN-QUESTIONS.md, `References/VIDEOS.md`). Each item says what to photograph or measure and why. A phone photograph is enough when it is square to the part and has a ruler, or a coin of known size, beside it.

---

**Measurements of a Hamilton Model 21 wanted for an open working model**

I'm building a working 3D model of the Hamilton Model 21 from the 1948 overhaul manual (NAVSHIPS 250-624) and teardown videos: https://www.marinechronometermodel.com. A few sizes can't be read from any source I've found. If you have a Model 21 apart for service, any one of these would help, and I'd credit you on the site.

1. **The barrel and mainspring:** the barrel's diameter outside and inside, with a caliper (the British Museum's catalogue gives 38 mm inside, which disagrees with the videos' scale); the mainspring's thickness with a micrometer, its width and, if it's out, its length (the catalogue gives 0.44 × 15.0 mm on a late example, the parts list 0.0165 in); the barrel arbor's diameter where the spring hooks (the catalogue: 13 mm).
2. **The detent:** the thickness of its spring's two strips and of the trip (passing) spring (their widths and lengths are known), or a side-on photograph of the detent out of the movement with a scale beside it.
3. **The balance's free run-down:** with the train held by the train-blocking screw and the balance given a full swing, a video of it swinging down for a minute or two, filmed square from above. (A start filmed from the twist to a steady swing helps too; a timegrapher's amplitude and beat error even more. The one start filmed so far, on one movement, builds up as if its balance ran down in 100-160 s and settles near 300°, where the model takes 67 s and 255°: a second movement would tell whether that's the Model 21 or that one.)
4. **The fusee with the chain off:** its large end, showing where the chain's hook goes into the groove's lowest turn and where the groove ends.
5. **The winding ratchet** on the fusee's large end, face-on and close, ideally with a scale: its teeth's depth.
6. **The hairspring collet** off its staff, from below, or the spring's inner end seen from under the balance: the collet's body under its sector plate.
7. **The fourth wheel** lying flat beside a ruler, its diameter over the teeth, or the distance between the fourth and escape arbors' holes on the bare plate (the third wheel is now measured).
8. **The set-up ratchet** (on the barrel arbor under its cover), off its arbor and face-on, still and in focus: whether its locking faces are radial or slightly undercut.
9. **The discharge jewel** on the balance staff's unlocking roller, looking along the staff: the shape of its tip (the British Museum's catalogue: "a radius to the back of the acting part").
10. **The fusee's sustaining ratchet with the two winding-pawl springs on**, face-on.
11. **The balance cock off the movement**, its top and its underside each square to the camera with a ruler beside it: where the screw's counterbore and the two steady pins stand against the straight edge and the balance's setting.
12. **The sustaining spring out of its wheel** (the steel split ring in the fusee wheel's recess): one photograph of it lying flat beside a ruler, and one of its edge with a caliper's reading of its height and of its band's width where it is narrowest and widest. Better still, its stiffness: the fusee wheel held, the force at the working end's pin that closes its gap by a degree.
13. **The balance locking arm fitted** (on movements overhauled after 1947, Sec. X): one photograph from above with the arm locked over a timing weight, and one with it swung clear: where its screw stands on the plate, how far it turns, and its end (Figs. 9 and 108 draw an eye standing up from the strip over the weight; the model's finger and place are a fit for room).

Doug Sinclair's and William O. Bennett's NAWCC articles on the Model 21 (Nos. 365 and 356) give no sizes; the Bulletin's other pages behind the members' login (Smith, No. 395, pp. 60-63) may answer 1 and 2; if you can read them, a note of the figures they give would help too. If you have Hamilton's drawings of the Model 21 (the NAWCC Library holds them in the Hamilton Records Collection, MG-335: "Chronometer Parts" 1943-1951, Box 14, Folder 6; the mainspring drawings, Box 7, Folders 7-8), the mainspring's, the barrel's and the detent's sheets would settle 1 and 2 outright.

---

# What we'd ask about the Wempe Einheits-Chronometer

**None of these holds up the Wempe's design either** (16 October 2026): each part below is built to a design value the checks pass, with a bench test for a maker (`wempe/README.md`, "Estimated, not from the drawings"; `tools/wempe_bench.js`). The answers would make the model truer to the drawings.

The 124 works drawings the Wempe model is built from are those published on dg-chrono.de, © Johannes Altmeppen, by courtesy of Gerhard D. Wempe KG (`References/wempe/README.md`). A few sheets the parts lists name aren't among them. Send the post to Johannes Altmeppen through the Deutsche Gesellschaft für Chronometrie (dg-chrono.de, its forum or its contact address); its book, *Das Deutsche Einheits-Chronometer* (Altmeppen, Dittrich; Heel Verlag), may print some of these sheets. A collector or restorer with an Einheits-Chronometer (Wempe, Lange, GUB, Kirova) apart could answer 2 and 3 with a photograph.

Ich baue nach den auf dg-chrono.de veröffentlichten Werkzeichnungen ein laufendes 3D-Modell des Wempe-Einheits-Chronometers: https://www.marinechronometermodel.com/wempe/. Jedes Teil ist nach seinem Blatt gezeichnet; die Quellen und ihr Copyright sind auf der Seite genannt. Einige Blätter, die die Stücklisten nennen, fehlen in der veröffentlichten Reihe. Wenn Sie eines davon haben oder eines dieser Fotos machen können, würde das sehr helfen, und ich würde Sie auf der Seite nennen.

1. **Die Blätter der Stellungssteg-Gruppe 05 06 03:** 05 06 03-1 (Stellungssteg), 05 06 03-2 (Stift) und vor allem 05 06 03-3 (Stegfeder): die Höhe und Stärke der Feder. Schnitt C-D auf 05 06_4 zeigt nur ihre Form in der Draufsicht; im farbigen Gesamtschnitt ist sie nicht geschnitten.
2. **Das kleine Ende einer Schnecke mit abgenommener Deckplatte** (05 06 00-4, eine Schraube M1,7): ein Foto schräg von der Seite, sodass man die Stegfeder an der Wand der Ausdrehung (1,6 tief) sieht, und eines senkrecht von oben, möglichst mit einem Maßstab daneben.
3. **Das Gangende N der Schneckenrille** (05 06 04-2): das kleine Ende ohne Kette, senkrecht von oben, sodass man sieht, wo der oberste Gang in die Nabe ausläuft, im Verhältnis zum Schlitz des Stellungsstegs.
4. **Weitere Teile ohne Blatt in der Reihe:** 05 05 05-2 (Gegensperrwelle), 05 05 06-1 (Feder, der Drahtfeder des Gegensperrkegels), 05 08 01-5 (Anschlagstift), 05 08 01-4 (Korkring), 05 02 01-2 (Zeigerfutter des Sekundenzeigers), 05 01 00-4 (Feststellkloben) und 05 01 00-7 (Verschlußfeder): für diese ein Foto des Gehäusebodens mit abgenommener Verschlußplatte, das die Feder in der Nut der Platte oder am Boden zeigt.
5. **Die Hemmung eines Wempe nach 1946 von der Seite** (mit der Unruhwelle 05 04 02 vom 29.5.46): ein Foto zwischen den Platinen, waagerecht auf das Gangrad, den Gangfederkloben (05 04 01-4) und die Begrenzungsschraube mit ihrem gewölbten Kopf, möglichst mit einem Maßstab; oder ein späteres Blatt der Gangfeder oder des Klobens. Die Höhe der Gangfeder unter der Oberplatte passt nach den Blättern nicht zugleich zum Kloben und zur Auslöserolle der Welle von 1946; die fotografierten Werke (Kirow DEC 422, 479; 6MX) zeigen die frühere Anordnung.

Vielen Dank!

# What we'd ask for a third model (Ulysse Nardin or Daini Seikosha)

**Nothing is built on these.** A survey on 16 October 2026 (`References/wempe/photos/catalogue-2026-10-16.md`, "Not for the Wempe"; E:\mc-video\third-model-survey\SURVEY.md) found no dimensioned source for any Swiss or Japanese marine chronometer: overall sizes, oblique photographs and one amateur 720p take-apart of a Nardin, against the Hamilton's dimensioned manual and the Wempe's 124 works drawings. The Daini Seikosha (1942-45) was copied from Ulysse Nardin's two-day chronometer with parts interchangeable, and Greenwich calls the Model 21 Hamilton's version of Nardin's standard M.G.F., so a source for any one of the three would serve the Nardin pattern. These are the requests that could change the verdict.

---

**1. Ulysse Nardin, heritage department (Le Locle)**

*Français:* **Plans d'atelier d'un chronomètre de marine Ulysse Nardin, pour un modèle de travail ouvert.** Je construis des modèles 3D en fonctionnement de chronomètres de marine, chaque pièce tracée d'après une source mesurée : le Hamilton Model 21 d'après son manuel de révision de 1948 (https://www.marinechronometermodel.com) et le chronomètre unifié allemand de Wempe d'après ses plans d'atelier (https://www.marinechronometermodel.com/wempe/). Le Model 21 fut conçu en 1941 d'après votre chronomètre de deux jours ; j'aimerais modéliser l'original. Existe-t-il dans vos archives des plans d'atelier, des cotes ou des instructions de révision du chronomètre de marine de deux jours (calibre M.G.F. ou ses successeurs, années 1900-1950) : la platine et les ponts, l'échappement à détente ressort (roue, détente, rouleaux), la fusée et sa chaîne, le balancier et son spiral ? Même une seule feuille cotée de l'échappement serait précieuse. Je citerais l'archive sur le site, et rien ne serait publié sans votre accord.

*English:* **Works drawings of an Ulysse Nardin marine chronometer, for an open working model.** I build running 3D models of marine chronometers, every part traced from a measured source: the Hamilton Model 21 from its 1948 overhaul manual and Wempe's German standard chronometer from its works drawings. The Model 21 was designed in 1941 after your two-day chronometer, and I'd like to model the original. Do your archives hold works drawings, dimensions or overhaul instructions for the two-day marine chronometer (the M.G.F. calibre or its successors, 1900s-1950s): the plates and bridges, the spring detent escapement (wheel, detent, rollers), the fusee and chain, the balance and hairspring? Even one dimensioned sheet of the escapement would help. I'd credit the archive on the site, and publish nothing without your agreement.

**2. NAWCC Library and Research Center** (research@nawcc.org): the Hamilton Watch Company Records Collection, Series V ("Chronometers"): Hamilton's research reports and drawings from 1940-42, in particular any study or measurement of the Ulysse Nardin two-day chronometer Hamilton examined before designing the Model 21 (JCK reports Hamilton "studied ... the Nardin model" in 1941). A list of the folders in Series V would show whether such a study survives; copies of any drawing of the Nardin's escapement, fusee or balance would carry a model.

**3. A library lookup: Jonathan Betts, *Marine Chronometers at Greenwich* (OUP 2017).** Every instrument in the catalogue was dismantled and measured. Does it have an Ulysse Nardin two-day chronometer (or the Seikosha, ZAA0268), and which measurements does its entry tabulate (plate, balance, escape wheel, fusee, train counts)?

**4. Seiko Museum Ginza** (セイコーミュージアム 銀座), on the Daini Seikosha marine chronometer No. 1001 in its collection:

*日本語:* **第二精工舎製 船用経線儀（マリンクロノメーター）の図面・寸法について。** 私は実在のマリンクロノメーターを、実測資料（整備マニュアル、工作図面、写真、動画）だけに基づいて3Dの動く模型として再現しています（ハミルトン Model 21：https://www.marinechronometermodel.com、ドイツのヴェンペ統一クロノメーター：https://www.marinechronometermodel.com/wempe/）。貴館所蔵の第二精工舎製経線儀（1942年、海軍艦政本部向け、ユリス・ナルダン2日巻を範とするもの）について、製作図面、部品寸法、整備要領書などの資料は残っておりますでしょうか。特に脱進機（ガンギ車、デテント、ローラー）、円錐滑車（フュジー）と鎖、テンプとヒゲゼンマイの寸法が分かれば大変助かります。ご協力いただけた場合はサイト上で貴館のお名前を記し、ご了承なしに公開することはいたしません。

*English:* **Drawings or dimensions of the Daini Seikosha marine chronometer.** The same project; does the museum hold works drawings, part dimensions or maintenance instructions for its Daini Seikosha marine chronometer (1942, for the Navy's technical department, after Ulysse Nardin's two-day chronometer): above all the escapement (escape wheel, detent, rollers), the fusee and chain, the balance and hairspring?

**5. National Diet Library** (国立国会図書館): *測器要報* (the Meteorological Instrument Works' journal, 1956-64, PID 1869363), readable only in the library or through its transmission service in Japan: any article on the Works' marine chronometer (Gunji Okada, 岡田群司; the trial chronometer of 1939, production from 1944) with figures or dimensions. Also Haruyama's full article (NAWCC Bulletin, October 2000, pp. 581-586) on the Seikosha, for its figures.

Where each answer goes: a dimensioned source for the Nardin pattern (1-4) decides whether a third model can be built to the standard of the other two (CLAUDE.md, "Build from measured lines"), and which: the Nardin itself or the Seikosha; 5 bears on a Japanese chronometer of its own design.

**Fotos zum 6MX (Kirow Nr. 247), für das offene Arbeitsmodell**

Auf https://www.marinechronometermodel.com/wempe/ ist der 6MX jetzt eine dritte Fassung des Einheits-Chronometers, nach Ihren Fotos von Kirow Nr. 247 gebaut. Inzwischen ist das meiste aus Ihrer Liste nach Ihren Fotos dieses und der anderen 6MX der Galerie gebaut, auch der Sekundenkontakt; einige Teile sind nur von der Seite zu sehen.
