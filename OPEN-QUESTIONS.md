# Open questions: what no source has settled

As of 12 October 2026 (release 2.24.00; researched again that day: the British Museum's catalogue, 17 more videos, new measurements on the videos). The model is built from the 1948 overhaul manual (NAVSHIPS 250-624), photographs of real Model 21s and videos of real Model 21s; where they disagree with the model, the model is wrong (CLAUDE.md, "Source of truth"). These are the readings that every source found so far leaves open. Each is drawn as an estimate meanwhile and listed in the model README's "Estimated, not from the manual". The full working record of each is its row in `SHAPE-PASS.md` (row numbers below); the sources are in `References/README.md` and `References/VIDEOS.md`.

The goal they are judged against: someone with only the site can make a Model 21 that keeps time. Two questions matter for that; the rest are appearance.

## Critical

### 1. The mainspring's thickness, and with it the arbor's core

**What is known.** The barrel is measured: r 18.3 ± 0.5 outside, its wall 2.0 thick (SHAPE-PASS 109-6). The spring is 13.55 mm wide (measured, 109-4) and 0.0165 in (0.419 mm) thick by the parts list. Its length, 1,064 mm, is estimated (the half-room rule; the videos give 0.9-1.25 m).

**The conflict.** On two movements (KLUwI2UUCMQ 15:54; TN24H `3SFDplGq6vs` 0:16) the let-down spring's packed coils are 0.375 mm apart, which a 0.419 mm strip can't pack to. And the barrel arbor's core reads r 4.9-5.6 on two movements (KLUwI2UUCMQ 33:20.5, TN24H 0:16), against the model's estimated r 1.8. On a core of r 5.2 the parts list's strip is coil-bound 4.75 turns past its slack, short of the fusee's 4.67 turns and a set-up of about 1: it can't be wound fully. A strip as thin as the measured pitch would fit. So either those two springs are thinner than the parts list's (replacements), or the coils counted were not one a peak, or the core reading is off.

**Why it matters.** The spring's thickness sets its torque (as its cube), the fusee's pull, the train's energy budget and so the balance's amplitude (`tools/physics.js`). The core sets how many turns the barrel holds.

**Searched without result.** Hamilton's material catalogues of 1947-1953 ("materials for all war timepieces ... require separate inquiry direct to the Material Sales Department"; their mainspring chart lists watch sizes only); supplier and cross-reference tables; Google Patents; museum catalogues (RMG, Smithsonian NMAH, Ingenium).

**What would settle it.** A micrometer reading of a Model 21 mainspring's thickness (and its length and width), or of the arbor's core diameter; or Doug Sinclair's article (below).

**Found (12 October 2026): a museum's measurements.** A. G. Randall and R. Good, *Catalogue of Watches in the British Museum VI: Pocket Chronometers, Marine Chronometers and Other Portable Precision Timekeepers* (British Museum Publications, 1990), cat. no. 117A, pp. 127-128, Plates 78-79 (a public scan in the Internet Archive's `horology-books-and-catalogs`; the figures checked on the page images), a Model 21 numbered ZE 10228 (BM 1988,4-10,2), "finished especially for" Hamilton UK's managing director "from the last parts available at the factory" (the BM dates it 1959-65: a late assembly, not wartime production): "The solid steel arbor snailed and 13 mm in diameter. *Barrel*: Inside dim. 38 mm, internal available h. 15·3 mm, mainspring h. 15·0 mm, th. 0·44 mm. The mainspring fitted with a brace." No length. A curator's measurements of one example: above a restorer's reading, below the manual.

What it settles and what it leaves:
- **The thickness is the parts list's, not the coils' pitch read in millimetres.** 0.44 mm (0.0173 in) is 5 % over the parts list's 0.0165 in (0.419) and 12-17 % over the 0.375-0.394 the videos' pitch gives on their barrels' scale: two sources, independent of each other, put it at 0.42-0.44.
- **The core is large.** The arbor 13 mm across (r 6.5), against the model's estimated r 1.8; the videos read r 4.9-5.6 on their scale, and the spring's eye at 33:06 is formed for about r 5 (References/VIDEOS.md, 12 October 2026).
- **The museum's proportions are the videos'.** Inside radius over thickness 19.0 / 0.44 = 43.2 (the videos' pitch 1/41.0-1/43.7 of the inside radius: KLUwI2UUCMQ 15:54, 33:06, TN24H 0:16); the core over the inside radius 0.34 (the videos 0.30-0.34); the spring's height over the inside radius 0.79 (the videos 13.55 / 16.3 = 0.83). So the coils counted are one a stripe, as the pitch read round the whole turn found (12 October 2026), and what disagrees is the absolute size of the barrel.
- **The conflict is now the barrel's size.** The model's barrel, r 18.3 ± 0.5 outside and 16.3 inside, comes from TN24H's cap flat beside the fusee wheel (1.009 of its tips, r 18.09) and fits the train bridge's cut round it (r 19.2); the museum's inside alone, r 19.0, is larger than that outside. Either its "inside dim. 38 mm" is the barrel's outside (r 19.0, just inside the bridge's cut; then the inside, about r 17, gives 0.44 a pitch of 1/38.6 of it, 10 % coarser than the coils counted), or the model's barrel and fusee wheel are drawn 10-17 % small. In the museum's barrel the strip has the room: an ideal annulus of r 6.5 to 19.0 with a 0.44 strip of about 1,190 mm (the videos' L/t about 2,700) winds about 6.6 turns, about 5.2 at the solver's realism (the model's needs 4.67 and a set-up of about 1); in the model's barrel no strip of 0.42-0.44 on a core of r 5-6.5 does.
- **The width:** 15.0 in 15.3 of room, against the 13.55 read on the video against its barrel (13.4-15.8).

**What would settle it now:** the barrel's diameter, inside and out, measured on a real Model 21 (a caliper on the barrel, or a frame with the barrel beside a rule), and a second measured mainspring; Sinclair's article (below).

**In the model meanwhile.** The parts list's 0.419 strip, 1,064 long, on the estimated core r 1.8; the spring's lie solved in the measured barrel (`tools/mainspring.py`): slack at 13.5 turns, set up 1.0, let down it packs about 10½ turns against the videos' 12 ± 1.

### 2. The detent's and the trip spring's sections

**What is known.** Their plan and form follow the manual's Fig. 90 and the photographs, confirmed by the Royal Museums Greenwich catalogue (ZBA7849: "a beryllium-copper (?) detent mounted on a nickel block ... a bifurcated spring and a screwed-on, Elinvar passing spring cranked out to run parallel to the detent blade"). Their thicknesses and heights are not known (110-7).

**Why it matters.** The detent spring's and the trip spring's thicknesses set their stiffness, and so the force and work of unlocking; without them a maker can't cut a detent that works. The model's escapement meets the manual's adjustment figures (`tools/escapement.js`), and its detent spring's share comes from Op. 78's test, but the sections themselves are estimated.

**A related conflict (110-10).** One reading puts the trip spring's bracket leg about 2 mm further along the detent (KLUwI2UUCMQ 11:08-11:13, and Fig. 90 agrees), but the trip spring it holds measures 8.6 ± 0.6 mm from clamp to tip (11:26) against the model's 7.9; moving the leg 2 mm would cut it to 5.9. Both readings are rough; the bracket stays as drawn.

**Searched without result.** Every frame of the restoration video near the escapement (10:27-11:33); TN24H 0:11-0:12 (the assembly flat, 8.6 px/mm: the blades under a pixel); Deafboy's and pefkipefki's running videos (blurred there); bunnspecial's `bEWF2ivi1Rg` and `wuobTVulVGY`; Google Patents (no Hamilton detent patent); Dewey Clark's NAWCC post (one screw holds the trip spring on its Z-shaped bracket; no sizes); chronometerbook's "typically about 0.04 mm thick by 2 mm wide", which is for chronometers in general, not the Model 21.

**Found (12 October 2026):** A. G. Randall and R. Good, *Catalogue of Watches in the British Museum VI: Pocket Chronometers, Marine Chronometers and Other Portable Precision Timekeepers* (British Museum Publications, 1990), cat. no. 117A, pp. 127-128, Plates 78-79, the late Model 21 ZE 10228 (above): "The detent built up on nickel silver block which is steady pinned and screwed and made so as to provide an adjustable stop for the detent; this extended portion carries a gold stop against which the block that carries the locking jewel banks. The detent is wide - 3·00 mm - and the spring part is composed of two narrow strips each 0·5 mm wide and 3·5 mm long. The passing spring is supported by a bracket some 2·4 mm in height, the horn being bent and with a nib for the spring to bank against. The locking stone D-shaped and pinned into its hole in a square section portion of the detent. ... The detent gilded." Against the model: the two strips' widths (0.50 and 0.45 drawn, `hD` 0.95) and length (3.82 drawn to the point of flexure) agree within 10 %; the bracket's 2.4 mm height supports the video's taller reading of its upright leg (0.8-1.9, 11:08-11:13) over the drawn 0.4, and so the leg further along (110-10); which of the detent's sizes "wide, 3·00 mm" is (its depth along the arbors, likely) isn't said. **Still not given: either spring's thickness**, so the stiffness stays estimated (with the width and length now measured, the thickness is the one unknown: `tools/tolerances.js`'s range stands).

**What would settle it.** A close side-on photograph of a Model 21 detent out of its movement with a scale beside it, or measured thicknesses; or the sources below.

## Meanwhile: how closely they must be made

`tools/tolerances.js` works out, from the model's own escapement, how far each of these can be off before the balance leaves the manual's running swing (1⅜-1½ turns, 247.5-270°), and the maker's sheets of the three lines (and so the build book) say so:

- **The mainspring:** its stiffness E b t³/12 L within −5 % to +11 % of the drawn spring's: 0.412-0.433 mm thick at the drawn 13.55 width and 1,064 length, about 0.89-1.03 N·m fully wound (0.85-1.00 until 11 October 2026, the fusee's torque then on the groove's floor). A 0.375 mm strip of the same length swings the balance only 210°: it runs, below the manual's figure. So the manual's swing, with the model's train and balance, favours the parts list's 0.0165 in over the coils' measured pitch (the balance's damping and the train's efficiency are estimated: physics.js gives their ranges).
- **The detent spring:** up to about 5 times the drawn stiffness (0.139 mm thick; the drawn 0.079) the balance keeps 247.5°; there is no lower limit from its work. Its preload is set by Op. 78's test. A thinner spring still returns the detent in time (`tools/dynamics.js`, 11 October 2026): from half to twice the drawn stiffness, and with no preload at all, the detent is back on its stop about 10 ms before the next tooth reaches the stone.
- **The trip spring:** up to about 2.6 times the drawn stiffness (0.082 mm; the drawn 0.06): it is bent far, round the discharge jewel's end, on every return swing.

## Found by integrating the escapement in time (11 October 2026)

`tools/dynamics.js` integrates the escapement beat by beat (model README, "Checked in time"). What it found is now in the model; two readings it rests on stay open:

- **The discharge jewel's end.** Drawn square, its 0.32 mm end held both springs' tips across its whole width and overall came to about 38°, past Op. 87's 26-30°. The model now stones it to a half round (`rnd`) and its settings (reach, trip spring's tip, angle, depth of lock) were re-chosen to the manual's figures: overall 27.8°. No source shows the end. Real recordings (11 October 2026, `tools/acoustic.py`) hear the return swing's trip-spring clicks, but where they fall against the active swing's depends on each movement's beat error (5-12° in the fits), so they don't test the end's shape. **Seen since (12 October 2026), a conflict:** KLUwI2UUCMQ 6:58.00-6:58.33 (4K, the balance out, a three-quarter view toward the pivot) and 7:08.00 (broadside) show the jewel whole: a flat ruby slab in the roller's slot, its outer edge straight along the axis with square corners at both ends, shellac at its root. Profiled from its outer corner at 6:58.17 and 6:58.33, the face toward the pivot narrows straight to a sharp corner (about 5, 8, 12, 18 px wide at 3, 9, 12, 18 px from it), where a half round of half the thickness would curve in (11-16 px at 3-6 px), and blur widens a corner rather than sharpening it: the end is square, or only slightly broken, not the model's half round (probable: one half-second stretch, a blur of about 3 px; square and pointed not told apart). Rough sizes at about 200 px/mm (by the unlocking roller's round end, 2.57): about 0.85-1.0 mm along the axis (model 0.7) and 0.4-0.5 proud of the roller (model about 0.53), ±25 %. The museum's catalogue (above, cat. no. 117A) describes it: "The discharge roller unusual being made as a long split collet with two flats for adjusting and set with a jewel with a radius to the back of the acting part of the tip" (and the impulse jewel's "acting face radial"). So the acting face stays flat to a sharp corner, which is what 6:58 shows, and only its back is radiused, the side the trip spring's tip slides round on the return swing: neither the model's half round (`rnd`, both sides) nor a square end. With a square end the model's settings give an overall of about 38°, so what keeps a real Model 21 within Op. 87's 26-30° lies elsewhere (the trip spring's reach or tip, the jewel's reach or angle); the escapement should be re-solved with the acting corner square and the back radiused (the radius not given) to find it. **What would settle it:** an end-on view along the staff (none found: 34:08-34:14 has the jewel on the far side or blurred; W9gy2gjvZHc and BunnSpecial's 16:40-16:55 too small); the trip spring's tip against the jewel in a running movement, sharp.
- **The balance's free run-down.** With the wheel's chase onto the impulse jewel and the detent's whole lift counted, the model's 255° at a torque the train gives wants a balance running down freely in about 52 s (Q 327; `TF` was 34): inside what the budget needs (225-370 with the mainspring's own losses; 210-291 without them, the fusee's torque on the chain's pitch line since 11 October 2026) and what the balance's parts give (202-390), but resting on both. **A real start disagrees** (11 October 2026): `We1dLNXiBj0`'s balance, let go from a twist at 0:55 and its swing read frame by frame (`tools/swing.py`, 66° to 244-267° at 2:03, still rising), builds up far more slowly than a 52 s run-down allows. Run in `dynamics.js` (`tools/startup.js`), 52 s misses the build-up by 15-16° rms at any drive; it fits within 3-6° only from about 120-160 s up (Q 750-1,000), the drive then 55-65 % of the calibrated torque, and the fit sets no upper bound. The sound of the same start (`tools/acoustic.py --every`) fits a slow build-up better too (rms 18° against 34° at 52 s), though it reads higher. Nor is it the damping's law (11 October 2026): split into its parts at physics.js's shares (the air, a drag in the speed's square, 26-43 % of the loss at 255°; the staff's end on its endstone, a constant friction, 49-53 %; the rest viscous), the same total at 255° (`dynamics.js` `o.split`, `startup.js --split`), the start still misses by 13-16° rms at 52 s and still wants 120-320 s; the running swing and rate don't move (0.04 s a day). One movement, started by hand, its absolute swing known only to a screw hole: not enough to change `TF`, but the budget's Q (202-390, from the pivots' friction and the air) is the reading most at risk. **Small free swings measured (12 October 2026):** no large free run-down is in any downloaded video (every one searched for a 2 Hz swing by its band power, each hit checked frame by frame), but BunnSpecial's Part 1 (`Jd2c3x8VKsE`, No. 2182, dial down, the staff upright as it runs, the camera on a stand) has the balance swinging freely after the mainspring is let down and the Navy stop taken off: 14:04.3-14:12.5, 2.23° to 0.16°, and 16:10.4-16:17.1, 2.00° to 0.44°, period 0.498 s, read off the rim unrolled frame by frame. Both decay in a straight line, −0.238 ± 0.003 and −0.231 ± 0.004° a second (rms 0.03°; an exponential misses by 0.25°): a constant friction, about 0.12° a period. Swings under about 25° never meet the trip spring (`escapement.js`: its first contact at −27.8°), so these are free of the escapement. Physics.js's split gives the staff's end on its endstone, a constant friction, 49-53 % of the loss at 255° with TF 52 s: about 2.4° a second, which would stop a 2° swing within a second. The real constant term is a tenth of that, about 5 % of the loss at Q 327 or 11-15 % at Q 750-1,000, on a movement not yet cleaned (so if anything high). This contradicts the split's shares (`startup.js --split`), not `TF` itself: at 2-10° the speed is too low for the air's drag to show, and a 2 s stretch at 9.4° (7:10.7-7:13.1, the Navy stop on: τ 42 s, 34-55) can't tell 52 s from 150. A friction of the oil at the endstone that grows with speed isn't bounded by it. A steady running swing on a tripod was found (`xM_mxtGfeqQ` 8:56-11:20, bunnspecial, a break-circuit Model 21: 128 turning points, period 0.5003 s), but a timing weight seen at both turning points leaves its swing among 90, 180, 270 and 360° (±8°); a camera fit of the rim's plane would choose. **What would settle it:** the free run-down of a real Model 21's balance from a running swing (the train held by the blocking screw, its swing timed on video), and its running swing on a timegrapher; `xM_mxtGfeqQ` with a camera fit.
