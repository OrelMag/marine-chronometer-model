# Open questions: closed by design

**Closed, 14 October 2026.** Every question below was settled, as a clockmaker completing the design would settle it, so that a chronometer made to the model keeps time for navigation. Where the sources settle a reading (the manual first, then photographs and videos of real Model 21s), the model follows them. Where no source does, the model takes a design value that the checks show works, and a test on the bench makes the made part right. What stays unknown is how the original was made, which matters for historical accuracy, not for whether a chronometer made to the model runs; those readings are listed at the end of each item, with what would settle them (`REQUESTS.md`). The searches of 14 October 2026 are recorded below too: about 130 more YouTube queries and 29 channels, Bilibili, Dailymotion and the forums, with no new footage of the parts wanted; the period texts on archive.org; the Navy's specification for ships' chronometers; and RMG's and the British Museum's catalogues.

Every design value that rests on an estimate is swept over the range its sources allow, the bench's correction applied as a maker would, by `tools/estimates.js` (the build-verification report, section 12): the chronometer works across all of them.

## Open since 16 October 2026: the energy budget against the measured spring

- **Known:** the mainspring solved coil by coil (`tools/mainspring.py`, its `M1`) pulls 0.96 N·m fully wound, not the 0.78 the linear strip's E b t³/12 L gave and the balance's `TF` 67 s was calibrated on (its packed coils don't bend, so less of the strip works). With the balance's damping from its parts (Q 405-710, `TF` 64-113 s) and the train's efficiency from its pivots and teeth, the budget (`tools/physics.js`) needs Q 199-313 to hold 255°; at the model's `TF` it swings the balance about 312° (265-359°). The one running Model 21 measured on video, We1dLNXiBj0, swings about 300° (`startup.js`, `acoustic.py`), and its start wants `TF` 120 s or more.
- **What was tried (16 October 2026):** thinner strips at the videos' L/t, solved (0.365, 0.368, 0.371, 0.374 mm): the fusee's rule sets each up further and they pull 0.80-0.83 N·m, and let down the 0.368 packs 9.4 coils on the wall where KLUwI2UUCMQ 15:54 shows 12 ± 1 (the 0.394: 11.2), packing at 1/44 of the barrel's inside radius against the video's 1/41.4: the videos' strip stays. A free curve set up to 20 % more (its reading is ±15 %) lowers the torque at most 10 % and runs full wind to within 0.2 turn of coil-bound. At these proportions the full-wind torque is the strip's stiffness a turn times the turns the fusee's ratio asks, all measured; any strip works at 2.6-2.8 GPa.
- **In the model (the owner's decision, 16 October 2026):** the page keeps the manual's 255° and `TF` 67 s; `physics.js` takes the solved torque and reports the gap (`~~`). The sustaining spring's band, drawn 1.45 mm high until 17 October 2026 (as high as cleared the sustaining ratchet, pushing 0.82 of the solved drive), is now traced on the video, 0.96 high, and pushes 0.22 (below, "The sustaining spring's section and gap"). A made chronometer is unaffected: the bench's run-down test sizes its spring to its balance.
- **What would settle it:** the swing of a correctly adjusted Model 21 measured (a running video read with `swing.py`, more than one movement), or a measured mainspring torque; the balance's damping from more footage (`startup.js`). Either the real movements run above the manual's band, or their trains and balances lose more than the parts give.

### Critical

#### 1. The mainspring's thickness, and with it the arbor's core

**What is known.** The barrel is measured: r 18.3 ± 0.5 outside, its wall 2.0 thick (SHAPE-PASS 109-6). The spring is 13.55 mm wide (measured, 109-4) and 0.0165 in (0.419 mm) thick by the parts list; by the videos' coils 0.394 mm (KLUwI2UUCMQ, at most, inside r 16.3) and 0.374 (TN24H), and 1,064 mm long (their L/t, about 2,700, no scale needed; the videos' frames 0.9-1.25 m).

**The conflict.** On two movements (KLUwI2UUCMQ 15:54; TN24H `3SFDplGq6vs` 0:16) the let-down spring's packed coils are 0.375 mm apart, which a 0.419 mm strip can't pack to. And the barrel arbor's core reads r 4.9-5.6 on two movements (KLUwI2UUCMQ 33:20.5, TN24H 0:16), against the model's estimated r 1.8. On a core of r 5.2 the parts list's strip is coil-bound 4.75 turns past its slack, short of the fusee's 4.67 turns and a set-up of about 1: it can't be wound fully. A strip as thin as the measured pitch would fit. So either those two springs are thinner than the parts list's (replacements), or the coils counted were not one a peak, or the core reading is off.

**Why it matters.** The spring's thickness sets its torque (as its cube), the fusee's pull, the train's energy budget and so the balance's amplitude (`tools/physics.js`). The core sets how many turns the barrel holds.

**Searched without result.** Hamilton's material catalogues of 1947-1953 ("materials for all war timepieces ... require separate inquiry direct to the Material Sales Department"; their mainspring chart lists watch sizes only); supplier and cross-reference tables; Google Patents; museum catalogues (RMG, Smithsonian NMAH, Ingenium).

**What would settle it.** A micrometer reading of a Model 21 mainspring's thickness (and its length and width), or of the arbor's core diameter. (Doug Sinclair's article, read 18 October 2026, gives no sizes: References/NAWCC-ARTICLES.md.)

**Found (12 October 2026): a museum's measurements.** A. G. Randall and R. Good, *Catalogue of Watches in the British Museum VI: Pocket Chronometers, Marine Chronometers and Other Portable Precision Timekeepers* (British Museum Publications, 1990), cat. no. 117A, pp. 127-128, Plates 78-79 (a public scan in the Internet Archive's `horology-books-and-catalogs`; the figures checked on the page images), a Model 21 numbered ZE 10228 (BM 1988,4-10,2), "finished especially for" Hamilton UK's managing director "from the last parts available at the factory" (the BM dates it 1959-65: a late assembly, not wartime production): "The solid steel arbor snailed and 13 mm in diameter. *Barrel*: Inside dim. 38 mm, internal available h. 15·3 mm, mainspring h. 15·0 mm, th. 0·44 mm. The mainspring fitted with a brace." No length. A curator's measurements of one example: above a restorer's reading, below the manual.

What it settles and what it leaves:
- **The thickness is the parts list's, not the coils' pitch read in millimetres.** 0.44 mm (0.0173 in) is 5 % over the parts list's 0.0165 in (0.419) and 12-17 % over the 0.375-0.394 the videos' pitch gives on their barrels' scale: two sources, independent of each other, put it at 0.42-0.44.
- **The core is large.** The arbor 13 mm across (r 6.5), against the model's estimated r 1.8; the videos read r 4.9-5.6 on their scale, and the spring's eye at 33:06 is formed for about r 5 (References/VIDEOS.md, 12 October 2026).
- **The museum's proportions are the videos'.** Inside radius over thickness 19.0 / 0.44 = 43.2 (the videos' pitch 1/41.0-1/43.7 of the inside radius: KLUwI2UUCMQ 15:54, 33:06, TN24H 0:16); the core over the inside radius 0.34 (the videos 0.30-0.34); the spring's height over the inside radius 0.79 (the videos 13.55 / 16.3 = 0.83). So the coils counted are one a stripe, as the pitch read round the whole turn found (12 October 2026), and what disagrees is the absolute size of the barrel.
- **The conflict is now the barrel's size.** The model's barrel, r 18.3 ± 0.5 outside and 16.3 inside, comes from TN24H's cap flat beside the fusee wheel (1.009 of its tips, r 18.09) and fits inside the train bridge's cut round it (r 21.0, fitted 21.0-22.0, centred 2.02 mm outside the barrel's axis since 2 October 2026, so it caps the barrel's outside at r 19.0, 20.0 at the fit's top end; r 19.2 about the barrel before); the museum's inside alone, r 19.0, is larger than that outside. #### 2. The detent's and the trip spring's sections

**What is known.** Their plan and form follow the manual's Fig. 90 and the photographs, confirmed by the Royal Museums Greenwich catalogue (ZBA7849: "a beryllium-copper (?) detent mounted on a nickel block ... a bifurcated spring and a screwed-on, Elinvar passing spring cranked out to run parallel to the detent blade"). ### How much each matters to the model's working, and how each could be settled without measuring (12 October 2026)

Judged here by the model's fidelity as a mechanism and a timekeeper only (not its likeness to the historical part, nor a maker's build). The running swing is calibrated: `TF` is set so the balance lands on the manual's 255° whatever the mainspring is, so the daily rate is largely protected. Across the manual's whole band (247.5-270°) the escapement's rate moves only −0.14 to +0.24 s a day (`tools/escapement.js`; its own error at the model's settings −3.23 s a day, which the timing absorbs: impulse −2.48, draw −0.26, detent spring −1.13, trip spring +0.64). What a wrong reading changes is the behaviour away from the steady swing (the build-up from Twist, recovery from a blow or the ship's motion, the swing following the drive) and the margins (full wind, tripping).

A limit that is not an open question but outweighs them: `escapement.js` takes the hairspring as isochronous, so the model's rate against swing is the escapement's alone. **Without a measurement on a real Model 21,** three kinds of evidence are left: documents that exist, deductions from what is already measured, and physics from first principles. Swings under about 25° never meet the trip spring (`escapement.js`: its first contact at −26.8°), so these are free of the escapement. Physics.js's split gives the staff's end on its endstone, a constant friction, 49-53 % of the loss at 255° with TF 52 s: about 2.4° a second, which would stop a 2° swing within a second. **The two together (12 October 2026; branch `claude/drive-damping`, ported 16 October 2026: since then `physics.js` takes the solved torque, "Open since 16 October 2026" above).** The hypothesis was a thinner spring driving a less damped balance. The fusee does point to the thinner strip, but not thin enough: a `TF` of 133 s swings the balance 255° at 68 % of the drive the model is calibrated at (200 s at 56 %), and the strips the fusee can set up give 85-100 % (0.374-0.394 mm); a strip weak enough would be about 0.33 mm, thinner than any source reads. And the calibration itself is weaker than the drawn spring: `physics.js`, whose energy budget sets the calibrated torque and so the model's `TF`, works the spring fully wound as 0.777 N·m from E b t³ / 12 L over the fusee's taper (a linear spring from slack, which would want a set-up of about 4.7 turns), where the solved spring (`mainspring_friction.json`, the bending energy's slope) gives 1.018 N·m, 31 % more; through the same train that would swing the balance about 295° at 68 s and want about 46 s for 255°, further from the videos still. So if the videos' slow settling is the balance's damping, those movements' trains and escapements give the balance about half the impulse the model's would from such a spring (the model takes each stage at 0.9, physics.js allows 0.85-0.95; mainsprings measured lose 10-20 % against the coils' 3.4 % worked here), or the model's escapement gives the balance its work differently as the swing changes than a real one does (how fast a swing settles depends on that as much as on the damping). Reported for the owner's decision; nothing changed.

**What would settle it:** the free run-down of a real Model 21's balance from a running swing (the train held by the blocking screw, its swing timed on video), and its running swing on a timegrapher; a steady swing measured (`KLUwI2UUCMQ` 47:03, `W9gy2gjvZHc`); the train's efficiency, which no source gives.

### The real start against the model's run-down (14 October 2026)

`We1dLNXiBj0`'s start wants a balance running down in about 104-160 s and settling at 294-316°, against the model's 67 s and 255° (`startup.js --chase`; References/VIDEOS.md, "The escape wheel's chase onto the impulse jewel, heard"). Two causes were tested. **The escape wheel's chase**: the clicks put the strike where the model's tooth lands up to 217° of swing, then the real landing stops moving (about -6.5°) while the model's goes on 6° later, so the model gives small swings a little more work than the real escapement (1.34 against about 1.24 times at 60°); no drive, inertia or train elasticity reproduces it, and the reason is open. **The balance's damping**: with the work flat, as the clicks', or as the model's, the start still wants TF 104-106, 136-142 or 156-160 s, each fitting to under 2° rms where 67 s misses by 5.7-10.1°. So the chase is not why the start is slow: this movement's balance loses less and its drive is smaller than the model's budget gives, both by about a third. It is one movement, started once, its swing known only to a screw hole, so the model keeps the budget's 67 s; a made chronometer's balance is measured on the bench (the run-down test) and chooses its own strip. **The sound agrees** (14 October 2026, `acoustic.py` on the current model): the strike-to-lock interval follows the video through the build-up within 2-11°, and the steady stretch reads about 300-320° (299° strike to lock; the return swing's own clicks 306-324°), where the fits settle. The loud click was tested as the detent coming home on its stop button instead of the strike: that reading's steady 255° the return swing's clicks rule out. **What would settle it:** a second start or free run-down of another movement filmed (REQUESTS.md item 3), or a timegrapher's amplitude of a running one.
