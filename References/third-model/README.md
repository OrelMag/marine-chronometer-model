# A third model? Swiss and Japanese marine chronometers: source material

Gathered 16 October 2026, to answer whether there is enough for a third model, Swiss or Japanese, to the standard of the Hamilton (its dimensioned 1948 manual) and the Wempe (its 124 works drawings). **Not yet**: no dimensioned source was found for either. `SURVEY.md` is the survey as written (Swiss candidates, the Nardin, the Seikosha in brief, the alternatives), with its sources and a coverage table; what follows are the Japanese search's finds and the files kept here. The requests that could change the verdict are in `REQUESTS.md`, "What we'd ask for a third model". Everything here is other people's work (photographs © their owners, auction houses and museums; papers © their publishers), cited, not ours to relicense. Nothing is built on it.

## The pattern
The Daini Seikosha (第二精工舎) marine chronometer, 1942-45, made for the Imperial Japanese Navy, was copied from **Ulysse Nardin's two-day chronometer**, "identical", with many parts interchangeable (Haruyama, NAWCC Bulletin, October 2000, pp. 581-586; excerpt at <http://www.knirim.de/haruyama.htm>); Kahaku's record says modelled on the Nardin. Greenwich catalogues the Hamilton Model 21 as Hamilton's version of Nardin's standard M.G.F. type. So the three share one layout, and a dimensioned source for any of them serves the pattern; the Hamilton manual's own sizes can't simply be carried over (Hamilton changed the plates' metal, the detent, the balance and the bridges).

## The Daini Seikosha: what is known
- **History** (Haruyama): about 1938 Cdr Sugiyama asked Hattori, which declined; Fuse, Sato and Masumoto led the work; a trial in 1940, production 1941-42, moved to Sendai in 1943; all parts made in Japan (jewels by Ogura); under 600 made; ±1 s a day, all under 0.5 s; an elinvar hairspring (by X-ray, not coelinvar); a break circuit; 56 h. The Navy had bought Nardin and Kullberg chronometers before; earlier Seikosha deck clocks ran ±12 s a day. Kahaku record 108811900036 (<https://sts.kahaku.go.jp/sts/detail.php?no=108811900036>): asked for by 海軍艦政本部 in spring 1942, production from October 1942, 400-odd delivered, the best rate 0.1 s a day. The Seiko Museum (<https://museum.seiko.co.jp/collections/clock/collect006/>): about 500 made; bimetallic balance, helical spring, detent, chain fusee. Watchonista (Donzé, <https://www.watchonista.com/node/448332>): research from 1941 under Yamada and Sato, a 1943 prototype imitating a Nardin, the factory bombed in 1945.
- **The only figures**: an 82 mm full plate, 13 jewels, baluster pillars, a two-arm Guillaume balance, the bowl 114 mm, the box 18.5 cm (Antiquorum, 14 November 2004, lot 265, No. V-574); a 4 in dial, the case 7.5 × 7.25 × 7.25 in (Sundial Farm, V-031).
- **Survivors** (about seven known, serials prefixed V): the Seiko Museum's No. 1001; Greenwich ZAA0268, V-776 (its "c.1920" is wrong); V-031 (Sundial Farm); V-574 (Antiquorum); V-844 (Yahoo); V-366 (Jones & Horan, May 2024, text only: US Army Map Service, Far East); a dismantled piece with a Yokosuka Navy dial (横廠航海實驗部).

## Not reached
- 測器要報 (the Meteorological Instrument Works' journal, 1956-64, NDL PID 1869363): readable only in the National Diet Library or through its service in Japan. The likeliest Japanese source for a technical write-up of a Japanese chronometer.
- JACAR (the Navy's archive) and J-PlatPat (patents): not searched directly. Daini Seikosha's annual business reports 1940-44 (University of Tokyo, microfilm). Dr. Crott lot 50207 (JavaScript only); NAWCC forum thread 199161 (403).
- Searches that found nothing: 精工舎 マリンクロノメーター 分解; 第二精工舎 経線儀 ムーブメント 写真 / 販売; 経線儀 東京海洋大学 / 船の科学館 / 大和ミュージアム; 海軍 航海実験部 経線儀; アジア歴史資料センター 経線儀; Google Patents クロノメーター 精工舎; Yahoo closed listings 精工舎 船舶時計 (wall clocks only); the NDL's 464 public hits for 経線儀 are general navigation texts.
