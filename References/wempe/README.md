# The Wempe Einheits-Chronometer: source material

Sources for the second model: the German *Deutsches Einheits-Chronometer*, the standard marine chronometer designed by Gerhard D. Wempe's chronometer works in Hamburg in the early 1940s. It was built by Wempe and A. Lange & Söhne in the war, by Wempe after it, and, from the same drawings, by the 1st Moscow Watch Factory as the Soviet 6MX. The model is the spring-detent version (*Feder-Chronometer*, Kaliber 05; the escapement sheet calls it Kaliber 020) as drawn. The 6MX and the post-war Wempes are supporting evidence only: where they differ from the drawings, the drawings decide.

## The works drawings (`drawings/`)

124 scans of Wempe's works drawings, about 1024 px each (the coloured overall section 2048 px), as published on dg-chrono.de (the Deutsche Gesellschaft für Chronometrie's site, its section on Russian watches) at
<https://www.dg-chrono.de/uhrenwissen/russische-uhren/schiffe-und-uhren/1945-2000/wempe-einheitschronometer/>
(downloaded 14 October 2026). Each carries the notice **"© Johannes Altmeppen (by courtesy of Gerhard D. Wempe KG) 2017"**. They're kept here at the user's decision, knowing that notice; they're other people's work, cited and not ours to relicense.

What the page says about them:
- The *geistige Väter* (intellectual fathers) were Ernst William Meier and Friedrich Leutert. The chief designers were John Schwarzer and Herbert Müller, whose names are in the title blocks. Several hundred drawings were made, and the wartime ones were revised from experience in series production (the balance staff and escape wheel among them; the revision dates run to 1948, and one balance staff sheet is dated 1965).
- The set comes from Wempe and private sources, and from the Deutsches Uhrenmuseum Glashütte: copies stamped "KOPIE" of the drawings Wempe supplied to A. Lange & Söhne in the war.
- The page shows 116 components, each by one of the several drawings that exist for it, with five exceptions.
- The escapement sheet, "Hemmung für Feder-Chronometer, Kaliber 020" (05 04_1), is dated 03.02.40, which the page takes for 03.02.41, since Wempe had no spring chronometer in 1940.
- The drawings held at A. Lange & Söhne (ALS) became the basis of the post-war Russian marine chronometers. Quoting Kurt Herkner, two Moscow specialists and an interpreter came to Glashütte in 1946/47, and the drawings were translated into Cyrillic. The Moscow chronometers have three pillars because the reparation drawings were of that type. The three-pillar movement is still made by Wempe, which the page calls the longest-built and most numerous chronometer of its kind. The 1st Moscow Watch Factory (later Poljot) made about 20 changes, with no drawings available.
- The book: *Das Deutsche Einheits-Chronometer*, by the page's author with Johannes Altmeppen and Herbert Dittrich (Heel Verlag; the page prints the year as 1912, an error).

What's read off each sheet is in `transcriptions/`, one file per group, every number with how sure the reading is. The model cites the drawing number beside each dimension it uses.

Drawing numbers follow Wempe's scheme: 05 = the Feder-Chronometer. Then the group: 01 case, 02 motion work and dial, 03 balance, 04 escapement, 05 going train, 06 fusee, 07 barrel, 08 frame, 33 hand setting, 36 balance holding device, 38 seconds contact; 51 the wooden box. "WN" numbers are Wempe's standard parts (screws, pins, bearings). Sheets 05 04_20–23 and 05 08_30–33 are for the lever (Anker) version, kept for comparison.

What the sources say differs between the drawings' chronometer and its copies (quoted, with their sources, in the catalogues):
- **The 6MX** (Altmeppen): pillar 39 is in another place. The seconds and intermediate wheels run in jewels in chatons, not brass bearings. The barrel cap is underneath. The detent block is new, with an adjusting slide. The lower balance cock has another shape. There's no window in the upper cock, and no three small windows by the pillar screws. The bowl and bezel are brass, not Buna. The dial's feet are at 19/39/59 minutes, not 20/40/59. There's no external hand-setting. The finish changed in the late 1950s.
- **Glashütte against Hamburg** (Knirim/Schmiedchen, watch-wiki): the bowl and gimbal ring in Bakelite, with a heavy bottom weight from No. 5100; the balance lowered to 3.8 mm from No. 5111; the counter-spring (Gegenfeder) redesigned at Lange from No. 5201. They also give these against Lange's earlier Kal. 100: three pillars instead of four, the barrel cap underneath, the fusee 12.2 mm high, the motion work 7 mm, the movement ring 104 mm.
- **Unsettled:** whether the fusee drew a chain or a steel band from the start. The drawings have both a Kette (05 07 04, 16.07.41) and a Schneckenband (05 07 03, 01.03.43). Greenwich's wartime Wempe has a band, and Wempe 3955's `14_Wempe_3955_Band_Kette.jpg` shows both. Also unsettled: whether the barrel's cap is above or below (Knirim: below; an uhrforum post: above, framed by the barrel).
