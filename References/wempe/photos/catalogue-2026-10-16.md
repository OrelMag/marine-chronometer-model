# Second search, 16 October 2026: German, Russian and Japanese web

> The notes of a second search for Einheits-Chronometer and 6MX material, in German, Russian and (for the first time) Japanese, made to find what the first (14 October 2026: `catalogue-de-en.md`, `catalogue-ru.md`) missed. Every find below was checked against those two catalogues, `../README.md` and `../VIDEOS.md`; none duplicates them. Every image and document found is committed, named here with its path (the Japanese chronometers and the survey in `References/third-model/`); the staging copies are in E:\mc-video\wempe-staging-2\ (`de/`, `ru/`, `ja/`). Videos stay outside the repository. All are other people's work (photographs © their auction house, museum or forum poster; book scans © their publishers), cited, not ours to relicense. The readings marked "checked" were read again off the scans at full size on 16 October 2026; the rest are as the search read them.

Variant key: (W) wartime Wempe of the drawings' design; (WA) wartime Wempe, lever version; (L) wartime Lange Einheits, Nos. 5001-5870; (V) post-war Lange, VEB or GUB; (S) Soviet MX / 6MX.

## Documents

### Belyakov, *Часовые механизмы* (Mashgiz 1957), pp. 99-108 (S, L): high, checked
`../documents/Belyakov-1957-Chasovye-mekhanizmy/p102-p104` (from <https://german242.com/books/belyakov2.djvu>, a DjVu scan with no text layer). The chapter on designing the spring detent escapement gives three tables of "basic design data":
- **Table 1, the Hamilton marine chronometer** (balance Ø 35.6, plate Ø 88.0): escape wheel 16 teeth, Ø 13.15, pitch 2.57, tip 0.11, undercut 21°31'; detent 26.33 long, spring 0.64 wide, 0.055 thick at the bend, 3.7 long, the passing spring 0.045 thick; β 21°, α (lift) 45°, lock δ 1°, linear lock 0.18, locking stone Ø 0.7, τ 10°55', release 1°20', passing spring to discharge stone 0.1, discharge circle r 1.85, impulse circle r 3.70, impulse 41°, the teeth 0.3 into the impulse circle.
- **Table 2, NIIChasprom's design** (balance Ø 32, plate Ø 83): not the Einheits.
- **Table 3, "the 1st Moscow factory's MX-2 (in brackets the data of Lange's MX)"**, balance Ø 32.25, plate Ø 85, so the Einheits calibre. MX-2 first, Lange's in brackets: 15 teeth; Ø 13.96 (14); pitch 2.92 (2.93); tip 0.08706 (0.09); undercut 23° (25°); detent 26.50 (26.8) long, spring 2.0 wide, 5.1 (5.0) long, 0.05 thick at the bend (both), passing spring 0.05 (0.06) thick; the detent's locking arm ("плечо покоя") 14 (14.25); β 21°51'27" (23°); α 42°12'40" (42°18'); lock δ 48'50" (1°); **linear lock 0.210 (0.22)**; τ 14°40'8" (12°); release 1°13'15" (1°45'); impulse circle r 3.4350 (3.8); the balance's release angle 4°20'; discharge circle r 1.54 (1.55).
- What it bears on: the depth of lock, the detent spring's and the gold spring's thicknesses, the impulse radius (the Wempe model's README, Unresolved, "The Soviet design and repair figures"). Which Lange MX the brackets describe (wartime or post-war) the page doesn't say.

### *Эксплуатация и ремонт измерителей времени* (the Navy's Hydrographic Service, 1958), §3 "Морской хронометр МХ" (S): high, checked
`../documents/Ekspluatatsiya-i-remont-izmeritelei-vremeni-1958/` pp. 52-65, 122-123, 128-132 (from <https://german242.com/books/remont_izmer_vremeni.djvu>).
- p. 54: the locking stone set in shellac; a screw sets the depth of lock (as the 1954 instruction).
- **p. 58, "Расчётные данные"** (checked): fusee wheel to centre 90/14; centre to seconds pinion 60; centre to escape pinion 480; wind indicator 1/10; the barrel 6.65 turns in all, 5.23 in 56 h (the chain's working length about 710.6 over 2π(20.75 + 0.9)); **the barrel's torque 103.2-110.3 kgf·mm fully wound (1.01-1.08 N·m), 56.2-61.2 kgf·mm two days on (0.55-0.60 N·m)**; **the hairspring's calculated length 502.35 mm, 12.78 working turns**; the period 0.5 s. Wound to "8".
- **p. 60, assembly checks** (checked): the tooth goes onto the locking stone **0.20-0.25 mm**; the gold spring's end stands **0.35-0.40 mm past the detent's end** ("за конец ходовой пружины": the book calls the detent the "going spring", as it calls the escape wheel the "going wheel"); at rest the gold spring's end stops 0.10-0.15 short of the discharge stone; the tooth to the impulse stone 0.04-0.07; set up 10 teeth of the barrel's ratchet; a cork washer on the fusee's flange. Regulation between +4 and +36 °C.
- **pp. 128-129, Appendix 4, "Таблица зазоров сопрягаемых деталей"** (checked; rows lined up by the ruled lines): the MX's side shake / endshake in mm: fusee arbor in the plate 0.015-0.03 / 0.08-0.12; barrel arbor in the barrel 0.02-0.04 / –; the barrel arbor in plate and bridge, the centre and intermediate pinions 0.01-0.02; the seconds pinion 0.01-0.015; the endshake of the barrel, centre, intermediate and seconds arbors 0.07-0.10 together; the escape pinion 0.0075-0.0125 / 0.05-0.07; the balance staff 0.005-0.01 / 0.04-0.06; the fusee ratchet's click 0.02-0.03 / 0.07-0.10; the stop in the fusee 0.03-0.05 / 0.03-0.05.
- p. 132: an oiling chart. pp. 122-123: faults and their remedies (table 6).

### Tarasov, *Приборы времени* (1976), pp. 244-251, "Морской хронометр 6МХ" (S, later): high for the 6MX
`../documents/Tarasov-1976-Pribory-vremeni/` (from <https://german242.com/books/tarasov_pribory_vremeni.pdf>; pp. 128-135 the escapement's theory). As the search read it: barrel Ø 41.5 / 37.8, arbor Ø 12.6, barrel to fusee 42 mm; the mainspring 1400 × 14.8 × 0.42, its computed torque 1143.5 / 715 N·mm; the chain 324 outer and 161 inner plates, pitch 2.25, 728.75 long, U10A steel; the fusee 8.75 turns, its wheel z 90, m 0.44, on a 14-leaf pinion; the escape wheel Ø 13.20, 1.25 thick, web 0.6; the balance Ø 32.62, 4.20 high, I = 2744 g·mm²; the hairspring 9 turns, Ø 13.2, 8.1 high, 0.43289 N·mm/rad; sections of the movement, fusee and motion work. Checked 16 October 2026: **this is a later, redesigned 6MX, not the first generation's (Kirov 247, 1952) nor the drawings'**: Table 28 gives the train 90/12 (m 0.35), 80/10 (m 0.29), **75/10** (m 0.28) and the escape wheel **16 teeth** (450 × 16 = 7,200 beats an hour), against the drawings' 80/10 and 15 teeth (Belyakov's MX-2 of 1957 and the 1958 book's MX still have 15 and 480 to the escape pinion). The figures: Fig. 140, a schematic section through every arbor (barrel, fusee, centre, intermediate, seconds, escape, balance; not to scale, no dimensions: the stack's order only); Fig. 141, the chain dimensioned (pitch 2.25, 0.85, 2.0, 728.75 between hooks); Fig. 142, the fusee in section; Fig. 143, the fusee's curve; Fig. 144, the motion work in section; **Fig. 145, the escape wheel, balance and hairspring dimensioned with tolerances**: the wheel Ø 13.20 −0.02, its rim's inside Ø 11.30 +0.10, 1.25 −0.05 thick, the web 0.6 −0.03, the hub 1.97 +0.01, the spokes 0.70; the balance Ø 32.62 −0.02, 4.20 −0.03 high, the rim 0.90 −0.03, the cuts 0.70, radii 31.19 +0.02 and 30.16 +0.02; the hairspring Ø 13.20, 8.10 high, pitch 0.80, drawn with its collet and stud column.

`../documents/Romanov-1975-Proektirovanie-priborov-vremeni/` (from <https://german242.com/books/romanov.pdf>). ### Patent DE 742 143 (W): high, checked
`../documents/patent-DE742143-Wempe-balance-holding-device-1943.pdf` (DEPATISnet). Firma Gerhard D. Wempe, Hamburg; inventor John Schwarzer; *Vorrichtung zum Anhalten der Unruhe bei Chronometern und Stoppuhren*; patented from 26 November 1941, published 23 November 1943; Kl. 83a Gr. 19. A brake ring (15) of moulded plastic ("Preßstoff") on the balance staff, gripped concentrically by spring-closed tongs (12, leaf spring 16) so that the pivots take no side load; a bell-crank (9, pin 11 in slot 13, spring 10) opens them, worked by the flattened eccentric end (7) of a knob in the bowl (1, bush 3), pressed in against spring 6 and turned 180° between "frei" and "fest", a pin (4) dropping into a slot (18). Cited: DE490631, DE435208, CH13140, US809196. It is 05 36's device (sheets of 29.08.41 and 26.02.42); its figures are schematic. DEPATISnet's other searches (Wempe before 1960, Schwarzer, Leutert, Lange & Söhne 1930-60, "Chronometer" in titles) found nothing else on marine chronometers.

## Not for the Wempe: a third model (Swiss or Japanese)
Surveyed the same day: `References/third-model/` (its README, `SURVEY.md` and the files). **Neither has a dimensioned source.** The Daini Seikosha marine chronometer (1942-45, just under 600 made for the Imperial Navy) was copied from Ulysse Nardin's two-day chronometer, "identical, with many parts interchangeable" (Haruyama, NAWCC Bulletin, October 2000, pp. 581-586; excerpt at knirim.de/haruyama.htm; Kahaku record 108811900036), and Greenwich catalogues the Model 21 as Hamilton's version of Nardin's standard M.G.F.: the three share one pattern. Published: overall sizes (the Seikosha's 82 mm plate, 13 jewels, two-arm Guillaume balance, 114 mm bowl: Antiquorum, 14 November 2004, lot 265), oblique photographs of about seven surviving Seikoshas (the Seiko Museum's No. 1001, Greenwich ZAA0268, Sundial Farm's V-031, a dismantled Yokosuka Navy piece on Yahoo u1231304991), and one amateur 720p take-apart of a Nardin (bunnspecial, YouTube MeRB0AGrJrw and yqf3jbSKkNQ). The Japan Meteorological Agency's instrument works (Gunji Okada, trained in Switzerland from 1929; a trial chronometer in 1939; the Fukawa works from 1944) also made chronometers; its journal 測器要報 (1956-64, NDL PID 1869363) is readable only at the NDL or through its service in Japan. What could carry a model is asked for in `REQUESTS.md`, "What we'd ask for a third model".
