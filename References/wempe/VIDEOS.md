# Videos of Einheits-Chronometers and 6MX

Videos of real chronometers of this design, found 14 October 2026 by searches in Russian, German and English. As with the Hamilton's (`References/VIDEOS.md`), the videos and their frames stay outside the repository (`$MC_VIDEO`, E:\mc-video); `tools/video.py fetch ID` downloads one. Each moment is cited by video ID and mm:ss.

What each is: the first list is the Russian-language web (the 6MX, made from the same drawings with about 20 changes: `README.md`), the second the German and English (wartime Wempe and Lange, and post-war Lange VEB and GUB). Only a wartime or post-war German chronometer shows the drawings' design; a 6MX shows it only where the 6MX kept it.

| **The swing of a 6MX, frame by frame** (moMvyx8eXfo 17:53-19:50, 1080p, 29.97 fps) | The balance is seen whole from above at about 25°, a 0.90 ellipse, under its cock, for the 6MX's own balance: a cut bimetallic rim with four cylindrical masses and two timing nuts on the arm's ends (the 1963 book's Fig. 5.62). Six still stretches, 2,394 frames. The arm (a grey steel bar on the gilt plate and masses) is read in every frame as a drop in saturation along the rim's ellipse, folded mod 180°. Each stretch's staff image is fitted where the arm's two ends fold onto one peak: it lies 19-32 px from the sweep ellipse's centre, since the masses stand 5.4 mm proud. Every 4 s window (about 8 periods) is fitted with θ0 + A cos(2πft + φ). **A from 110.3° to 184.5° (37 windows); f 1.9989 Hz in file time.** The balance looks the same every 180°, so a turning point gives 2A only mod 180°. The frames between the turning points decide among the aliases (A + 90k): in every window the next alias scores 1.40-2.09 below the best, against best scores of 3.11-3.64. The alias is certain. Neighbouring windows agree to about 1°. The scale (an affine reading of a perspective view) is good to a few percent. |

| **TF, the balance's free run-down** (`wempe_startup.js`) | All the stretches are fitted together with the Wempe's averaged balance equation, d(A²)/dt = 2 Wn(s, A)/T − 2A²·(damping)/TF. Wn is the Wempe's escapement work (makeEsc, WESC.SET). The drive s is free, and each stretch's start is free. **The damping split as the parts give it (air 0.57, endstone 0.06, spring 0.37 at 214°): TF 114 s at the model's swing (111-121 s within the scatter; 99-129 s with the twist's stretch read 3 % low or high against the others, since each stretch's ellipse is fitted anew).** Viscous damping gives the same 114 s. The rms is 0.8° on 37 readings. The steady swing is 149° on 0.56 of the model's drive at 12 h (0.53 before the masses' height was read). With the escapement's work held flat (the plain A² law), TF comes out at 80 s. Fitted alone, the stretches disagree (set down: 38 s; after the twist: 219 s). Only together, rising and falling toward one swing, do they fix TF. |
| What it means for the Wempe | The model's TF is 125 s, estimated from the Wempe's parts (78-198 s), and it lies inside the measured 99-129 s. It stays, and `wempe_escapement.js` checks it against `wempe_startup.json`. Three things limit the transfer. The 6MX's balance is its own design, not drawing 05 03: it is of the same kind and size class (a cut bimetallic rim, four masses, nuts on the arm), and air on the masses dominates the damping in both. The movement is out of its bowl, dial down: the balance runs on the other endstone, and there is no case air. The chronometer was part wound: on 0.53 of the model's drive it settles at 149°, and at the model's drive that swing would want TF 41 s, outside every reading. So **the 6MX's swing says nothing about the Wempe's swing fully wound**: it bounds the damping, not the drive. |
