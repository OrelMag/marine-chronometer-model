### Hand of the click wheel's teeth

- Sheet 05 06_16, enlarged: going counter-clockwise (top to left) each tooth rises from its root to a tip and drops steeply at the CCW end. Tips are farther from the centre than roots (checked by distance from the centre). Built (`outlines_by_level` at z 0, from +Z): radius rises with increasing angle 7.2 to 8.0 over about 11°, then a steep face. Same hand: the built plan from +Z matches the sheet as drawn. A ramp rising CCW lets a CW wheel turn and blocks CCW.
- Sheet 05 06_4, section A-B, enlarged at the right-hand click: ramps rise clockwise, the steep faces at the CW end, and both clicks (pivots at 3 and 9 o'clock) have their tips pointing counter-clockwise. That drawing is internally consistent (free = CCW) and is the opposite hand to 05 06_16's plan, so 05 06_16 and 05 06_4 are drawn from opposite faces.
- The built click's own plan from +Z, rotated 90° (wheel above the pivot to wheel at left of the pivot) lands on the sheet's right click with no mirror; its tip is CCW of its pivot, so as built the click holds CW and lets CCW pass (compression test on the tip-pivot line).
- Built wheel from +Z: free CW. Built click from +Z: free CCW. If the two parts' measure frames both look from +Z = +y (the part boxes give z = y for both), they disagree. I could not settle this: the measure +Z may be flipped for the wheel (its y box -8.625..-6.675 is a translation of z 0..1.95 only if there is no flip, which I can't test without the code). Verdict: hand: can't tell. Flag for the page: fine.py passes the click on the teeth, which speaks for the page being right and the measure frame of the wheel being flipped, but nothing here proves it.
- Winding direction from the groove: both pitch-and-angle tests (hook at -9.7°, 14.75 floor at 0°) are about one turn apart in either direction (0.973 against 1.027 turns), so the groove's hand is not fixed from the y0 section; not used.
