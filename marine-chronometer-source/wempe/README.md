# The Wempe Einheits-Chronometer model

An interactive 3D model, in progress, of the German *Deutsches Einheits-Chronometer*: the standard marine chronometer designed by Gerhard D. Wempe's chronometer works in Hamburg in the early 1940s and made by Wempe and A. Lange & Söhne. The model is the spring-detent version (*Feder-Chronometer*, Kaliber 05) as its works drawings draw it, each sheet **as last revised** (about 1946-48). Superseded figures are history: they're noted below, not built. The lever chronometer's sheets (05 04_20-23, 05 08_30-33) are used only to cross-check the holes it shares.

Phase P1a (14 October 2026) built the frame: the two plates with every hole of their tables, the three pillars and their screws, the movement ring, the barrel bridge with its click, the fusee bush and dust bush, the two balance cocks and the intermediate bridge, each with its screws and steady pins. Holes for the parts of later phases (the train's arbors, the detent block's screw, the balance holding work, the escape wheel's settings) are cut now, as the tables give them; the parts themselves come later.

Phase P1b (14 October 2026) built the going train: the centre (minute), intermediate and seconds arbors, each a brass wheel riveted on its steel pinion and arbor, in their bearings: the brass bushes WN-301 and WN-300 in the top plate, WN-302 in the pillar plate and WN-300 in the intermediate bridge, and the seconds arbor's jewel in its setting in the bridge; and the pillar plate's two stud bushes (05 08 02-2) with the studs the motion work turns on. The escape wheel (05 04) turns the train: until it is built, P0's stand-in escapement steps it a tooth a beat.

Phase P1c (14 October 2026) built the fusee, its wheel and Harrison's maintaining work (ratchet, clicks and their springs, the maintaining spring, the maintaining detent on its arbor at hole 4), the Vorreiber, cover plate, dust plate and the stop work (the bar across the fusee's small end and the stop pin in the top plate's hole 7), the barrel with its lid, arbor, hook and set-up ratchet, the mainspring, and both the chain and the band, chosen under Variants (the chain by default). Winding (the panel) sets the hours since full wind or winds it through the key, the maintaining spring keeping the train going; the hash keeps both (`wind=`, `conn=band`).

Phase P1d (14 October 2026) built the balance and the spring detent escapement (drawing groups 05 03 and 05 04): the balance with its rim, masses, timing weights and staff; the helical hairspring with its collet and stud; the impulse and discharge rollers; the escape wheel with its pinion and collet; the detent with its gold spring and locking stone, on its block; and the balance's and escape wheel's jewels, settings and endstones. They move: the balance swings twice a second and the escapement unlocks, impulses and locks as 05 04_1 lays it out (kinematically, at the swing phase 2a solves: Physics).

Phase P1e (14 October 2026) built the dial and what is under and over it (drawing group 05 02): the cannon tube with its pinion on the centre arbor, the change wheel and the hour wheel, the up-and-down wheel and its pinion on the fusee arbor, the four hands, and the dial with its printing, screws and pin. The hands show the time (the page's clock is local time, as the Hamilton's), the up-and-down hand the fusee's turns from full wind. The hand setting (05 33), the balance holding device (05 36) and the seconds contact (05 38) are not built (below).

Phase P1f (15 October 2026) built the case and the box (drawing groups 05 01 and 51 00): the cast brass bowl with its closing plate, brackets and locking bracket, the bezel and its glass, the gimbal ring with its bracket, pivots and lock nuts, the transit lock, the oak box with its glazed lid, hinges, handles, maker's plate and latch tongue, and the one-way winding key of 1943, lying in the box; and the movement ring's radial pin, which sets the movement's turn in the case. Two views show them: Box (shut, from high enough to read the dial through the lid's glass) and Case (open), and the Dial view now looks down on the dial in its case; the other views lift the movement out and turn it train side up, as the Hamilton's do. The gimbals stand still (their swing is a later phase); their axes are as drawn.

Phase 2a (15 October 2026) made the escapement run physically, as the Hamilton's does: the balance's swing comes from the drive, the fusee's force as 05 06_20 measured it, through the train, and the rate from the escapement (the shared `makeEsc` with the Wempe's settings, `js/escphys.js`); run down, the train stops and the balance swings down to rest, and once wound it needs a twist to start (below, Physics).

Phase 2c (15 October 2026) settled the hairspring's length (the whole strip 13 turns less 3.5 mm, 05 03_7: the period 0.468 s with the weighed balance), checked its terminal curves against Phillips' conditions and the force they leave on the pivots, gave the model the drawn spring's own isochronism, and worked the cut bimetallic balance and steel spring in heat and cold against the 6MX's TU (below, Physics: hairspring and temperature).

Readings of 15 October 2026 settled four open figures from the sheets and photographs (each with its evidence and sureness under How the balance and escapement were measured and Physics: energy): the balance's masses stand from the rim's lower face to 6.46 (they were built from 1.1, the hidden pair's outline), 30° from the cuts' line (32 scaled), which makes the balance 10 % heavier (2,501 g·mm²) and the hairspring's period 0.491 s; the detent's horn is 4.4 long (3.5 built); the detent's banking is the block's cross screw, bearing on the pipe at the stone (not drawn yet); and the mainspring's "0,37" is not overwritten. The physics was run again on them.

## Files

- `index.html`: the page. It loads three.js and the fonts from `../../vendor/`, the Hamilton's `core.js` (materials, geometry helpers, sections, the ink drawing) and stylesheet by relative path, then `../shared/kit.js`, `../shared/viewer.js` and its own scripts.
- `js/dims.js`: `WD`, the shared dimensions: the plates, the pillars and their screws, and the hole table `WD.H` (every hole by its number on the sheets, in the movement frame). Each number names its sheet and how sure the reading is. The pillars' fits keep their limits beside the middles the model draws (`PIL.sF`, `PIL_HF`, `PIL.LT`, `PIL_SEAT_T`), which the essay works the fit from.
- `js/frame.js`: `WFRAME.build(mv, M, K)`, the frame's parts. Each part's own sizes sit in the comment beside the code that draws them, with the sheet, the figure and how sure.
- `js/fusee.js`: `WFUSEE.build(mv, M, K, FR, TR)`, the fusee, maintaining work, stop work, barrel, mainspring, chain and band, and `update(s, c)`, which sets them from the centre arbor's angle `c` (the fusee wheel turns with it by 14/90) and `s.n`, the fusee's turns from full wind (the Hamilton's MoveState: 0 fully wound, `NRUN` 8.81 run down), `s.winding` (the key on the square); it returns the fusee arbor's angle, which `update()` publishes as `mv.userData.ang.f` for the up-and-down work. `setConn('chain'|'band')` chooses the connector.
- `js/train.js`: `WTRAIN.build(mv, M, K)`, the going train and its bearings, and `update(s)`, which turns the arbors from the escape wheel's place `s.E` and returns their angles (`c` the centre arbor's, for the motion work and the fusee; `s` the seconds arbor's, for the seconds hand). `mv.userData.R.tr` holds the arbors' turning groups, each with its `wheel` and `pin` meshes, for the fusee wheel and the escape pinion to phase against (`gearLash`); `mv.userData.ang` the last angles.
- `js/movement.js`: `buildWempe(M)`, which calls the groups' builders (the frame so far), and `update()`. Later groups each get a file of their own (`train.js`, `escapement.js`…), loaded before `movement.js`, each declaring one global.
- `js/escapement.js`: `WESC`, the balance and escapement: `WESC.build(mv, M, K)` builds them and returns `update(s)`; `WESC.ESC` is the escapement's motion (`ESC` in movement.js, which the page and the tools drive, `ESC.state(p, amp, drive)`, at the swing `ESC.A` by default): since 15 October 2026 makeEsc's solved state as the model draws it (`WESC.adapt`, called by escphys.js; The page's escapement, below), the layout's kinematic motion kept as `ESC.layout`; `WESC.HOLES` the holes it needs in the frame's parts (frame.js cuts them); `WESC.SET` the escapement's settings for makeEsc, from its layout (`MK` puts a plan point in makeEsc's frame; `toKin` turns makeEsc's lift into the detent's turn); `WESC.HSL` the hairspring's working length and its other readings, `WESC.HSP` its plan (the path pin to pin or end to end, which the page draws and `tools/wempe_hairspring.js` checks).
- `js/escphys.js`: `WPHYS`, the escapement in time: makeEsc (`../shared/escapement.js`, loaded before it) built with `WESC.SET` and the drive (05 06_20's force, the train's ratio and efficiency), its reference driven at the escape wheel's torque 12 h from full wind (`TQ`); `drv(n)`, the drive n fusee turns from full wind, times the reference's; `ampStep`, the swing stepped as the Hamilton's; the hairspring's stiffness and the period it gives (`HS`); the maintaining spring's minute (`SUS`). It hands its makeEsc to `WESC.adapt`, so the page's escapement (`WESC.ESC.state`) moves as it solves it, at the reference's solved swing by default. `app.js`'s tick keeps the swing (`H`), stops the train when it is too small or run down, and starts it again (a twist, when the balance has stopped). `tools/wempe_escapement.js` prints and checks it; `tools/wempe_load.js` runs these scripts in Node.
- `js/dial.js`: `WDIAL`, the dial, motion work, hands and up-and-down work: `WDIAL.build(mv, M, K)` builds them and returns `update(s, ang)` (from the train's angles `ang.c`, `ang.s`, the fusee's `ang.f` once the fusee group gives it, else the wind `s.n`, fusee turns from full wind as the Hamilton's MoveState); `WDIAL.face()` draws the dial's printing on a canvas: the geometric parts from the numbers measured on Wempe 3955's dial, the glyphs from `js/dialface.js`.
- `js/dialface.js`: `WDIALFACE`, generated by `tools/dialtrace.py --write` (don't edit): every numeral, figure and letter of the dial's printing, the serial and the anchor, traced off Wempe 3955's dial as contours in 0.01 mm, and the two tracks' divisions' measured angles. Loaded before `dial.js`.
- `js/movement.js`: `buildWempe(M)`, which calls the groups' builders (the frame, the escapement), and `update()`. Later groups each get a file of their own (`train.js`, `escapement.js`…), loaded before `movement.js`, each declaring one global.
- `js/case.js`: `WCASE.build(M)`, the case and the box, outside the movement: the box is the scene's group, the gimbal ring's group in it turns about x (the outer pivots, at `YP`), the bowl's group in that about z (the inner pivots), and the page puts the movement into the bowl's group, lifting it out (600 mm) and turning it over in the movement's views (`app.js`'s tick, from each view's `case`: the lid's opening and the lift). `?qa` gives `window.__BX` (root, ring, bowl, lid), which `tools/audit.py box --model wempe` audits (`checks.py`'s `FLOAT_BOX`, `audit-expected.json`'s `box`).
- `js/app.js`: the parts' cards (`PARTS`, `PIECES`), views, sections and labels, given to the shared viewer; it passes the viewer the essay (`essay: ESSAY`) and binds the essay to the model once built (its time, wind and swing).
- `js/essay.js`: `ESSAY`, the Essay tab, *The Einheits-Chronometer* (its text and figure markup the `<article id="essay">` in `index.html`; open `index.html#essay`, or `#essay=detent`): what the drawings and the makers' own measurement tell us and where they disagree, phase A (15 October 2026): the dial keeping the model's time (`WDIAL.face` and the hands' outlines, `WDIAL.HANDS`), the sheets' dates, the top plate's hole table and plan (`WD.H`) and the pillar's fit (`WD.PIL`'s limits), 05 06_20's force against the solved mainspring, the escape wheel's chase against the layout's 21°, and the 6MX's TU against GOST 8916-58; phase B (15 October 2026): the hairspring (05 03_7's curve drawn from `WESC.HSP` with its compass arcs and its centroid against Phillips' point, and the force on the pivots against the helix's turns), heat (the balance cloned from the model with its masses moved along the rim, the coefficient against their place, and the 6MX's 35-day trial for that place, its figures worked as `wempe_errorbudget.js` works them), the escapement in plan (the model's own pieces flattened onto the plan in the frame of what moves them, moved by `WESC.ESC.state`: `drawEscPlan` draws the Hamilton's parts, not these), the box and gimbals (`WCASE.build`), and what the drawings leave open. Its facts come from `References/wempe/` and this README only; its numbers are live (`fillLive`: `data-live` spans from `WPHYS`, `WESC`, `WD`) or generated (`data-wphys` spans and the figures' data from `js/essaydata.js`). No drawing scan or photograph is shown: every figure is drawn from the model's numbers, and the text links to dg-chrono.de for the sheets. The machinery is the shared kit, `../shared/essaykit.js` (`ESSAYKIT`), the look the Hamilton's `css/essay.css` (with a few rules in `css/wempe.css`). While the essay shows the viewer doesn't draw the model (it keeps its time), leaves the keys to it and writes `#essay[=section]` (`../shared/viewer.js`'s `cfg.essay`).
- `js/essaydata.js`: `WEDATA`, generated by `../chronometer-working-model/tools/wempe_essaydata.js --write` (don't edit): the sheets' catalogue from `References/wempe/README.md`, the fusee's measured and solved curves and the losses' range (`wempe_mainspring.json`, `wempe_physics.js`), the detent's fall after it lets a tooth go (makeEsc's state), the rate a °C against the masses' place (`wempe_thermal.js`'s `sweep`), one seeded TU trial's days less the balance's linear term (`wempe_errorbudget.js`'s `essay()`), the force on the pivots against the helix's turns (`wempe_hairspring.js`'s spring, `HSPR.lateral`), the gimbals' bodies (`gimbals.js`'s, the Wempe's and the Hamilton's), and the figures the text quotes from them (`data-wphys`, which the tool also writes into `index.html`). Without `--write` the tool fails if this file or the spans are stale (`ci.py`'s `wempe-essaydata`, `selfcontained.py`): rerun it after a change to `WESC.SET`, `WPHYS`, the mainspring's solve, the balance or hairspring, the case or the catalogue (about 35 s: the trial and the rate over the wind run in child processes at once).
- `../shared/kit.js` (`KIT`): the part and screw makers and the plan helpers copied from the Hamilton's `movement.js`, and, added for this frame, `solidGeo` (a closed solid from plan cells in depth bands, with holes, counterbores, pockets and coned walls) and `bool` (two outlines crossed, sharing their crossing points). `KIT.screw` takes a material.
- `checks.py`, `bom.json`, `audit-expected.json`: the tools' tables for `--model wempe` (`../chronometer-working-model/tools/model.py`).

## Coordinates and units

Millimetres. The movement frame is the Hamilton's: dial side +y, 12 o'clock −z, 3 o'clock +x. The pillar plate's inner face is at y 0 and its dial face at +3.35; the top plate's inner face at −19 and its outer face at −22.35.

The sheets' hole tables give every hole as N/S and O/W (Ost, east, and West) from hole 1, the centre wheel's arbor, which is the plates' centre. Every plan of the frame is drawn with N up and O to the right, seen from above with the movement lying dial down: the top plate from its outer face (its pillar seats, in its inner face, are dashed; its sections mark that face "oben") and the pillar plate from its inner face. N is 12 o'clock: the seconds wheel is at S 23 and the up-and-down at N 23. O is the dial's 9 o'clock: Wempe 3955's dial side (`photos/dg-chrono-dec/3_Wempe_3955_uZ.jpg`) has the Wechselrad (hole 19, O 10.9) left of centre, and the movement ring's sheet, drawn from the dial side, puts its screws 20° clockwise from 12, where Wempe 3955's top plate has them at 339° from N toward O. So x = W and z = S (`WD.NO`).

## How the going train was measured (P1b)

The sheets: 05 05_1 (Laufwerk, the train in section, 1941: plates drawn 3.0, superseded by the plates' own 3.35), 05 05_2 to _4 (Sekundenrad kompl., the wheel, the pinion revised 24.3.48), _5 to _7 (Zwischenrad), _8 to _10 (Minutenrad); 05 08_2 (Oberplatte kompl.: the bushes WN-300, WN-301), 05 08_10 (Zwischenbrücke kompl.: the bush WN-300 and the jewel), 05 08_32.2 (Unterplatte kompl.: WN-302, the stud bush 05 08 02-2 and the studs); the coloured section for the bearings' names and order; Wempe 3955's photographs for the bushes' oil sinks and the wheels' look (`8_Wempe_3955_Räderwerk_plus.jpg`, `12_…obere_Platte_oben.jpg`, `3_Wempe_3955_uZ.jpg`). Each number is in `js/train.js` beside the code, with its sheet and how sure.

- **Places.** Each arbor exactly at its hole of `WD.H` (1, 38, 35); the centre distances 18.12 and 12.63 (05 05_1) are the tables' to 0.001.
- **Teeth.** Each wheel's count, module (its pitch diameter over its teeth), tip and root diameters and tooth thickness from its table. The form is core.js's cycloidal one (gearOutline's wheel tooth: radial flanks below the pitch circle, the epicycloid of its pinion's rolling circle above), run up to the table's tip: the epicycloid would meet the tooth's middle at 1.60-1.69 m and the tables' tips are at 1.56-1.58 m, so the teeth are the sheets' rounded ogives, just topped. The pinions are gearOutline's leaves (radial flanks, a round end) with their radii put on the sheet's tip and root, long and round-ended as 05 05 03-2's end view draws them. The centre pinion's 14 leaves, tip 6.79 and root 4.24 are on its sheet. **The intermediate pinion's 12 and the seconds pinion's 10 leaves are on no sheet**: (2 × 18.12 − 31.976)/0.35529 = 12.0 and (2 × 12.63 − 22.453)/0.28066 = 10.0, and no other count fits the centres (confirmed; centre 90/12 × 80/10 = 60 turns of the seconds arbor an hour).
- **Heights.** Every shoulder, seat, pinion and collar from the pinion sheets as last revised, the wheels' thicknesses from their sheets. The arbors' train-side shoulders rest on bushes flush with the top plate's inner face; the sheets' lengths then put the dial-side shoulders on: the centre arbor's riveting collar Ø 3.0 on WN-302, set 0.1 under the pillar plate's inner face (05 08 02), 19 (+0.05, framed) from its other shoulder, so 0.07 endshake; the intermediate's on the bridge's bush set 0.7 +0.02 in (05 08 04), 23.06 between the bearings against 23.05 −0.1, 0.06; the seconds' on its jewel 1.7 in (05 08 04), 24.1 against 24.1 −0.1, 0.05 (bom.py measures 0.070, 0.060, 0.050). The centre wheel's rim then stands 0.49 above the pillar plate (05 05_1's "0,5"), the seconds wheel 13.7 under the top plate (05 05_1's 13.8, of 1941), and the seconds wheel's rim clears the intermediate pinion's end by 0.055: its hub, left 0.2 thicker than the rim (the 1.2 sheet, the rim turned to 1.0), stands proud toward the pinion (05 05 01's "0,2"), which is what lets it clear.
- **Wheels.** Five straight spokes, hub, rim and hole from each wheel's sheet; each hub left the sheet metal's thickness, 0.2 proud of the rim on one side (the kompl. sheets' "0,2" and the "10∅", "7∅", "6∅" they draw there): the centre and intermediate wheels' on the dial side, the seconds wheel's toward its pinion.
- **Pivots.** Diameters from the pinion sheets as last revised (the intermediate's 0.85 −0.05 of 1946 over the kompl.'s 0.82 −0.02 of 1940; the seconds' 0.82 −0.02 and 0.76 −0.02); lengths from the kompl. sheets (3): the pinion sheets' 4.5-5.1 are the turned blanks', each with its 60° centre, which the kompl. sheets show cut to 3 and rounded. Tapered as the sheets write ("Kegel 1:90" on the seconds pinion, read on the intermediate's as the same; 1:30 on the centre arbor's).
- **The centre arbor's dial end.** The journal "C" Ø 2.2 e8 (05 05 03; 05 05 03-2's fit letter is unreadable) to 23 +0.05, then the shank Ø 1.8 tapering 1:80, 14.3 long to the cannon tube's top (05 05 03). ## Physics (phase 2a)

The escapement runs as the Hamilton's: `makeEsc` (`../shared/escapement.js`) solves it from its parts' shapes and bodies, and the page steps the balance's swing by its averaged equation of motion (`js/escphys.js`, `app.js`'s tick). `tools/wempe_escapement.js` prints every number below and checks them (about 20 s).

- **The settings** (`WESC.SET`, js/escapement.js) are the layout's own numbers put in makeEsc's frame: makeEsc sees the escapement as the sheets draw it, from above (the wheel turning clockwise), this file's plan from the dial, so each plan point is mirrored about the line of centres (`MK`). The wheel's 15 teeth, Ø 14, 10.00 from the balance; the lock 36.5° on, 0.175 deep, the stone Ø 0.75 half round, its flat on the wheel's radius (it draws −3.7° against the detent's line); the detent's flexure at its spring's middle, 12.87 from the locked tip; the gold spring's square end 1.54 from the staff, free 11.18 from its bending point; the discharge stone's acting corner, its bevelled end (0.3 back over its 0.4) and its place at rest; the impulse stone's face on its radius to 3.35, 24° before the line of centres at rest; the tooth (05 04_23: the face's root 2.43° behind the tip, the 1.5° land, the back the Ø 6 cutter's arc). makeEsc's lift is the gold spring's tip's travel; the drawing's (`WESC.ESC.state`) is the detent's turn: `toKin` divides by the tip's distance from the flexure, and the tool checks that the stone, the locked tip and the stone carried by the detent's turn agree in both frames (to 1e-15 of the wheel's radius). makeEsc took three settings for it (15 October 2026), each 0 or as before by default, so the Hamilton's results are bit for bit what they were (`tools/escgolden.js`, and tools/escapement.js, physics.js, keystone.js, tolerances.js, dynamics.js --quick and tripping.js --quick print the same): `BEV`, the discharge jewel's end bevelled back from its acting corner; `TSQ`, the passing spring's tip the square end of its strip, not a disc (a disc's round end reaches 0.075 nearer the staff, and carried the detent 17° too long); `TH1`, the contact tables carried past the Wempe's impulse, which ends 45° past rest (the Hamilton's at 21°). Its swing solver now starts from the least swing that sees an impulse through, and the stone rides an escaping tooth whenever the jewel lets the detent down onto it (both reached only by the Wempe). Checked in time (phase 2d, below), makeEsc took two more for it, each 0 for the Hamilton: `DEP`, the detent's work counted only as far as it rides the stone (flung off the stone's square corner, it never rides its bevel back down), and `RW`, the wheel's chase run with the stone riding the tooth it has let go; and `depart` takes the detent's speed off a square-ended spring as the slope it came up (`TSQ`).
