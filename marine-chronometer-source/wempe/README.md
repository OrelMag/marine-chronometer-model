# The Wempe Einheits-Chronometer model

An interactive 3D model, in progress, of the German *Deutsches Einheits-Chronometer*: the standard marine chronometer designed by Gerhard D. Wempe's chronometer works in Hamburg in the early 1940s and made by Wempe and A. Lange & Söhne. The model is the spring-detent version (*Feder-Chronometer*, Kaliber 05) as its works drawings draw it, each sheet **as last revised** (about 1946-48). Where a sheet was revised the model is built as last revised; the parts the revisions replaced are built as well, shown under Variants, The drawings: As first drawn (below, As first drawn). The lever chronometer's sheets (05 04_20-23, 05 08_30-33) are used only to cross-check the holes it shares.

Phase P1a (14 October 2026) built the frame: the two plates with every hole of their tables, the three pillars and their screws, the movement ring, the barrel bridge with its click, the fusee bush and dust bush, the two balance cocks and the intermediate bridge, each with its screws and steady pins. Holes for the parts of later phases (the train's arbors, the detent block's screw, the balance holding work, the escape wheel's settings) are cut now, as the tables give them; the parts themselves come later.

Phase P1b (14 October 2026) built the going train: the centre (minute), intermediate and seconds arbors, each a brass wheel riveted on its steel pinion and arbor, in their bearings: the brass bushes WN-301 and WN-300 in the top plate, WN-302 in the pillar plate and WN-300 in the intermediate bridge, and the seconds arbor's jewel in its setting in the bridge; and the pillar plate's two stud bushes (05 08 02-2) with the studs the motion work turns on. The escape wheel (05 04) turns the train: until it is built, P0's stand-in escapement steps it a tooth a beat.

Phase P1c (14 October 2026) built the fusee, its wheel and Harrison's maintaining work (ratchet, clicks and their springs, the maintaining spring, the maintaining detent on its arbor at hole 4), the Vorreiber, cover plate, dust plate and the stop work (the bar across the fusee's small end and the stop pin in the top plate's hole 7), the barrel with its lid, arbor, hook and set-up ratchet, the mainspring, and both the chain and the band, chosen under Variants (the chain by default). Winding (the panel) sets the hours since full wind or winds it through the key, the maintaining spring keeping the train going; the hash keeps both (`wind=`, `conn=band`).

Phase P1d (14 October 2026) built the balance and the spring detent escapement (drawing groups 05 03 and 05 04): the balance with its rim, masses, timing weights and staff; the helical hairspring with its collet and stud; the impulse and discharge rollers; the escape wheel with its pinion and collet; the detent with its gold spring and locking stone, on its block; and the balance's and escape wheel's jewels, settings and endstones. They move: the balance swings twice a second and the escapement unlocks, impulses and locks as 05 04_1 lays it out (kinematically, at the swing phase 2a solves: Physics).

Phase P1e (14 October 2026) built the dial and what is under and over it (drawing group 05 02): the cannon tube with its pinion on the centre arbor, the change wheel and the hour wheel, the up-and-down wheel and its pinion on the fusee arbor, the four hands, and the dial with its printing, screws and pin. The hands show the time (the page's clock is local time, as the Hamilton's), the up-and-down hand the fusee's turns from full wind. The hand setting (05 33), the balance holding device (05 36) and the seconds contact (05 38) are not built (below).

Phase P1f (15 October 2026) built the case and the box (drawing groups 05 01 and 51 00): the cast brass bowl with its closing plate, brackets and locking bracket, the bezel and its glass, the gimbal ring with its bracket, pivots and lock nuts, the transit lock, the mahogany box with its glazed lid, hinges, handles, maker's plate and latch tongue, and the one-way winding key of 1943, standing in its holder in the box's back right corner (since 15 October 2026: until then it lay on the floor); and the movement ring's radial pin, which sets the movement's turn in the case. Two views show them: Box (shut, from high enough to read the dial through the lid's glass) and Case (open), and the Dial view now looks down on the dial in its case; the other views lift the movement out and turn it train side up, as the Hamilton's do. The gimbals stood still until 15 October 2026, when they were given their swing ("The page's time, ship motion and reports", below); their axes are as drawn.

Phase 2a (15 October 2026) made the escapement run physically, as the Hamilton's does: the balance's swing comes from the drive, the fusee's force as 05 06_20 measured it, through the train, and the rate from the escapement (the shared `makeEsc` with the Wempe's settings, `js/escphys.js`); run down, the train stops and the balance swings down to rest, and once wound it needs a twist to start (below, Physics).

Phase 2c (15 October 2026) settled the hairspring's length (the whole strip 13 turns less 3.5 mm, 05 03_7: the period 0.468 s with the weighed balance), checked its terminal curves against Phillips' conditions and the force they leave on the pivots, gave the model the drawn spring's own isochronism, and worked the cut bimetallic balance and steel spring in heat and cold against the 6MX's TU (below, Physics: hairspring and temperature).

Readings of 15 October 2026 settled four open figures from the sheets and photographs (each with its evidence and sureness under How the balance and escapement were measured and Physics: energy): the balance's masses stand from the rim's lower face to 6.46 (they were built from 1.1, the hidden pair's outline), 30° from the cuts' line (32 scaled), which makes the balance 10 % heavier (2,501 g·mm²; 2,561 since the shape batch below, and the tool weighing one revision: the period 0.497 s) and the hairspring's period 0.491 s; the detent's horn is 4.4 long (3.5 built); the detent's banking is the block's cross screw, bearing on the pipe at the stone (not drawn yet); and the mainspring's "0,37" is not overwritten. The physics was run again on them.

Phase E1 (15 October 2026) built the escapement as first drawn, the parts the later sheets replaced, beside those they replaced, chosen under Variants (The drawings: As last revised, As first drawn; `rev=first` in the link): 05 04_1's layout of 1940-41 with the escape wheel and rollers just under the top plate, the 1941 staff with its brass collar (05 04_5), the 1941 escape arbor and collet (05 04_7.1), the detent at 1436's first sizes and the block of 1941 with its long arm and cone. Wempe 3955 is made so (below, As first drawn). Phase E2 added the barrel of 1941 in three pieces, its lid on the pillar plate's side, and the 1941 maintaining spring (020605), widening round its turn; phase E3 the winding key of 1940, with its flat pear-shaped handle, and on the cards the figures the revisions changed by too little to show.

## Files

- `index.html`: the page. It loads three.js and the fonts from `../../vendor/`, the Hamilton's `core.js` (materials, geometry helpers, sections, the ink drawing) and stylesheet by relative path, then `../shared/kit.js`, `../shared/viewer.js` and its own scripts.
- `js/dims.js`: `WD`, the shared dimensions: the plates, the pillars and their screws, and the hole table `WD.H` (every hole by its number on the sheets, in the movement frame). Each number names its sheet and how sure the reading is. The pillars' fits keep their limits beside the middles the model draws (`PIL.sF`, `PIL_HF`, `PIL.LT`, `PIL_SEAT_T`), which the essay works the fit from.
- `js/frame.js`: `WFRAME.build(mv, M, K)`, the frame's parts. Each part's own sizes sit in the comment beside the code that draws them, with the sheet, the figure and how sure.
- `js/fusee.js`: `WFUSEE.build(mv, M, K, FR, TR)`, the fusee, maintaining work, stop work, barrel, mainspring, chain and band, and `update(s, c)`, which sets them from the centre arbor's angle `c` (the fusee wheel turns with it by 14/90) and `s.n`, the fusee's turns from full wind (the Hamilton's MoveState: 0 fully wound, `NRUN` 8.81 run down), `s.winding` (the key on the square); it returns the fusee arbor's angle, which `update()` publishes as `mv.userData.ang.f` for the up-and-down work. `setConn('chain'|'band')` chooses the connector.
- `js/train.js`: `WTRAIN.build(mv, M, K)`, the going train and its bearings, and `update(s)`, which turns the arbors from the escape wheel's place `s.E` and returns their angles (`c` the centre arbor's, for the motion work and the fusee; `s` the seconds arbor's, for the seconds hand). `mv.userData.R.tr` holds the arbors' turning groups, each with its `wheel` and `pin` meshes, for the fusee wheel and the escape pinion to phase against (`gearLash`); `mv.userData.ang` the last angles.
- `js/movement.js`: `buildWempe(M)`, which calls the groups' builders (the frame so far), and `update()`. Later groups each get a file of their own (`train.js`, `escapement.js`…), loaded before `movement.js`, each declaring one global.
- `js/escapement.js`: `WESC`, the balance and escapement: `WESC.build(mv, M, K)` builds them and returns `update(s)`; `WESC.ESC` is the escapement's motion (`ESC` in movement.js, which the page and the tools drive, `ESC.state(p, amp, drive)`, at the swing `ESC.A` by default): since 15 October 2026 makeEsc's solved state as the model draws it (`WESC.adapt`, called by escphys.js; The page's escapement, below), the layout's kinematic motion kept as `ESC.layout`; `WESC.HOLES` the holes it needs in the frame's parts (frame.js cuts them); `WESC.SET` the escapement's settings for makeEsc, from its layout (`MK` puts a plan point in makeEsc's frame; `toKin` turns makeEsc's lift into the detent's turn); `WESC.HSL` the hairspring's working length and its other readings, `WESC.HSP` its plan (the path pin to pin or end to end, which the page draws and `tools/wempe_hairspring.js` checks).
- `js/escphys.js`: `WPHYS`, the escapement in time: makeEsc (`../shared/escapement.js`, loaded before it) built with `WESC.SET` and the drive (05 06_20's force, the train's ratio and efficiency), its reference driven at the escape wheel's torque 12 h from full wind (`TQ`); `drv(n)`, the drive n fusee turns from full wind, times the reference's; `ampStep`, the swing stepped as the Hamilton's; the hairspring's stiffness and the period it gives (`HS`); the maintaining spring's minute (`SUS`). It hands its makeEsc to `WESC.adapt`, so the page's escapement (`WESC.ESC.state`) moves as it solves it, at the reference's solved swing by default. `app.js`'s tick keeps the swing (`H`), stops the train when it is too small or run down, and starts it again (a twist, when the balance has stopped). `tools/wempe_escapement.js` prints and checks it; `tools/wempe_load.js` runs these scripts in Node.
- `js/dial.js`: `WDIAL`, the dial, motion work, hands and up-and-down work: `WDIAL.build(mv, M, K)` builds them and returns `update(s, ang)` (from the train's angles `ang.c`, `ang.s`, the fusee's `ang.f` once the fusee group gives it, else the wind `s.n`, fusee turns from full wind as the Hamilton's MoveState); `WDIAL.face()` draws the dial's printing on a canvas: the geometric parts from the numbers measured on Wempe 3955's dial, the glyphs from `js/dialface.js`.

- `js/dialface_lange.js`: `WDIALFACE_LANGE`, the same for A. Lange & Söhne No. 5177's dial, generated by `tools/dialtrace.py --dial lange --write` (don't edit); the page's Variants show it (Dial, `#dial=lange`). Loaded before `dial.js`.
- `js/movement.js`: `buildWempe(M)`, which calls the groups' builders (the frame, the escapement), and `update()`. Later groups each get a file of their own (`train.js`, `escapement.js`…), loaded before `movement.js`, each declaring one global.

## Coordinates and units

Millimetres. The movement frame is the Hamilton's: dial side +y, 12 o'clock −z, 3 o'clock +x. The pillar plate's inner face is at y 0 and its dial face at +3.35; the top plate's inner face at −19 and its outer face at −22.35.

The sheets' hole tables give every hole as N/S and O/W (Ost, east, and West) from hole 1, the centre wheel's arbor, which is the plates' centre. Every plan of the frame is drawn with N up and O to the right, seen from above with the movement lying dial down: the top plate from its outer face (its pillar seats, in its inner face, are dashed; its sections mark that face "oben") and the pillar plate from its inner face. N is 12 o'clock: the seconds wheel is at S 23 and the up-and-down at N 23. O is the dial's 9 o'clock: Wempe 3955's dial side (`photos/dg-chrono-dec/3_Wempe_3955_uZ.jpg`) has the Wechselrad (hole 19, O 10.9) left of centre, and the movement ring's sheet, drawn from the dial side, puts its screws 20° clockwise from 12, where Wempe 3955's top plate has them at 339° from N toward O. So x = W and z = S (`WD.NO`).

The wheel then drops onto the impulse stone at 21° before the line of centres and follows it, the tip sliding on the stone's face, until the tip leaves the stone's end 21.2° past. It runs on to the next lock in 3° more of the balance. The escaping tooth's back holds the stone up while it passes under it. On the return swing the discharge stone bends the gold spring away from the horn, from about 7° to 20° before rest, and the detent stays down. The escape wheel's E (teeth) is the Hamilton's: E = beats − 1 + progress. Its turn, 24° a beat, drives the train through `update()` (the train phase).

Setting never turns the train (RESOLVED.md, "Setting the time turned the train"): the hour and minute hands go to the minute by the cannon pinion's slip on the centre arbor (`slip`, seconds of time; `dial.js`'s `update` turns the motion work and both hands by it, not the centre arbor, the fusee or the train), leaving the dial fast by under a minute; the balance is then stopped and the train held until the time the hands should read comes round to the dial's, and a twist (the Twist button's) starts it. Run down, only the minutes are set. The link keeps `tz=local` and `t=HH:MM:SS` (a time in the link puts the train there before the model shows, as the Hamilton's does: the fusee and dial are stateless in the centre's angle). The Hamilton's With the key and Stop to set follow its manual's Sec. III; no Wempe source in the set gives its procedure, so the Wempe has the simpler setting alone.
- **Ship motion** (Display, Motion: Ship motion, Transit lock, Roll period): the box rolls and pitches, the case hanging in its gimbals leans toward the apparent vertical. The equations are box.js's `GIM.sea` and `gimStep`, copied into app.js (`GW`, `gwStep`) with the Wempe's own figures from `tools/gimbals.js --model wempe` (the weighed bowl and ring, levelled): the bowl on its 12-6 pivots 0.958 s, the ring on its 3-9 pivots 1.046 s, and no stop within 60°. The transit lock turned in holds both gimbals level with the box (its lever isn't drawn turning); turned over to wind, the bowl is held the same way. The Hamilton's Jolt the box, which shows its detent setting or tripping, has no counterpart: `tripping.js --model wempe` gives the Wempe's figures in the report, not on the page.
- **Report a bug or inaccuracy**: the Hamilton's `report.js` and dialog, its subject and its Inaccuracy hint given by `REPORT.bind({name, inacc})` (the drawings first, then photographs and videos of real ones); from the panel, a part's card and the essay's end.

## Building it: tolerances, the bench and the Monte Carlo
