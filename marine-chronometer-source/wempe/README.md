# The Wempe Einheits-Chronometer model

An interactive 3D model, in progress, of the German *Deutsches Einheits-Chronometer*: the standard marine chronometer designed by Gerhard D. Wempe's chronometer works in Hamburg in the early 1940s and made by Wempe and A. Lange & Söhne. The model is the spring-detent version (*Feder-Chronometer*, Kaliber 05) as its works drawings draw it, each sheet **as last revised** (about 1946-48). Superseded figures are history: they're noted below, not built. The lever chronometer's sheets (05 04_20-23, 05 08_30-33) are used only to cross-check the holes it shares.

Phase P1a (14 October 2026) built the frame: the two plates with every hole of their tables, the three pillars and their screws, the movement ring, the barrel bridge with its click, the fusee bush and dust bush, the two balance cocks and the intermediate bridge, each with its screws and steady pins. Holes for the parts of later phases (the train's arbors, the detent block's screw, the balance holding work, the escape wheel's settings) are cut now, as the tables give them; the parts themselves come later.

Phase P1b (14 October 2026) built the going train: the centre (minute), intermediate and seconds arbors, each a brass wheel riveted on its steel pinion and arbor, in their bearings: the brass bushes WN-301 and WN-300 in the top plate, WN-302 in the pillar plate and WN-300 in the intermediate bridge, and the seconds arbor's jewel in its setting in the bridge; and the pillar plate's two stud bushes (05 08 02-2) with the studs the motion work turns on. The escape wheel (05 04) turns the train: until it is built, P0's stand-in escapement steps it a tooth a beat.

Phase P1c (14 October 2026) built the fusee, its wheel and Harrison's maintaining work (ratchet, clicks and their springs, the maintaining spring, the maintaining detent on its arbor at hole 4), the Vorreiber, cover plate, dust plate and the stop work (the bar across the fusee's small end and the stop pin in the top plate's hole 7), the barrel with its lid, arbor, hook and set-up ratchet, the mainspring, and both the chain and the band, chosen under Variants (the chain by default). Winding (the panel) sets the hours since full wind or winds it through the key, the maintaining spring keeping the train going; the hash keeps both (`wind=`, `conn=band`).

Phase P1d (14 October 2026) built the balance and the spring detent escapement (drawing groups 05 03 and 05 04): the balance with its rim, masses, timing weights and staff; the helical hairspring with its collet and stud; the impulse and discharge rollers; the escape wheel with its pinion and collet; the detent with its gold spring and locking stone, on its block; and the balance's and escape wheel's jewels, settings and endstones. They move: the balance swings 225° each side, twice a second, and the escapement unlocks, impulses and locks as 05 04_1 lays it out (kinematically: the physics comes later).

Phase P1e (14 October 2026) built the dial and what is under and over it (drawing group 05 02): the cannon tube with its pinion on the centre arbor, the change wheel and the hour wheel, the up-and-down wheel and its pinion on the fusee arbor, the four hands, and the dial with its printing, screws and pin. The hands show the time (the page's clock is local time, as the Hamilton's), the up-and-down hand the fusee's turns from full wind. The hand setting (05 33), the balance holding device (05 36) and the seconds contact (05 38) are not built (below).

## Files

- `index.html`: the page. It loads three.js and the fonts from `../../vendor/`, the Hamilton's `core.js` (materials, geometry helpers, sections, the ink drawing) and stylesheet by relative path, then `../shared/kit.js`, `../shared/viewer.js` and its own scripts.
- `js/dims.js`: `WD`, the shared dimensions: the plates, the pillars and their screws, and the hole table `WD.H` (every hole by its number on the sheets, in the movement frame). Each number names its sheet and how sure the reading is.
- `js/frame.js`: `WFRAME.build(mv, M, K)`, the frame's parts. Each part's own sizes sit in the comment beside the code that draws them, with the sheet, the figure and how sure.
- `js/fusee.js`: `WFUSEE.build(mv, M, K, FR, TR)`, the fusee, maintaining work, stop work, barrel, mainspring, chain and band, and `update(s, c)`, which sets them from the centre arbor's angle `c` (the fusee wheel turns with it by 14/90) and `s.n`, the fusee's turns from full wind (the Hamilton's MoveState: 0 fully wound, `NRUN` 8.81 run down), `s.winding` (the key on the square); it returns the fusee arbor's angle, which `update()` publishes as `mv.userData.ang.f` for the up-and-down work. `setConn('chain'|'band')` chooses the connector.
- `js/train.js`: `WTRAIN.build(mv, M, K)`, the going train and its bearings, and `update(s)`, which turns the arbors from the escape wheel's place `s.E` and returns their angles (`c` the centre arbor's, for the motion work and the fusee; `s` the seconds arbor's, for the seconds hand). `mv.userData.R.tr` holds the arbors' turning groups, each with its `wheel` and `pin` meshes, for the fusee wheel and the escape pinion to phase against (`gearLash`); `mv.userData.ang` the last angles.
- `js/movement.js`: `buildWempe(M)`, which calls the groups' builders (the frame so far), and `update()`. Later groups each get a file of their own (`train.js`, `escapement.js`…), loaded before `movement.js`, each declaring one global.
- `js/escapement.js`: `WESC`, the balance and escapement: `WESC.build(mv, M, K)` builds them and returns `update(s)`; `WESC.ESC` is the escapement's motion (`ESC` in movement.js, which the tools drive); `WESC.HOLES` the holes it needs in the frame's parts (frame.js cuts them).
- `js/dial.js`: `WDIAL`, the dial, motion work, hands and up-and-down work: `WDIAL.build(mv, M, K)` builds them and returns `update(s, ang)` (from the train's angles `ang.c`, `ang.s`, the fusee's `ang.f` once the fusee group gives it, else the wind `s.n`, fusee turns from full wind as the Hamilton's MoveState); `WDIAL.face()` draws the dial's printing on a canvas.
- `js/movement.js`: `buildWempe(M)`, which calls the groups' builders (the frame, the escapement), and `update()`. Later groups each get a file of their own (`train.js`, `escapement.js`…), loaded before `movement.js`, each declaring one global.
- `js/app.js`: the parts' cards (`PARTS`, `PIECES`), views, sections and labels, given to the shared viewer.
- `../shared/kit.js` (`KIT`): the part and screw makers and the plan helpers copied from the Hamilton's `movement.js`, and, added for this frame, `solidGeo` (a closed solid from plan cells in depth bands, with holes, counterbores, pockets and coned walls) and `bool` (two outlines crossed, sharing their crossing points). `KIT.screw` takes a material.
- `checks.py`, `bom.json`, `audit-expected.json`: the tools' tables for `--model wempe` (`../chronometer-working-model/tools/model.py`).

## Coordinates and units

Millimetres. The movement frame is the Hamilton's: dial side +y, 12 o'clock −z, 3 o'clock +x. The pillar plate's inner face is at y 0 and its dial face at +3.35; the top plate's inner face at −19 and its outer face at −22.35.

The sheets' hole tables give every hole as N/S and O/W (Ost, east, and West) from hole 1, the centre wheel's arbor, which is the plates' centre. Every plan of the frame is drawn with N up and O to the right, seen from above with the movement lying dial down: the top plate from its outer face (its pillar seats, in its inner face, are dashed; its sections mark that face "oben") and the pillar plate from its inner face. N is 12 o'clock: the seconds wheel is at S 23 and the up-and-down at N 23. O is the dial's 9 o'clock: Wempe 3955's dial side (`photos/dg-chrono-dec/3_Wempe_3955_uZ.jpg`) has the Wechselrad (hole 19, O 10.9) left of centre, and the movement ring's sheet, drawn from the dial side, puts its screws 20° clockwise from 12, where Wempe 3955's top plate has them at 339° from N toward O. So x = W and z = S (`WD.NO`).

## How the going train was measured (P1b)

The sheets: 05 05_1 (Laufwerk, the train in section, 1941: plates drawn 3.0, superseded by the plates' own 3.35), 05 05_2 to _4 (Sekundenrad kompl., the wheel, the pinion revised 24.3.48), _5 to _7 (Zwischenrad), _8 to _10 (Minutenrad); 05 08_2 (Oberplatte kompl.: the bushes WN-300, WN-301), 05 08_10 (Zwischenbrücke kompl.: the bush WN-300 and the jewel), 05 08_32.2 (Unterplatte kompl.: WN-302, the stud bush 05 08 02-2 and the studs); the coloured section for the bearings' names and order; Wempe 3955's photographs for the bushes' oil sinks and the wheels' look (`8_Wempe_3955_Räderwerk_plus.jpg`, `12_…obere_Platte_oben.jpg`, `3_Wempe_3955_uZ.jpg`). Each number is in `js/train.js` beside the code, with its sheet and how sure.

- **Places.** Each arbor exactly at its hole of `WD.H` (1, 38, 35); the centre distances 18.12 and 12.63 (05 05_1) are the tables' to 0.001.
- **Teeth.** Each wheel's count, module (its pitch diameter over its teeth), tip and root diameters and tooth thickness from its table. The form is core.js's cycloidal one (gearOutline's wheel tooth: radial flanks below the pitch circle, the epicycloid of its pinion's rolling circle above), run up to the table's tip: the epicycloid would meet the tooth's middle at 1.60-1.69 m and the tables' tips are at 1.56-1.58 m, so the teeth are the sheets' rounded ogives, just topped. The pinions are gearOutline's leaves (radial flanks, a round end) with their radii put on the sheet's tip and root, long and round-ended as 05 05 03-2's end view draws them. The centre pinion's 14 leaves, tip 6.79 and root 4.24 are on its sheet. **The intermediate pinion's 12 and the seconds pinion's 10 leaves are on no sheet**: (2 × 18.12 − 31.976)/0.35529 = 12.0 and (2 × 12.63 − 22.453)/0.28066 = 10.0, and no other count fits the centres (confirmed; centre 90/12 × 80/10 = 60 turns of the seconds arbor an hour).
- **Heights.** Every shoulder, seat, pinion and collar from the pinion sheets as last revised, the wheels' thicknesses from their sheets. The arbors' train-side shoulders rest on bushes flush with the top plate's inner face; the sheets' lengths then put the dial-side shoulders on: the centre arbor's riveting collar Ø 3.0 on WN-302, set 0.1 under the pillar plate's inner face (05 08 02), 19 (+0.05, framed) from its other shoulder, so 0.07 endshake; the intermediate's on the bridge's bush set 0.7 +0.02 in (05 08 04), 23.06 between the bearings against 23.05 −0.1, 0.06; the seconds' on its jewel 1.7 in (05 08 04), 24.1 against 24.1 −0.1, 0.05 (bom.py measures 0.070, 0.060, 0.050). The centre wheel's rim then stands 0.49 above the pillar plate (05 05_1's "0,5"), the seconds wheel 13.7 under the top plate (05 05_1's 13.8, of 1941), and the seconds wheel's rim clears the intermediate pinion's end by 0.055: its hub, left 0.2 thicker than the rim (the 1.2 sheet, the rim turned to 1.0), stands proud toward the pinion (05 05 01's "0,2"), which is what lets it clear.
- **Wheels.** Five straight spokes, hub, rim and hole from each wheel's sheet; each hub left the sheet metal's thickness, 0.2 proud of the rim on one side (the kompl. sheets' "0,2" and the "10∅", "7∅", "6∅" they draw there): the centre and intermediate wheels' on the dial side, the seconds wheel's toward its pinion.
- **Pivots.** Diameters from the pinion sheets as last revised (the intermediate's 0.85 −0.05 of 1946 over the kompl.'s 0.82 −0.02 of 1940; the seconds' 0.82 −0.02 and 0.76 −0.02); lengths from the kompl. sheets (3): the pinion sheets' 4.5-5.1 are the turned blanks', each with its 60° centre, which the kompl. sheets show cut to 3 and rounded. Tapered as the sheets write ("Kegel 1:90" on the seconds pinion, read on the intermediate's as the same; 1:30 on the centre arbor's).
- **The centre arbor's dial end.** The journal "C" Ø 2.2 e8 (05 05 03; 05 05 03-2's fit letter is unreadable) to 23 +0.05, then the shank Ø 1.8 tapering 1:80, 14.3 long to the cannon tube's top (05 05 03). The tube (Minutenrohr 05 05 03-3, with the cannon pinion turned on its foot) is the motion work's.
- **Bearings.** The top plate's three bushes in its holes reamed 4.59, flush both sides, an oil sink in the outer face as Wempe 3955's plate shows them (`12_…oben`, a cup nearly the bush's width); WN-302 in the pillar plate as 05 08 02's Schnitt A-B draws it (0.1 +0.02 under the inner face, a sink on the dial side); the bridge's WN-300 at 38 (0.7 +0.02 in, a sink on the dial side, Ø 3.7 on `3_Wempe_3955_uZ.jpg`) and the seconds jewel's setting at 35 (its mouth Ø 3.8 on the dial side, the same photograph).
- **Motion.** E, the escape wheel's place in teeth, turns the seconds arbor once in 120 teeth (15 teeth × 80/10), the intermediate once in 960, the centre once in 7,200 (an hour at a tooth a beat); each mesh turns the other way, so the centre and seconds arbors turn clockwise from the dial (rotation.y falling) and the intermediate anticlockwise. The meshes are phased under load as the Hamilton's (`gearLash`): the centre wheel drives the intermediate pinion, the intermediate wheel the seconds pinion.
