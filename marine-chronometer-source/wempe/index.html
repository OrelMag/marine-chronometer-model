Assembling the movement…
In progress. The Wempe is being built from its works drawings, part by part. So far the frame: the two plates with every hole of their tables, the pillars, the movement ring, the barrel bridge and its click, the fusee and dust bushes, the two balance cocks and the intermediate bridge, with their screws and steady pins; and the going train: the centre, intermediate and seconds wheels in their brass bushes and the seconds jewel; and the balance and spring detent escapement, with their jewels.

About this model

Orel Magidish (assisted by Claude Opus 5.5) – October 2026 · version dev

A 3D model, in progress, of the Deutsches Einheits-Chronometer: the standard marine chronometer designed by Gerhard D. Wempe’s chronometer works in Hamburg in the early 1940s, made by Wempe and A. Lange & Söhne in the war, by Wempe after it, and from the same drawings by the 1st Moscow Watch Factory as the 6MX. It is the spring-detent version, with a fusee and chain and three pillars, as its works drawings draw it.

Sources

  1. Wempe’s works drawings, as published by the Deutsche Gesellschaft für Chronometrie (dg-chrono.de), “© Johannes Altmeppen (by courtesy of Gerhard D. Wempe KG) 2017”, each sheet as last revised. Each dimension in the model names its sheet.
  2. Johannes Altmeppen’s photographs of Wempe 3955 and Lange 5177 (dg-chrono.de, © 2017), for what the sheets leave open: the standard screws’ heads, finishes, which way a part turns.

How it is being made

  • From the drawings. Every size and place is read off a sheet: a figure where the sheet gives one, and where it doesn’t, measured on the drawing at its scale and marked estimated. Each part’s card says which.
  • So far. The frame (drawing group 05 08): the plates, 85 mm across and 19 mm apart on three pillars, in the movement ring, with the bridges, cocks and bushes that are screwed to them. Holes for the parts still to come are cut as the plates’ tables give them. Then the balance and the spring detent escapement (groups 05 03 and 05 04): the bimetallic balance on its staff, the helical hairspring, the rollers, the escape wheel, the detent with its gold spring and block, and their jewels, working as the escapement’s layout drawing lays it out.

The Hamilton Model 21 working model, the U.S. Navy’s chronometer, built from its 1948 overhaul manual.
