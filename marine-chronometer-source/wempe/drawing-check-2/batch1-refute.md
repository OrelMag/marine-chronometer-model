## How it was checked

- **Model.** The read-only worktree `wempe-check2-audit2` (main 28ab3a44, 3.46.00). I read `wempe/js/escapement.js` 296-590 for the balance, staff, hub, collet, stud, pins and rollers.
  - `seatcheck.py --model wempe -v` over all five states: 503 pieces, 481 seated, 11 expected, 0 failing (`seat.txt`).
  - `probe.py` loads the page (`?snap&qa`, GPU), freezes it at phase 0 and prints the world boxes of the masses, WN-12, the timing screws and weights, the staff, collet, spring, stud and both pins (`probe.json`).
- **Sheets.** Cropped with PIL (`python -P crop.py`). Circles were fitted on rays with `circ.py` and `rimfit2.py`, plus radial profiles and ASCII pixel dumps for the stud views.
- **Scratch.** Every file named here is in `C:\Users\USER\AppData\Local\Temp\claude\e--Marine-Chronometer\68700a8e-be00-4ada-9796-297f431020bf\scratchpad\dc2\b1r\`.

## Disagreements after reading batch1-model.md

1. **J16 / the 05-03_9 table's "hole Ø 0,7 … 0.72 from the end … match (J16), sure", and the 05-03_5 row's "3,15: +0.63 … recorded" as a by-product of the turns reading.**
   - batch1-model.md's argument: 05-03_9 draws the hole at the end, and the spring's height follows from reading 05 03_7's "13 Umgänge −3,5 mm" as the whole strip.
   - My answer: the hole's *place* is not a drawn figure. 05-03_9 says it is drilled at assembly and dimensions only its Ø. The place is dimensioned twice elsewhere: 05-03_5's 3,15 under the bar, and 05-03_1's stud detail with 3,15 under the bar and 0,7 under the cock. The coloured section draws it there too (0.8 under the cock, about 0.7 above the upright's end, on a shorter upright).
   - So the 0.63 is not only a consequence of the turns. The model placed the hole by scaling an undimensioned feature against two dimensions, and then raised the spring's top end to it (`hsUp`).
   - The joint (the strip in the hole with its pin) holds. Its height is wrong, likely. Grade it a dimension finding (likely), not "match, sure". It shares its fix with the turns reading.
2. **M5 (WN-12a)**: I agree with "possible" and with the owner's choice. Two additions:
   - 05-03_2 draws the small circle in all four masses (holes) and shows no head in its side view. Only the coloured section shows a screw fitted.
   - The coloured section draws that hole plain, with no countersink: a cheese head on a flat top. The model's 90° countersink to Ø 1.85 in every top comes from the lever Wempe 2965's photograph and is not in these sheets. This is a further finding: likely, the drawings' versions.
3. **M9**: batch1-model.md grades it "possible", measuring 2.0 ±0.7° on the scan. I measured 2.7-3.7°, and the sheet's explicit "90°" to the upper side states the rule outright. I would grade it likely, turned by the side's full slope (3.5-3.7°), not half of it.
4. **M3**: agreed. bom.json's WN-10 `fn` ("From under the hub's flange into the arm") also contradicts the code and the coloured section. Add it to M8/M13.
5. **F1/F28 (05-03_1 is this balance)**: agreed, "likely", with the same qualifier: a lever's sheet whose balance proper is shared. The rollers (a table roller with an axial impulse pin, a safety roller, "Hebelstein") are not this chronometer's.
6. **05-03_2's "39"**: batch1-model.md doesn't treat it. The drawn arc is the masses' envelope at Ø 37.7, which the model matches. The figure is unexplained. Record it, no change.
7. **05-03_1's dashed pair "0,5" inside the stud's upright**: not treated there. It is unexplained and not a slot open at the end (the end view has none). Record it as open.
