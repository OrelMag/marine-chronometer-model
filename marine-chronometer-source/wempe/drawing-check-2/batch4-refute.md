# Drawing check 2, batch 4: the refuter's verdicts

Refuter (step 7), 28 October 2026. Sheets read: 05-07_01 to _14 and the coloured section (Wempe_Einheitschronometer.jpg), cropped and enlarged with PIL. The model was read on main b134d60e (worktree wempe-check2-audit): `wempe/js/fusee.js` and `app.js`, plus a Playwright dump of every 0507/0508 mesh's world box in the `last` and `first` states (`?snap&qa&start=movement`, GPU). In that dump world y rises toward the barrel bridge: the bridge is 622.35-625.67 and its outer face is at 625.67.

I wrote the verdicts below before reading batch4-read.md or batch4-model.md.

## Disagreements after reading batch4-model.md

I wrote this section after the verdicts above, once I had read batch4-model.md.

Agreed with no substantive difference: M1 (C1), M2 (C2), M3 (C3a, and its proposal to open the 1941 lid's Steg slot to the rim per 07, which matches my C3b split), M4 (C4), M7 (C7), M8 (C8), M11 (C11: "inconsistent", the 5° and 3° missing), M14 (C14), M16 (C16) and M20 (C20). M20 reached the same 50° for the drawn edge independently. My plan-view argument for M1 (first-angle, view from the bridge side) is the one its mirrored overlay also found.

Where I disagree:

- **M5 / C5 (the 1941 square's end): I would not call it "wrong".**
  - The comparer reads 09's 44.2 as counting both turning centres and drops the right-hand point as a centre too. That is fair for the plate end, where 01 and the 1943 sheet draw a ball.
  - At the square's end, though, the only real 1941-type clock photographed (Wempe 3955) has a point about 2.2 high, its sides ending 13.1 from the shoulder. That is close to 09's 15 with the point, and not 01's rounded end at about 13.3.
  - 01's end is a schematic assembly line. Its own other end (a ball, against 09's centre) shows that it doesn't draw the turned blank.
  - With a real clock showing the point, the sheets split (01 against 09), and the project's rule that photographs outrank estimates, I'd leave 'first' as built and record 01's "10, rounded" as a sheet conflict. I wouldn't cut it to −35.67.
  - Verdict stays: numbers confirmed, defect undecidable or refuted.
- **M6 / C6 (the 1941 diameters):** the comparer calls the journals "wrong against 09 and 01". I agree with the figures, but neither side can be shown to rule. 02 is the barrel's own part sheet, and its Ø 4.5 runs straight on into 06 and 07 (4.5 +0.1). 01 and 09 agree with each other but may simply predate 02's change.
  - Its correction's "otherwise" branch (record 4.48/4.55 as an estimate for 'first') is the one I'd take until batches 8 and 9 settle the 1941 plate holes.
  - The pivots' 0.12 is the frame's, not this batch's.
- **M12 / C12:** agreed that it is only "possible". I add a piece of evidence it lacks: the coloured section, whose numbering is post-1944, draws the Steg about 0.45-0.55 thick (4-5 px at 9.3 px/mm, against the wall's 14-15 px = 1.55). That weighs against a 0.8 Steg. Its point that a 0.8 Steg leaves too little room for the outer coil is a second argument the same way. I'd close it as "not implied" rather than leave it open.
- **C3b, on the 1948 lid:** the comparer measured the 1948 lid's notches open to the rim, which matches my code reading. The claim's "closed slot" therefore applies only to the 1941 discs.
