Does the model behave like a real Hamilton Model 21?

The model already agreed with itself and with the 1948 manual's adjustment figures: that is parity, not proof. Here it is compared with recordings of real, running Model 21s it was never fitted to, by two independent tests: the sound (the timing of the clicks inside each beat) and the picture (the balance's swing read frame by frame).

What is tested

A detent escapement makes its sounds once per oscillation of the balance (every 0.5 s). On the active swing: the discharge jewel meets the passing spring and lifts the detent (unlocking), the wheel is released, the detent lands back on its stop, the escape wheel's tooth strikes the impulse jewel, and the next tooth locks on the stone. Half a period later, on the return swing, the jewel meets the trip (gold) spring and the spring comes clear.

The times between these clicks depend on the balance's swing (a larger swing crosses the escapement's angles faster) and on how the escape wheel moves once released. That is exactly what release 2.21.00 changed: the old model treated the wheel as a gear that lands on the jewel at once; the corrected one lets it start from rest and chase the jewel. The two make different predictions:

At a swing of 255°Corrected model (dynamics.js)Old model (39d6919)
Unlocking → strike7.2 ms2.1 ms
Strike → lock8.5 ms12.9 ms
Their ratio (does not depend on the swing)≈ 0.85 (0.77–0.89 over 190–330°)0.16

Both intervals shrink as the swing grows, so one interval alone reads as a swing. Their ratio doesn't, so hearing all three clicks tells the models apart at any swing.

Test 1: the sound (tools/acoustic.py)

Each recording's beats are averaged (each placed on the running average by cross-correlation; beats with speech or handling are rejected by their quiet floor), the clicks' onsets are found on the 4–12 kHz envelope in decibels (the case rings near 1 kHz, so lower bands blur), each with its spread over 200 bootstrap resamples of the beats. The model's events are then paired one to one with the onsets, the swing the only free parameter (then the beat error too), for both models.

s7VW3RiJ97E: the cleanest recording corrected model fits; old model can't

  • Onsets at −12.44, −3.68 (the loudest), +6.63, +246.16 and +254.97 ms (±0.05–0.3 ms; 49 beats).
  • The corrected model, as it stands (14 October 2026), pairs four of them (unlocking, strike, lock, the trip spring met) at a swing of about 205° (202°, 187–207° over resamples), χ² 2.4. Single intervals read 188–220°. With the beat error free too, all five onsets fit at 207° (203–212°), 11° out of beat. (On the model of 11 October: 212°.)
  • Measured unlocking→strike 8.76 ms, strike→lock 10.31 ms. On the corrected model the first hardly changes with the swing (7.3–7.7 ms from 200° to 255°: the wheel chases the jewel); on the old model it is about 2 ms at any swing. The old model's best fit leaves the loudest click unexplained.

A real start: the best test (We1dLNXiBj0)

In We1dLNXiBj0 the balance stands still until 0:52, a stick turns it about 65° and lets go at 0:55.2, and the escapement builds the swing up over the next minutes. The video measures that swing; the sound, read in 6-second stretches, shows the clicks moving as it grows. This turns one recording into a whole range of swings.

The swing through the start: video, sound, and the model

Data table

The video's swing (blue line; shaded: its possible readings, a screw hole apart) rises from 66° to 244–267° at 2:03 and is still rising about 1°/s. The sound's swing (orange dots, read from the strike-to-lock interval on the current model) follows it within 2–11° from 1:23 to 1:59, and the steady stretch after 2:08 reads about 300–320°. The grey curves are the same start run in the model (tools/startup.js): with the model's balance run-down TF = 67 s the swing builds up too fast and flattens too early (11.5–12.2° rms from the video at its best drive; 14.5–16.7° at the 52 s it had until 12 October 2026); a run-down of 160 s or more, with the drive at 60–75 % of the model's torque, follows the video within 2.8–5.1° rms.

Is it the escape wheel's chase onto the impulse jewel? The clicks of each stretch, read on the video's swing rather than the model's timing (startup.js --chase), put the strike at the balance angle where the model's tooth lands up to about 217° of swing (within 0.4°); above it the real strike stops moving (about −6.5°) while the model's tooth lands later and later, 6.2° over 196–252°: its wheel, let go from rest, chases the jewel, so the model gives a small swing a little more of the escapement's work than a running one (1.34 times at 60° against 255°; moved as the clicks move, about 1.24). That is not why the start is slow: the averaged build-up law, refitted with the work falling as the model's, the clicks' or not at all, still wants TF 156–160, 136–142 or 104–106 s (each within 2° rms; 67 s misses by 5.7–10.1°), settling at 294–316°. No drive, inertia or give of the train's teeth makes the landing stop moving; what does is open.

Test 2: the swing on video (tools/swing.py)

At 30 frames a second a 0.5 s oscillation has only 15 frames, and in mid-swing the balance turns about 100° per frame and is a blur. Near each turning point it is almost still, so those frames are nearly sharp. The tool finds the turning points by mirror symmetry (frames equally before and after a turn look alike), reads the screws' angle there, and measures how far each extreme moves from one turning point to the same side's two periods later.

The frames it reads are not reproduced here (the video is other people's work): the balance at rest at 0:49, held by the stick and let go at 0:55.2, its turning points through to 2:01; swing.py draws them, with the screws' 5° ticks, into $MC_VIDEO/swing.

  • 256 ± 9° at 2:03.4, four possible readings 244, 252, 259, 267°: the start is known only modulo half a screw hole (7.5°), the growth modulo a hole (15°). 267° is the likeliest (its growth, 196.8°, is nearest the summed 191°). A +30° slip is unlikely (it needs a 9°/s burst in 4.8 s with no screws in view).
  • This is a start, not a steady swing: a lower bound on this movement's running swing.
  • Rejected, with the reasons in swing.json: xDKFZaY88cM (edited: frames repeated and held), FpolZSipVmQ (rollers edge-on), s7VW3RiJ97E (5 s from above, panning), XJQCgMV_GA8, bEWF2ivi1Rg, R-DrJz1DYOY (no balance in view from above).

Do the two tests agree?

Data table

Yes, within their uncertainties. On the model as it stands (14 October 2026), through the start the sound's strike-to-lock reading follows the video within 2–11° (198–255° against 196–252° on its middle reading, 1:23–1:59), and the steady stretch, about 300–320° (299° strike to lock; the return swing's own clicks 306–324°), sits at and above the video's extrapolated 280–311°. On the model of 11 October the sound read 15–30° above the video, which looked like a strike-to-lock arc 6 % too long; the changes since (the detent's preload, the balance's run-down, the train's measured inertia) closed it. Is the loud click the strike? Taken as the detent coming home on its stop button instead, the first interval's trend fits better, but the lock comes 1.4–1.9 ms early, and the steady 255° that reading gives, the return swing's clicks (which involve neither) rule out: it is the strike.

Implications for the model

  1. The corrected escapement is right where it mattered. On real Model 21s the wheel catches the impulse jewel 7–9 ms after the unlocking, and the intervals follow the swing as dynamics.js computes them, through a whole start. The old kinematic wheel (strike 2 ms after the unlocking) is ruled out by the cleanest recording and by every stretch of the start.
  2. Real swings vary from unit to unit. About 205° on one movement, about 300–320° running on another (about 150–200° on a third, roughly). The manual's 247.5–270° and the model's 255° lie between: the model's swing is a reasonable middle, not a measured value for any one chronometer.
  3. Each movement has a beat error. The return swing's clicks fall a few milliseconds off where an in-beat balance puts them, differently in each recording: 5–12° out of beat when fitted. The model assumes a chronometer exactly in beat (the manual's adjustment); that is fine, but it means the sound can't test the trip spring's geometry or the shape of the discharge jewel's end (its back's radius).
  4. The balance loses its swing more slowly than the model says. A real start rises far more slowly than the model's 67 s run-down allows; the fit wants TF of at least about 120–160 s (Q ≈ 750–1,000) with about 60–80 % of the model's drive, and gives no upper bound. That is beyond the 65–113 s the balance's parts allow in physics.js with the endstone's friction measured (32–62 s before it was). A second movement says the same (12 October 2026): xM_mxtGfeqQ's swing, recovering after handling and read to ±2–4°, fits TF 133 ± 4 s by the averaged law and 200–320 s in dynamics.js (the model's 68 s then: 7.3° rms). Against that, published figures put chronometer balances at Q 300–700 (Rawlings, as quoted by Vardi 2014) and a "Hamilton chronometer" at about 220 (Bateman's chart in Vardi 2014): TF 35–110 s. It is recorded in OPEN-QUESTIONS.md; the model is not changed on one hand-started movement whose absolute swing is known only to a screw hole.
  5. The escape wheel reaches the jewel sooner than the model's at a running swing. The real strike stops moving with the swing from about 217°, the model's tooth lands later and later: a small difference in the escapement's work (1.24 against 1.34 times at 60°), not the cause of the slow start.

Method, limits and files

  • Sound: python acoustic.py s7VW3RiJ97E We1dLNXiBj0 xDKFZaY88cM XJQCgMV_GA8 FpolZSipVmQ --write (about 4 min) and python acoustic.py We1dLNXiBj0 --every 6 --from 0:56 --write (30 s). Onset: the level first 1.5 dB over its highest 4–2 ms before, on a rise reaching 2 dB; an onset of 3 dB or more in 80 % of resamples must be explained by the fit, a weaker one may be. Cost: χ² of the pairs + 4 for each model event unheard + 9 for each strong onset unexplained. Only intervals inside a beat are used: the recordings' clocks are good to tens of ppm, not to the rate.
  • Video: python swing.py We1dLNXiBj0 (7 s). Start in the model: node startup.js (about 8 min; --chase 2 s).
  • Results in the repository (branch claude/validation, not merged): tools/acoustic.json, acoustic_stretches.json, swing.json; the model README's "Checked against real chronometers", References/VIDEOS.md (Measurements, Methods), OPEN-QUESTIONS.md.
  • This report: verification/real-chronometers/, rebuilt by python build_report.py there (its model curves by node reportdata.js, about 10 s); python build_report.py --local DIR builds a fuller copy, with audio excerpts and video frames, outside the repository.
