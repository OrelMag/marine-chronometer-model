About this model

A working 3D model of the Hamilton Model 21 marine chronometer: a two-day fusee chronometer with a spring detent escapement, made in Lancaster, Pennsylvania, for the U.S. Navy from 1941. Every part runs from one clock: the balance beats twice a second, the hands step in half-seconds, and the fusee and chain follow the hours since winding.

Using it

  • Drag to turn; pinch or scroll to zoom.
  • Tap a part to see what it does and its Hamilton part number.
  • Right-click a part to fade or hide it; right-click empty space to bring hidden parts back.
  • Space bar stops and restarts the movement.

Sources

  1. Manual for Overhaul, Repair and Handling of Hamilton Ship Chronometer, with Parts Catalog, NAVSHIPS 250-624, Bureau of Ships, 1948. The structure and how it is assembled, the fusee and maintaining work, the winding stop, the detent and its adjustments (lock, let-off, overall, drop and roller shake), the roller sizes, the balance and hairspring, the gimbal mounting, the dial, the winding figures and the part numbers.
  2. A new-old-stock Model 21 pillar plate listing: 87.57 mm across, 3.86 mm thick.
  3. W. Rawlings’ plan of the Model 21 escapement, a redrawing of the manual’s Fig. 90, and a photograph of a Model 21 detent (chronometerbook.com, post 4).
  4. A restorer’s escape-wheel specification (chronometerbook.com, post 30): 16 teeth, 13.16 mm across, 1.3 mm thick, and the locking jewel set at 8–12° of draw.
  5. Photographs: the manual’s Fig. 2, a near top-down view of a 1941 movement, and side and three-quarter views of others.

How it was made

  • Measured. The balance, fusee and barrel positions were triangulated from two photographs, each with its own fitted camera. The bridge outline, balance cock, setup cover, screws, engraving and damascene direction were traced on the top view; rendered from that photograph’s camera, the model’s key axes land within 0.4 mm of it.
  • Solved. The escape wheel sits 9.40 mm from the balance, where the manual’s impulse roller leaves its specified 0.002 in of roller shake. The third wheel’s position and the train’s tooth sizes were solved so that every arbor clears every wheel and the barrel.
  • Checked. Every part was tested for collisions through a full escapement cycle, and every screw, pivot and pawl for proper seating and engagement.

Estimated

  • The tooth counts of the centre, third and fourth wheels and pinions, chosen to give the ratios a half-second train needs. The 16-tooth escape wheel and the 96:14 first stage (seven half turns of the key a day) are Hamilton’s.
  • The fusee profile, the heights of the levels, and the mainspring’s coils.
  • The detent’s dimensions (its plan follows the manual’s Fig. 90, its settings the manual’s adjustment figures), the balance rim diameter, and the sustaining pawl’s position.
  • Above real time, the balance is shown swinging at a viewable rate.

Finish

Nickel plates with bold damascening and gilt wheels, as on the photographed movements. The gilt option matches the gilt movement in one of the reference photographs.
