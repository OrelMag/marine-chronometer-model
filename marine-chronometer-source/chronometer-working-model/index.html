Sources. Manual for Overhaul, Repair and Handling of Hamilton Ship Chronometer, NAVSHIPS 250-624 (1948): structure, maintaining work, stop-bar, detent, balance and hairspring, dial markings, winding figures, part numbers. New-old-stock pillar plate listing: 87.57 mm diameter, 3.86 mm thick. W. Rawlings’ plan of the Model 21 escapement and a restorer’s escape-wheel notes (chronometerbook.com): 16 teeth, 13.16 mm, 1.3 mm thick.

Measured. The balance, fusee and barrel positions were triangulated from two photographs, the manual’s Fig. 2 and a near top-down view of a 1941 movement, each with its own fitted camera. The bridge outline, balance cock, setup cover, screws, engraving columns and damascene direction were traced on the top view and mapped into the model; rendered from that photograph’s camera, the model’s key axes land within 0.4 mm of it. The dial orientation follows Fig. 107. The hands advance in half-second steps (Sec. IX, p. 66); 7 half turns of the key per 24 h give one fusee turn per 6.86 h (96:14).

Solved. The third and escape wheel positions and the going-train modules (0.29, 0.30, 0.31) were solved so that every arbor clears every wheel and the barrel; every part was then checked for interference through a full escapement cycle.

Estimated. Tooth counts of the centre, third and fourth wheels and pinions (chosen to give the ratios), the fusee profile, the exact height of each level, and the mainspring’s coils. Above real time the balance is shown at a viewable rate.

Finish. Nickel plates with bold damascening, gilt wheels, as photographed. The gilt option matches the reference photograph.
