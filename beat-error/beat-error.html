How to get a good reading
  1. Quiet room. Wind the watch.
  2. Lay the phone down and set the watch on it, case back on the microphone (usually at the bottom edge). Or hold it there firmly.
  3. Tap Listen and keep still. It stops by itself when the reading settles, within about 20 seconds.
  4. For a full check, measure dial up and crown down too.
What the numbers mean

Beat error is how unevenly the balance swings to each side. A watch goes tick, tock, tick: if tick to tock takes 126 ms and tock to tick 124 ms, the beat error is half the difference, 1.0 ms. Timegraphers measure it the same way. Each beat is timed at its first click.

A common workshop guide: under 0.5 ms is good, up to 1.0 ms is fine for an older watch, above that the hairspring's stud or collet usually wants setting.

± is how far two halves of the recording, measured apart, disagree. Rate is seconds a day, + for fast, over the time it listened, in the position it lay.

Uncertain means the tick and tock didn't line up clearly, usually because one of them is faint: one of its clicks may be lost in the noise. Press the microphone closer and listen again.

A phone's own recorder (Voice Memos and the like) compresses sound, which blurs the clicks. Listen live where you can; a WAV recording is next best.
