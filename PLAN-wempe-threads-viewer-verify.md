# Handover: the Wempe's threads, its 3D viewer, and whether a made one would navigate

Written 26 October 2026 at the end of the Wempe's per-part audit (six batches, live as 3.32.05; the ledger `WEMPE-PROVENANCE.md`; the before/after renders on the private page https://claude.ai/artifact/QYvm2mZL8MfZbfckLpGiSk). Three tasks follow from it. Read `CLAUDE.md` first: its rules (measured lines, closed solids, the checks, releases) all apply. Model: the Wempe Einheits-Chronometer, `marine-chronometer-source/wempe/` (its README is the reference), as last revised unless a task says otherwise.

## How to work here (learned the hard way on 25-26 October)

- **Your own branch and worktree** from `origin/main`, pushed at its first commit; never touch the shared checkout. Other sessions release to `main` about hourly.
- **Main moves under a long CI.** Before releasing, fetch; if main moved, merge it, take main's copies of generated files (built pages, `site/`, `CHANGELOG.md`, `essaydata.js`, the Hamilton's `tripping.json`/`bench.json`/`makers.js`, the report's assets), regenerate what your change moves, and run CI again (mostly cached). Never chain a `git commit -a` after a `git merge` without checking for conflicts: one release went out with markers in `CLAUDE.md`. Check `CHANGELOG.md`'s top version just before `--release`: two sessions both released a 3.32.03.
- **The release chain that works:** CI on the release build, then in the same script `git fetch`; if `HEAD..origin/main` is empty, merge `--no-ff` in a detached worktree at `origin/main`, push, `python deploy.py`, push the tag. Release and deploy each finished batch without asking (the owner's standing rule); suggest the version kind in the commit.
- **CI timings.** A change to `fusee.js`, `escapement.js` or any file every Wempe check reads invalidates most of the cache: `ci.py --full` then takes 1-2 h (151 steps). Don't run browser work (renders, `isolate.py`, other checks) beside it: they share the machine's browser slots and stretch it. `wempe-maintaining` sometimes fails under load: rerun it alone before believing it.
- **Data that goes stale after a Wempe geometry or mass change**, regenerate in this order (each `--write`): `wempe_inertia.py` (and `--rev 6mx`), `mechanisms.py --model wempe`, `massprops.py --model wempe`, then `wempe_6mx.js`, `wempe_build.js`, then for `MC_WREV` unset, `build40`, `build`: `wempe_tolerances.js`, `wempe_bench.js`, `wempe_montecarlo.js`; then `wempe_makers.py`, `wempe_navigation.js`, `wempe_essaydata.js` (about 9 min), `python build.py --site-url https://www.marinechronometermodel.com`. If `WESC.SET`'s I, JW or ID no longer match the weighed bodies, `wempe_escapement.js` says so: update them (and `SET6`) by hand. The Wempe report: `node reportdata.js`, `python geometry.py` (give it two hours: its new checks run past 40 min), `python build_report.py`, in `wempe/verification/`.
- **Before/after renders:** `tools/beforeafter.py SPECS.json BEFORE_ROOT AFTER_ROOT OUTDIR --model wempe` (cameras aimed on the AFTER page, the same on both); add each batch's pairs to the artifact page above (its source `index.html` and PNGs were in the previous session's scratchpad; if gone, read it back with the Artifact tool's `read` and republish to the same URL).
- **At most two subagents at a time** (the owner's rule for this work); subagents can't write report files, so have them answer in their final message.

## Task 1: draw the threads

**What is there now.** `shared/kit.js`'s `screw()` (line ~278) draws each shank as a lathe of *rings* (turned grooves, not a helix) at a pitch of `rs × 0.42` (clamped 0.06-0.3), not the screw's metric pitch; the holes are plain bores: tapped holes `hT` at the thread's radius + 0.02, clearance holes `hC` at 1.12 × + 0.01 (kit.js ~155). The only helical threads are the bezel's and the bowl's rim (`wempe/js/case.js`, the `TH`/`yr` helix machinery, ~lines 38-170: an inside and an outside thread on one helix, drawn a constant apart). Every Wempe screw is built through `KIT.screw` (case.js 19 calls, frame.js 11, escapement.js 9, train.js 6, fusee.js and dial.js 1 each); the Hamilton has its own `screw()` in `movement.js` (leave it alone unless asked).

**The task.** Helical ISO metric threads at each screw's drawn size and the standard coarse pitch (M0.7 0.175, M1 0.25, M1.2 0.25, M1.4 0.3, M1.7 0.35, M2 0.4, M2.6 0.45, M4 0.7, M5 0.8, M6 1.0; the sizes are on the sheets and in the README; a screw whose size is estimated keeps its estimate), on the shanks, and the matching internal thread in every tapped hole (`hT`), so a screw and its hole mesh as a thread does: the basic profile (60°, H = 0.866 p, flats p/8 and p/4), external at the maximum material less a small allowance, internal at the minimum, the two a few µm apart so no solid overlaps (state the allowance). Reuse case.js's helix machinery where it fits.

**What to watch.**
- Closed solids, no `DoubleSide`: `solids.py --model wempe` must stay at 0; the STL export too (`gltf_check.py --model wempe`; fusee.js's `snapNear` shows how µm-close corners were handled).
- Cost: hundreds of screws. Keep triangles sane (a helix of 8-12 segments a turn and the shank's 28 round; measure the page's build time and frame cost before and after: `perf.py` for the Wempe if it takes `--model`, else time the build as the batch-3 work did, 3.9 s → 5-8 s was already noticed). Consider threads only on screws a viewer can see, or a level-of-detail switch, if the cost is large; ask the owner before dropping any.
- Every tapped hole is a feature of its part: cutting an internal helix into a plate needs a way that keeps the plate closed (fusee.js's `holeCut`/`bore` cut round holes into turned and extruded parts; a helical bore is new). A first step that leaves the holes plain and threads only the shanks is acceptable if stated.
- The checks that read screws: `fine.py` (shank in hole: the `EXPECTED` thread overlaps and their size limits), `holecheck.py` (a hole must hold its screw), `bom.py` (`tap`/`clear` relations), `audit.py` (screws on their seats), `exploded.py` (screws lift out along their axis). Run each with `--model wempe` and in all five versions where they take `--rev`.
- Isolate a few screws close up (`isolate.py --model wempe NAME --look ...`) beside a photograph that shows threads (Wempe 3955's loose parts, `References/wempe/photos/dg-chrono-dec/`), and render before/after for the page.

## Task 3: verify it can be built, and keep time to navigate by

**What exists** (the Wempe report, sections 12 and 17-21; the README's "Building it", "The build", "Making it", "Navigating by it"): `wempe_tolerances.js`, `wempe_bench.js`, `wempe_montecarlo.js` (2,000 made ones), `wempe_build.js` (both builds' acceptance checks), `wempe_errorbudget.js` (the 6MX TU's 35-day trial, a voyage), `wempe_navigation.js` (the ship's position from a made one), `dfm.py --model wempe` (each machined part against a 0.4 mm end mill and the like), `assembly.py --model wempe` (the order it goes together), `wempe_wear.js`, `wempe_overwind.js`, `wempe_motionwork.js`, `wempe_estimates.js`, `fea.py --model wempe` (the frame under load), `book.py --model wempe` (the build book on paper), `wempe_makers.py` (the maker's sheets).

**Today's baseline** (`node wempe_navigation.js`, 26 October 2026, after 3.32.05): the clock's longitude error at the equator after 30 days, median (95 % of voyages): as drawn 4.2 miles (9.1) — **fails** the fix's probable error, 2 miles; the 1940s build 1.5 (4.17) — passes within sampling; the 1950s build 1.5 (4.15) — passes. `wempe_build.js`: both builds pass (the 1950s 226-237° over the wind, lock windows 193/196 µm, 5.8 s at 30 days). The 1950s build's Monte Carlo: 99.9 % work after the bench.

**The task: an independent, adversarial pass, not a rerun.** The audit changed parts these analyses rest on (the escape pinion, the detent's spring and heights, the endshake 0.06, the fusee's place, the barrel's angles, the mainspring's set, the gimbals' masses and periods). For each analysis: does it read the changed parts or a stale copy of their numbers (a constant duplicated in a tool, a JSON not regenerated)? Do its criteria match its sources (the TU's trial, Bowditch's fix error, GOST 8916-58, the 1958 repair book)? What does it assume that a maker couldn't meet (the bench's adjustments, the tolerances' ranges, materials available in the 1950s)? Then:
1. Buildability: every part through `dfm.py`; every drawn tolerance and fit a maker must hold through `wempe_tolerances.js`/`wempe_bench.js`; the order of assembly (`assembly.py`); the build book (`book.py`); find any part that can't be made or put together as drawn, and any fit the sheets leave impossible (the audit found several: the collet's bore against the 1946 staff, the hour wheel's collar against the dial — README, Readings corrected and Superseded).
2. Timekeeping for navigation: the chain from a made one's swing and rate to the ship's position (`wempe_montecarlo.js` → `wempe_errorbudget.js` → `wempe_navigation.js`), its weakest link quantified; which version passes and why the drawn one doesn't (the low swing: drive and damping are the owner's choices — report, don't change); what a maker must do on the bench to get a passing one.
3. Write the result into the Wempe report (a section or an update to 17-21: method, results, verdict per version, what would change it) — results belong in the HTML report, not the console (the owner's rule) — and a short verdict in the README.

**Out of bounds without asking:** changing the drawn version's drive or damping, the builds' choices, or a check's criteria. Propose; the owner decides.
