# The Hamilton's constants by the ruler they were read with (31 October 2026)

A read-only triage for Phase 0 of `PLAN-hamilton-drawings.md`. The Hamilton model took its absolute scale from one number, the pillar plate's 87.57 mm (a Cas-Ker sale listing). The British Museum's measured Model 21 (Randall and Good 1990, cat. 117A) gives the pillar ("front") plate as 97.8 mm, and Hamilton's factory drawings agree. Every size whose ruler chains back to the 87.57 plate is therefore about **f = 97.8 / 87.57 = 1.11682** too small. Sizes that are absolute in their own right stay as they are.

This file sorts the model's dimensional constants by ruler. It was read from the code at `2c68defa` (branch `claude/hamilton-drawings`). Nothing was measured, run or changed to make it. Line numbers are for that commit. Paths are relative to `marine-chronometer-source/` unless they start at the repository root.

Categories:

- **P**: plate-scaled. The reading was taken on a frame or photograph whose millimetres come from the 87.57 plate. That includes the plate's own rim (r 43.785), the "five holes the bare plate places" homographies, the train bridge rim, `PT`'s photographed group, the side photograph's 18.93 px/mm and its plate edge (3.86), and anything scaled by a model length that is itself P (the model's fusee wheel, `|L.Fu|`, the 37.59 Fu–Ba, `L.F`'s 21.6, the flange's 95.9, the bezel's knurl). Multiply by f.
- **A**: absolute. The size comes from the manual's inches or tables, the 0.249 in impulse roller (or a camera-free ratio to it), Table II, a patent, or a published measurement. Keep it.
- **D**: derived in code from other constants. It follows its inputs, and the table names them. Do not scale a D by hand.
- **M**: mixed or unclear. The notes say what decides it.

**A trap for whoever does the rescale.** Many fusee-group sizes are written as `literal*FK`, and FK = (fu+2)·MOD.fusee/40.87 (movement.js:210). MOD.fusee comes from `L`. If `L` is scaled, FK goes from 0.882 to 0.985 by itself, and every `…*FK` size grows by f automatically. Those literals must **not** be multiplied again. Literals in the same group written without FK (for example `SUT 7.64`, `SRT 16.55`, `WRT 7.4`, `RB_O 18.3`, the chain's `PC`/`H`) must be. The same holds for `PT`: scale `PHOTO_K` and `PHOTO_T`, not the photo-frame literals passed through it.

## Joints: where an A part meets a P part

Rescaling only the P side changes these. Numbers are from the constants above (f = 1.11682).

1. **The balance, the escape wheel and the fourth (`L.B`, `L.E`, `L.F`, `ES`, `rRoll`, `EX`).** \|B−E\| = 9.40 is designed. It is the 0.249 in roller (r 3.163) and the 13.16 wheel (r 6.58), overlapping by 0.34 for the crescent's dip and Op. 84's 0.002 in roller shake, so it stays. Plain ×f would make it 10.50 and the escapement would not work. Two ways to re-solve:
   - Keep E's measured direction about B (38.9°). Then E′ = (9.114, 17.379) and \|F′−E′\| = 11.34. The fourth stage's module is 0.2668, and its tips r 10.27 against the drawing's 42073 OD 21.76 (r 10.88).
   - Put E′ 9.40 from B′ and 11.90 from F′ (the drawing's 75:10 at 0.28). Then E′ = (9.467, 16.912), 3.6° round from the measured direction, module 0.28.

   The drawings favour the second. The first holds the frames' direction (±0.6°), so the second's 3.6° would have to be the frames' error or B's (B rests on `PT`, ±0.5). Either way the escape group stops being a scaled copy of its surroundings, and:
   - The train bridge's passage `TB_KEY` (r 6.89 about E, 0.31 round the tips) and the lower bridge's concave edge (r 7.12 about (11.48, 17.46), "0.18 outside the tips") become r 7.70 and 7.95 if scaled. The gaps open to 1.1 and about 1.0, offset by however far E′ is from E×f: 1.06 mm (E×f = (9.969, 18.068)) for the first, 1.26 for the second. The passage and the lower bridge's opening should be re-placed on E′ and re-read against the frames at the new scale.
   - The escape upper bridge (`S.eb` ±8.3, `ebp`, `ebc`, `TB_SEAT`) is built from E, so it moves with E′ but scales about it.
   - B to the fourth's setting goes from 11.44 to 12.77. The slab's measured 10.8-11.8 was P, so it becomes 12.1-13.2, which still agrees.
2. **The balance (A, r 14.5 with its screws and weights) against the cock and the barrel bridge (P).** The barrel bridge's cut round the balance (`BB_CUT`, r 20.06 about a point 0.44 from B, "19.5 at its nearest" to the screws' reach) becomes r 22.40, so the gap grows by about 2.3 mm. The cock's body wall `CK_RP` 22.6 becomes 25.24 and `CK_WR` 21.55 becomes 24.07, gaps up by about 2.6. The locking arm's finger (15.6 from the staff, 0.02 clear of a timing weight) must stay A. Its screw (20 from the staff, wedged between the cock's foot and the escape upper bridge) is placed by P parts, so the arm's length and the swing `ARM_U` are re-solved. Relative to the plate the balance shrinks from 0.331 of its diameter to 0.297. PLAN Phase 0's "balance against the plate in one frame" is the check.
3. **The balance (A) in the P height stack.** `BAL_Y`, `LB_T`, `CK_T` and `EY` scale, but the rim's 3.5 height, the hub and the roller's place on the staff do not. The staff's length (≈25.75 between pivots, from `LB_T` and `CK_T`) grows to about 28.8. Its diameters were scaled by that length, so its collar r 1.31 becomes 1.46 and its hub seat 0.92 becomes 1.03. Those sit inside A bores: the hub sleeve 3.24 and flange 7.0 were read against the roller. So either the staff's diameters are A too (the watchdoc photograph's scale is the model's own length, which is P), or the A bores must grow. This needs a decision. The escape wheel's level `EY` and the roller's level on the staff must still meet, which is consistent if both scale. The vertical gaps balance-to-cock and balance-to-lower-bridge open by about 1-1.5 mm.
4. **The hairspring (A) against the stud and cock (P).** The coil r 5.1 stays and the stud's clamp moves from r 3.83 to 4.28. The outer terminal curve's run from coil to clamp changes, so it must be re-solved to Phillips' conditions (`hairspring.js`, `hairspring-fe.js`, `essay_hamdata.py --write`: `hsOut` 14.9, `hsIn` 21.8). The spring's height `HS_H` goes from about 6.4 to 7.15, close to 6:49.5's 7.3, which is open in PROVENANCE.
5. **The detent (P lengths stored in ES units) against the escape wheel and roller (A).** `BL` (the locking face 10.21 from the flexure), `PSL`, `SPL`, the trip spring 6.45 and `DBLK`'s holes all came through the plate-placed pins (10.12 apart). If they scale, BL becomes 11.40 mm. That is the old "Fig. 90 scaled by an 11.3 mm flexure-to-jewel" layout, which was set aside on 20 October 2026 as "12-17 % large", so f vindicates it. The detent's angle `DA` 68° and the lock's place on the wheel stay A, which moves the flexure point and the block. Then the escapement's work and the detent's ring change: `ID`, `tripping.js` (137 Hz scales about as 1/L², roughly −20 %), `dynamics.js`, `escgolden.json`. Drawing 42087 gives the blank absolutely (1.355 × .140 × .045 in, foot hole to slot .3947 in = 10.03). That is the arbiter.
6. **The key against the squares and holes.** The key's socket is 2.46 square and its pipe Ø 6 (M ruler). The fusee square 2.4 becomes 2.68 (drawing 2.79) and the cannon square 2.16 becomes 2.41. Scaled, the fusee square no longer fits the key's socket, so the socket must go to about 2.85 (the drawing's .110 plus clearance). The pipe's Ø 6 must pass the case's key hole (r 3.34, becoming 3.73) and the box socket (r 3.55, becoming 3.96). Those only get looser. The key's 98 length against the box's 41-deep socket and the case's depth: if the key stays A while the box scales, it stands about 4 mm lower in the block. Either scale the key (it was never measured absolutely on a Hamilton) or treat it as A. The square is what matters (`overwind.js` reads `KR`, `KP`).
7. **The case and box (both P) against the movement (P).** These scale together, so no joint, provided all of `box.js` is scaled. `LATCH_X` (BOX_W − BOX_T − LATCH_H − 0.5) and `RI` (CR + 16.5) are D-plus-estimate expressions that follow. The case's `FD` (49 to the bottom) against the balance cock's −37.96: the 4.8 clearance becomes 5.4.
8. **The mainspring and the drive (P) against the escapement (A).** The scaled strip (t 0.440, width 15.13, length 1188) in the scaled barrel gives torque ∝ b·t²·(turns) ≈ f³ = 1.39 times as much, while the fusee's and train's ratios are scale-free and the escapement's calibrated torque is A. The budget gap already recorded (`hamilton_transmission.js`'s KDRV: the solved spring gives 1.6 × makeEsc's calibrated torque) would grow to about 2.2 unless pivot radii (×f) and the train's friction close part of it. `mainspring.py` (about 20 min), `mainspring_friction.py`, `physics.js`, `drive.js`, `tolerances.js` and `bench.js` have to be rerun before TF 67 can be judged.
9. **Wheel and arbor inertias.** P parts' masses go ×1.39 and their inertias ×1.74. `JW` (5.65 g·mm²), the fourth's reflected 0.81 and the escape arbor's change while the balance's I (A) does not. That shifts the escapement's chase (`dynamics.js`, `traindyn.js`). The gimbals' bowl mass and inertia change, and with them `GIM.W0` (the 0.58/0.65 s periods).

## Open questions (where the comments don't settle the ruler)

1. **What the 87.57 × 3.86 listing measured.** If it was the upper train bridge (PLAN Phase 0), the 3.86 is that bridge's thickness. That would mean the side photograph's 3.86 edge, which scales every height, was not the plate's either. Its 75 px gave 18.93 px/mm, "agreeing to 2 %" with the 1658 px width, so the heights stay P×f as long as the width reading holds. The museum's frame height of 46.6 is the check. The model's plate-to-train-bridge-top is 26.5 scaled, its cock top 42.4 scaled. What does the catalogue's 46.6 span: the frame with the barrel bridge, the cock, the squares?
2. **Where `BAL_R` 29 first came from.** PROVENANCE ("seam 5") lists "the balance rim's 29 mm" as still from the top-view photograph, a P frame. Yet 6:49.8 makes the rim/roller ratio agree with 0.249 in to 1 %. If the photograph's group is P, a balance read on it at 29 would be 29·f = 32.4 true, which contradicts the roller. Either the 29 was read through another scale (the photograph's "fusee wheel taken as 40.87", which happens to be about the drawn 40.97) or the roller check is what set it. The roller ratio is decisive for A either way, but confirm on 34:08-34:16.
3. **The balance staff's diameters**: P (scaled by the model's 25.75 pivot-to-pivot) or A (they must fit the roller-scaled hub sleeve 3.24 and flange 7.0)? Joint 3.
4. **`RIT` 1.47** (impulse roller thickness, "measured") and the jewel stones' sizes: which scale were they read at?
5. **The escape wheel's 13.16** is a quotation of a Hamilton specification in a forum post, not a drawing. f makes the escape tooth thickness 1.23 against that specification's 1.3, which supports the specification. Is 13.16 also right, or should the escape pinion's module 0.28 with the measured direction decide E (Joint 1)?
6. **E's place after the rescale**: the frames' direction about B (fourth stage module 0.267) or the drawing's 11.90 to the fourth (0.28, 3.6° round)? It also depends on whether B itself (via `PT`, ±0.5) moves.
7. **The barrel arbor**: its square comes out 2.53 scaled against the drawing's 2.79, and its core 10.9 against the collar's 13.2. The barrel's inside comes out 36.4 against 38.10. These parts disagree by more than f, so something other than scale is involved: wrong readings, or a different design.
8. **The case thread** (pitch 1.1 → 1.23 scaled): read as 24 tpi before. Is it 20 tpi (1.27)?
9. **The winding key's ruler** (a modern maker's Ø 6 pipe): keep it A, or scale it with the box it was read beside (0:45, the box at the bezel's scale)?
10. **Readings made on P frames but stated against A parts.** For example, the hub flange 7.0 and sleeve 3.24 on 34:12 were read "against the roller's 1,012 px", so they are A. Some others say "scaled by the cap screws' 6.66" or "the bushing ring 7.03 in both frames", which are P. Rows not covered above (the escape bridges' and caps' screws, the cock's endstone cap, the dust seals, the setup cover, the stop-bar 15.0 × 1.8) should be checked line by line against their comment's ruler. The default is P, since nearly every frame in VIDEOS.md is "at the plate's 87.57".
11. **The single factor.** f = 1.1168 comes from the museum's plate. The drawings' ratios run 1.115-1.15, and the fusee wheel's tips and the barrel come out 1.7 % and 2.5 % short at f. One global factor may leave the fusee group about 2 % small. PLAN Phase 0's "factor fitted over every drawn size at once" should come before choosing 1.117 or a fitted value.
