The Wempe Einheits-Chronometer: does the model hold to its drawings, and would it keep time?

The model of the German standard marine chronometer of the 1940s is built from Wempe's works drawings, each sheet as last revised. Two questions follow from that. Does the model hold to its drawings: is every part a closed solid where its sheets put it, clear of its neighbours, counted against the drawing list, every figure read and every guess said to be one? And would a chronometer made to those drawings keep time: does the escapement run, does the fusee even the spring, does the energy budget close, do the teeth and springs hold, does it stay in rate from cold to hot and at sea, and does it pass the trial its Soviet copy, the 6MX, was made to pass? Every figure below comes from a script in the repository, run on the model's own numbers, and is shown as the script prints it: ok a check that holds, ~~ one the script reports but doesn't fail on (an open question, or the strong end of a range only), !! a failure.

Approach

The Wempe's checks are the Hamilton's, pointed at the second model (--model wempe), with tools of their own where the two chronometers differ: the Wempe's detent carries a gold passing spring on its own horn, its fusee's force was measured by its makers on the drawing itself, its balance is a cut bimetallic one with movable masses, and its hairspring is a helix with drawn terminal curves. The tools run on the same files the page draws from (js/escapement.js, js/escphys.js, the shared makeEsc), so what they check is what the page shows.

1. Geometry: does the model hold to its drawings?

The model is a set of closed solids, as a CAD model is, so it can be checked as one. Five browser checks open the Wempe's page itself and measure the parts as built. Solids: every mesh closed (no open edge), consistently wound and with a positive volume, as loaded and in a section with Moving parts only. Fine interference: every part voxelized at 0.05 mm in states (the escapement through its unlocking, impulse and return; the train at many phases; the fusee from full wind through winding to run down), every pair of parts that overlaps reported; only the contacts listed below, each with its reason and a size it may not exceed, may overlap. The screw audit: screws overlapping, screws with nothing under their heads, arbor and pin ends sitting in nothing, faces lying on each other, parts touching nothing, in the movement and in the case and box, against the leftovers expected. Exploded: the Spread view lifts each part on its own path; no two may pass through each other. The parts list: every line of bom.json, transcribed from the drawing list, counted on the model's pieces, and its relations measured on the built meshes.

The contacts that are meant

Every overlap fine.py finds is one of these, each a joint the drawings make that a turned or extruded solid can't show cut: a screw in a thread not cut across a turned body, a pressed fit drawn at its fit's middle, the chain in its groove. The measured column is the largest overlap over all the states, against the size it may reach.

2. The sources: what the drawings say, and what was guessed

The model's evidence is, in order: the works drawings (sheets of Wempe's, as published by the Deutsche Gesellschaft für Chronometrie, each read as last revised); the coloured section of the whole movement; photographs of real German movements (Wempe 3955 above all); and, for what only the Soviet copy shows, the 6MX's own documents. None of them is reproduced here: they are other people's work, cited and linked from the model's page. 3. The escapement

The Wempe's spring detent escapement is laid out on one sheet, 05 04_1 (Hemmung für Feder-Chronometer, 10:1): the escape wheel 10.00 mm from the balance, the locking stone 36.5° on from the line of centres, the impulse 21° either side of it. Its parts come from their own sheets: the escape wheel's tooth (05 04_23), the detent and its gold passing spring (1436, 05 04 01-2), the rollers and the balance.

3.1 Solved from the layout (makeEsc)

Method. The shared escapement solver, makeEsc, takes the layout's points put in its frame, the parts' shapes and bodies (the balance weighed on the model's solids, the train behind the escape wheel, the detent about its flexure), and solves the escapement as the Hamilton's is solved: the wheel released from rest chasing the impulse stone, the strike's loss, the unlocking's work, the detent's fall and ride on the tooth it has just let go; then the swing at which the escapement's work each oscillation balances the balance's damping, at the drive 05 06_20's measured force gives through the train. The rate is Airy's sum of the escapement's disturbances with the drawn hairspring's own isochronism (section 8).

3.2 In time (dynamics.js)

Method. The balance, the escape wheel with the whole train behind it, and the detent move as bodies through every contact, beat by beat, on contact geometry worked out apart from makeEsc's: the bevelled discharge stone on the gold spring's square end by separating axes, the stone on the tooth it let go from the tooth's drawn arcs. The result is held against makeEsc's: the swing, where the tooth lands, the detent's fall and its home, the rate and its isochronism. Then the detent's estimates are varied, the chronometer is started by a twist, starved of drive, knocked, and taken to sea.

3.3 The detent as a frame (tripping.js)

Method. The detent becomes a plane frame: beam elements with stretching, bending and their consistent mass for the spring (0.07 × 1.9 mm, clamped at its foot), the blade's two sections, the horn and the gold spring along its drawn path; the square with the gold spring's head and the pipe with the stone rigid. Its modes carry it in time with the balance and the wheel through elastic contacts: the discharge stone on the gold spring, the gold spring on the horn, the pipe on its banking (the block's cross screw), the tooth on the stone and on the impulse stone. Validated rigid against dynamics.js, it is then run as drawn, with its estimates varied, pushed to larger swings, and knocked.

4. The mainspring and the fusee

The drawing set has a sheet no other chronometer's drawings are known to have: 05 06_20 (Herb. Müller, 24.7.41), the fusee's curve and its measured force, the pull in grams on a lever 262 mm long over the whole wind. It gives the strip (15 × 0.37) but not its length or free shape.

Method. The Hamilton's mainspring.py solves the strip in the drawn barrel (Ø 38.5 on the arbor's Ø 13.3 core) for its shape of least bending energy at every quarter turn, coils pressing on each other, its torque the energy's slope. Its free curve is the Hamilton's measured one scaled to the length, its turns scaled by a set. Set up ¾ turn (the sheet's "3/4 Umgang gespannt") and wound through the drawn fusee and chain, its torque on the fusee arbor at each of the sheet's readings is set against the measured force, each scaled to its mean. Twelve lengths and sets were solved; the best fit is the model's spring.

The level is the drawing's open figure.

5. The energy budget

Method. Where the drive goes, from the mainspring through the fusee and chain, the train and the escapement to the balance's damping, each loss from the Wempe's own parts: the coils' friction (section 4), the barrel on its journals, the chain's links on their rivets, the fusee arbor's pivots; each mesh tooth by tooth (section 6) and each pivot on its tooth loads; the escapement's strike and unlocking (makeEsc). The balance's damping, which no Wempe source gives, is built from its parts: the air's drag on its masses, timing weights, screws and arm (a pressure drag at the masses' Reynolds number), the endstone's friction under the balance's weight, the spring's own. Then: does some balance within its parts' damping swing the sources' band on the measured drive?

6. The going train, tooth by tooth

Method. The train is four meshes: the fusee wheel (90) on the centre pinion (14), centre wheel 90 on the intermediate pinion 12, intermediate 80 on the seconds pinion 10, seconds 80 on the escape pinion 10; every count, module, tip and root from the wheels' tables, the two pinions no sheet gives fixed by the drawn centres. gearing.js takes the page's own outlines, places each pair at its drawn holes, and for 120 steps through a pitch turns the pinion back against its load until the outlines touch, giving the torque it receives with the teeth's friction (µ 0.10-0.20, steel leaves on brass). The four ratios as their wheels turn drive the balance through the escapement's equation over six hours. Then each mesh's centres are moved until it jams or its friction takes the swing out of the model's band: the window the holes must hold.

7. The parts' strength

Method. Fully wound, at the measured level of the drive, every stage lossless (the largest forces): the mainspring's bending stress; the chain's plates, rivets and hooks under its pull; the band; each mesh's teeth by Lewis' formula (Y 0.2, clock teeth); the pivots as cantilevers carrying their arbor's whole load; the maintaining spring, ratchet and click wheel; a strong hand on the key against the stop. Each against its material's published range: the margin at its weak and its strong end (~~ where only the strong end holds).

8. The hairspring and Phillips' curves

The hairspring is a helix (05 03_6: 0.285 × 0.52, Ø 12.8, left-hand), its ends bent to a curve drawn on 05 03_7 as a table of radii every 10°, and cut, before the curves are bent, "auf 13 Umgänge −3,5 mm": to 13 turns less 3.5 mm.

Method. The length is read four ways and each gives the balance a period. The curve's table is fitted as two compass arcs and tested against Phillips' conditions (1861): its centroid on the perpendicular to the junction's radius, at R²/l from the staff, so that the spring pulls the balance with a pure couple. The force it leaves on the pivots under a couple follows (HSPR.lateral), with the helix's turns varied. At large swings the drawn spring is solved as an inextensible elastica by shooting and again as co-rotational beam elements; the two must agree on the couple's shape, the force and the rate.

9. Temperature: the cut bimetallic balance

A steel hairspring weakens as it warms, so the balance must open to compensate: the rim, steel inside and brass outside, cut beside the arm, curls in as it warms and carries its four masses toward the staff. How much depends on where the masses sit on the free arc.

Method. Each quarter of the rim is a bimetal cantilever from the arm's edge to the cut, curling by Timoshenko's bimetal (1925), each element's radius moving by the layers' mean growth, the masses clamping the rim over their slot. Every part is weighed on the model's solids. The spring's own loss is its thermoelastic coefficient's (−240 to −270 × 10⁻⁶) with its expansion. The rim's "Nickelstahl Sonderlegierung" is read as Guillaume's 42-44 % nickel steel (estimated); a plain nickel steel is tried beside it. The secondary error (Dent's) is taken as the integral balance's of the Neuchâtel trials.

10. The case and gimbals

Method. The bowl with the movement in it and the gimbal ring are weighed on the model, every closed solid at its material's density (massprops.py --model wempe): mass, centre of gravity and inertia about the gimbal centre. 05 01 says to move the case brackets "bis Gleichgewicht erreicht ist", until it hangs level, and the tool does so. The two bodies then run on their pivots under the Hamilton's seaway (gimbals.js: narrow-band random roll, pitch, yaw and heave; NORDFORSK's 0.12 g limit), and what reaches the balance's staff is run through the Wempe's escapement.

11. The 6MX's trial and a voyage

The Soviet copy of the Einheits-Chronometer, the 6MX, was made to a published specification (TU 25-07.1533-84) with a trial: 35 days at 36, 20, 4, 20 and 36 °C, the first two days of each period not counted, wound daily, its figures worked as E (the mean variation of the daily rate), R, D, C (the temperature coefficient), S (the secondary error), each period's mean, and N = 42E + 4.5R + 60C + 2.4S. No Wempe test card is to hand, so the trial is the 6MX's.

Method. Each day's rate is summed from its parts: the temperature curve (section 9, with the masses set by the adjuster at their compensating place to ±0.25°), the air's density, the swing over the daily wind, the day-to-day scatter and drift (the Hamilton's, calibrated on its No. 3390's card), the barometer and the oil; the trial is drawn 2,000 times. Then a 60-day voyage from a winter port into the tropics, rated over ten days in port, gives the error a navigator would carry.

12. Building it: tolerances, the bench, the Monte Carlo

The checks above take the Wempe as drawn. A chronometer made to the drawings is made to tolerances, some the sheets give and some they don't, and is then tested and adjusted on the bench as its makers did. Three tools follow it there, as the Hamilton's tolerances.js, bench.js and montecarlo.js do. They are guidance for anyone who would make one, proved by their own runs: the model was never made.

12.1 How closely the unsized parts must be made

Method. From the escapement as the page runs it (makeEsc with the Wempe's settings, driven by 05 06_20's force through the train), the swing and rate are solved with the escape wheel's torque and the detent's and gold spring's stiffness each scaled (a strip's stiffness as its thickness cubed). Each part's range is the one that keeps the swing in the model's guard band, 193-270°, over the whole 56-hour wind; the sources' 225-270° is reported beside it. The parts: the mainspring's strip (05 06_20's "15 × 0,37", no tolerance), the detent's spring (1436's faint "0,07") and its preload, the gold spring, the depth of lock (05 04_1's 0.175, set by the banking), and the hairspring against the period, which the timing weights must take up.

12.2 The tests on the bench

Method. Each test turns the model's figures into what a made part or movement must read, and how to measure it with a maker's means: the mainspring's pull against 05 06_20, the fusee's evenness, the depths, the endshake from the sheets' fits, the balance vibrated, its free run-down, the detent's ring, the escapement's figures and the banking's preload, the swing, the maintaining spring, and the 6MX TU's 35-day trial as the acceptance test (the Wempe's sheets give no trial of their own).

12.3 The whole movement made to tolerances

Method. 2,000 movements, each drawing every tolerance at once (uniform within the sheets' limits where they give them, normal where estimated): the arbors' holes, every term of each arbor's endshake stack from its sheet, the mainspring's strip, the teeth's friction, the detent's and gold spring's strips, the wheel's tips and the banking (the depth of lock), the preload, the hairspring's strip, coil, cut length and modulus. Each is judged as made (the train turns, every arbor free, the escapement unlocks and locks in its window, the swing in the guard band over the wind, it times within the weights' reach), then after the bench's tests as a maker applies them (bench_wempe.json's figures): a close mesh re-bushed, endshake set again, a detent spring out of its band replaced, the banking and preload set, another strip, the set-up turned, the hairspring matched to the balance.

13. The whole transmission in time

Sections 3 to 7 take the movement a mechanism at a time, each with the others held still or rigid. Here every part that moves is one system: the barrel on its solved mainspring, the chain, the fusee with its two clicks, the maintaining ratchet, spring and detent, the fusee wheel and the going train to the escape wheel, the motion work and hands, the up-and-down work, the key and the stop: each a body with its inertia weighed on the model's solids (or estimated, said where), joined by the elements a real movement has, none of them linearised.

Method. wempe_transmission.js. The elements: the mainspring's torque as solved (section 4) with its coils' friction as a stick-slip element, so winding takes more than running gives; the chain in tension only, so it can go slack, its lever on the fusee where it lies and rising and falling as each 2.05 mm link comes onto a drum; each pawl (the clicks, the maintaining detent) a body on its pivot under its spring, riding the teeth's backs and catching a face only if it is down; the maintaining spring's drawn ring, stiffening as it closes, with its bank; every mesh tooth by tooth on the page's own outlines (section 6's), the driven gear's place under load through each pitch, the backlash to its back flank and the teeth's friction, contact on either flank or neither; the cannon pinion's friction fit, which slips past its torque; the key's one-way coil spring (it grips whenever the hand holds the key against the fusee, and slips only if the key is turned the wrong way); the stop bar on its pin. The escapement is dynamics.js's own (section 3.2), its escape wheel driven each step by the train through the escape pinion's mesh. It is stepped in time at a microsecond. The stiffnesses no sheet gives (the teeth's give, the chain's, the clicks' faces, the bank) are estimated from the parts, and those the results rest on are varied.

14. Against a period text

The drawings decide the Wempe; a period text says how a detent escapement was usually proportioned, and so where the Wempe's makers chose otherwise. keystone.js --model wempe measures the escapement against Watch and Clock Escapements (The Keystone, 1904), Ch. Wempe 3955's Bakelite bowl (16_Wempe_3955_Gehäusetopf_Kardan.jpg) is beaded and rounded, not R-05 01 00-1's 40° cone: an undrawn bowl. The 6MX's TU gives the whole chronometer ≤ 2.2 kg (6MX-figures.md, TU 1.2.8), where the model's bowl and ring alone weigh 3.26 kg: if the TU's mass leaves out its box, the bowl would be about 280 g, near the sheet's 350 (moderately sure the brass bowl is too heavy; whether the TU's "масса" counts the box isn't said). The machined bowl's sheet (05 01 00-1 without the R-) would settle it.
    • The bezel: screwed onto the bowl (likely): Wempe 5377's bezel is knurled at its lower edge (bonhams_26816_327_wempe5377_type05.jpg, sure; its case later), Wempe 3955's edge serrated (moderately), Wempe 8711's "threaded glazed bezel" (Antiquorum's text, catalogue-de-en.md), and 05 01 01 draws a thread's zigzag hidden in the lower bore (about 3 teeth over 4.4: likely); the 6MX's bowls (KirPol 24020, leben-des-6mx/13_, 14_KirPol_24020_E-Kontakt*.jpg; Kirow 247 12_Kirow_247_E-Kontakt_3.jpg) have a thin threaded collar at the top, about 5 turns at about 1 pitch, the ring's flange on a step inside it. The thread isn't sized on any sheet, and the drawn sizes (the bezel's bore Ø 106, the flange's Ø 105.95) leave no metal for a collar: not built.
    • The ring's pin: in a notch in the bezel at 12 (built, estimated). A screwed bezel can't hold a notch to the pin, and KirPol 24020's bowl has the notch cut down through its threaded collar at 12 o'clock, about 1.6 wide and 4-5 deep (sure for the 6MX); so the Wempe's is likelier in the bowl, but 05 01 01 lays the flange in the bezel's recess, above the bowl's top face, where only the bezel meets the pin. Left as built until the machined bowl's sheet shows a seat.
    • The closing spring (05 01 00-7): no sheet; hidden under the plate on Wempe 3955 and in moMvyx8eXfo 7:10-7:45.
    • The Feststeller: on Wempes 4585 (rmg_L5393_wempe_wartime_001.jpg) and 5377 a knurled knob about Ø 20-21 on a short post, on a flat lever on a block on the right wall, with an upright pin about Ø 4 on the lever between the knob and the ring, where Schnitt A-B draws a circle Ø 8.6 under the lever (built Ø 8, as the sheet); the knob's top about 10 (5377) and 17-19 (4585) over the lever against the model's 29 (rough readings in perspective; the "17" from the front wall agrees). A side-on photograph of a Wempe's lock with the ring in place would settle the heights.
    • The Zunge (P. 51 00 02-2): on Wempes 4585 (aluminium) and 5377 (brass) the box's latch is an angle plate at the front's middle on the body, its top flange slotted about 6.5 long, with a knob about Ø 7 beside the slot, and an angle plate on the lid with a C-shaped hook into that slot; the model's tongue swinging on the lid's front over a keeper doesn't match. The Zunge (iron 10 × 4, 45 long, the M3.5 for a knob, a thinned round end) fits as the bolt behind the body's plate, which the knob moves to catch the hook (inferred); how it moves isn't seen. The 6MX's boxes have escutcheon hasps instead.
    • The Holzkasten's own sheet (127-556.02) isn't in the set; the 1943 key is its assembly's (127-556.02 U 01, parts -01 to -05 on 05 01_6), which fits its holder in the box.
