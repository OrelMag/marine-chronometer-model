# CLAUDE.md

This file provides guidance to Claude Code (claude.ai/code) when working with code in this repository.

## What this repo is

A static, browser-only project about the Hamilton Model 21 marine chronometer (three.js r128 and the fonts vendored in `vendor/`; no npm, no bundler, no test suite):

- `marine-chronometer-source/chronometer-working-model/`: the interactive 3D working model of the Hamilton Model 21 (on the site at `/hamilton/` since 21 October 2026). Its Essay tab is a link to the essay's own page. The model's README documents files, coordinates, timing and sources in detail; read it before changing geometry or timing.
- `marine-chronometer-source/essay/`: **The Marine Chronometer**, a long-form interactive essay (Ciechanowski-style) on how the chronometer keeps time at sea, shared by both models, on a page of its own (`/essay/#section` on the site; the Hamilton's Essay tab until 21 October 2026): its text follows the Hamilton Model 21, its 3D figures and dial are the Wempe's, built by the Wempe's own scripts (the owner's choice, 21 October 2026). Its own `build.py` writes `dist/the-marine-chronometer.html` (committed). The Hamilton model README's "The essay" has the details.
- `marine-chronometer-source/shared/`: `escapement.js` (`makeEsc`, the escapement's solver, also run by Node in `tools/escapement.js`; `makeEsc(o, base)` solves another chronometer's, base its settings and its reference) and `escplan.js` (`drawEscPlan`, its plan drawing, for the walkthrough's inset, the adjuster's bench and the essay), and `almanac.js` (`ALM`, a nautical almanac and the navigator's arithmetic for the essay's sky sections, also run by Node in `tools/almanac.js`; the model README's "The almanac"), and `contacts.js` (`CONTACTS`, the Contacts debug view: the Wempe's viewer draws it for a page that gives it `cfg.contacts`, the Hamilton's `app.js` binds it itself; `tools/contacts.py`), and `select.js` (`PSEL`, the selection: Ctrl-click or Select, Alt-drag or Lasso, its parts faded, hidden or isolated together; bound by the Wempe's viewer and the Hamilton's `app.js`).
- Root `chronometer-working-model.html` is the published single-file copy of the build, fully self-contained (three.js and fonts inlined). The model's own `build.py` writes `dist/chronometer-working-model.html` (committed); the root build runs it. `python build.py` at the root regenerates it, the root copy and `site/` (the upload folder, committed); commit the regenerated root copy and `site/` with source changes. The root `README.md` is the user guide: setup, build, checks and publishing. On 21 October 2026 the site's addresses were swapped: the Wempe is the home page (`site/index.html`), the Hamilton `/hamilton/`, the essay `/essay/`; the reports and the manual kept theirs. `worker.js` redirects `/wempe/` to `/?from=wempe` and the essay's first address (`/marine-chronometer`) to `/essay/`; the Wempe's page sends the Hamilton's old links on (its `index.html`'s script: `#essay=…` to the essay, the Hamilton's own keys to `/hamilton/`; its `app.js`'s `away`: a part, view, panel or step it hasn't). `tools/sitecheck.py` checks every address, old and new.
- `marine-chronometer-source/wempe/`: the second model, the German Wempe Einheits-Chronometer (spring detent, fusee, three pillars), its own page (the site's home page, `site/index.html`, since 21 October 2026; `/wempe/` until then), built from Wempe's works drawings in `References/wempe/` (its README; what's read off each sheet in `transcriptions/`), which decide its geometry as the manual and photographs decide the Hamilton's. It loads the Hamilton's `core.js` by relative path, `shared/kit.js` (`KIT`: `part`, `screw` and the plan helpers, copied from `movement.js`) and `shared/viewer.js` (`VIEWER`: the generic viewer lifted from `app.js`); its dimensions are in `js/dims.js` (`WD`, each with its drawing number). The tools take `--model wempe` (`tools/model.py`; its tables in `wempe/checks.py`). Its page has the Hamilton's Moving parts only (`drive=1`), Train, Fusee, Balance and Laid out views (Laid out: its `movement.js`'s `develop(e)`, the viewer's `dev`), tick sound, Swing and isochronism (`WEDATA.swing`), Rate and timing weights, the 35-day trial (`WEDATA.trial`'s `airK`, `Sint`, `sum`) Escapement in plan (the model's pieces flattened, as its essay's; no adjuster's bench), the walkthrough (`tour=N`), Contacts (a debug view of where parts touch, come close or overlap: `contacts=1|sweep`; `shared/contacts.js`, its data `js/contactdata.js` from `checks.py` by `tools/contacts.py --write`) Making it (the Hamilton's `js/maker.js`, shared, in the Wempe's words: each card's maker's sheet and drawing, Measure, the build book, Data; its data `js/makers.js` from `tools/wempe_makers.py`) and Download (STL of the movement or, on a part's card, the part; the export to Blender: `wempe/js/blender.js`, lifted from the Hamilton's, with the Hamilton's `gltf.js` and rig) (the viewer's `cfg.drive` and `cfg.TOUR`; the Wempe README's "The page's capabilities against the Hamilton's" lists what was ported and what was left out).
- `vendor/`: three.js r128 (verified against cdnjs's SRI hash) and Latin-subset woff2 fonts with their licences. The page references them as `../../vendor/...`; `inline.py` inlines them at build time (and any local `<img>` file), and the root build fails if the page still loads a script, stylesheet, font or image from the network.
- `site-assets/`: `social.png` (the link preview, rendered by `tools/social.py`), `social-movement.png` (the mechanism titled for the essay, for posting; the same tool), `social-wempe.png` (the Wempe page's link preview; `social.py wempe`) and `_headers`, copied into `site/`; the build also copies the manual's scan from `References/` into `site/` as `navships-250-624-1948.pdf`, which the page links to beside itself; and the checks' two reports (`verification/build-verification/`, `verification/real-chronometers/`, as their `build_report.py` last wrote them) into `site/verification/` at the same paths, their `../../tools/` links pointed at GitHub, linked from About and the essay (rebuild a report before the build when what it reports changed).
- `References/` holds the source material: the 1948 NAVSHIPS 250-624 overhaul manual PDF, reference photographs and drawings. Its README lists each file, what the model took from it and its original filename. `References/VIDEOS.md` catalogues videos of real Model 21s (teardowns and a 4K restoration): what each shows minute by minute, every measurement taken on them (the train's tooth counts came from one), the methods (`tools/video.py`) and which open gaps they could close. The videos and their frames stay outside the repository.
- **No physical chronometer:** the user has no real chronometer and never will: neither an original Hamilton Model 21 nor one built from the model. Never ask them for measurements, recordings, photographs of their own, test equipment or bench results, and don't plan work that waits on them. Everything is settled from the sources (the manual, other people's photographs and videos, published literature) and the model's own analysis; what only a made chronometer could settle stays an estimate, and says so. The build-oriented tools (bench.js, benchcheck.py, rating.py, the build book, the jigs) are guidance for any maker, proved by their own demos, not a queue of the user's tasks.
- **Source of truth:** the manual, photographs of real Model 21s and videos of real Model 21s decide what the model looks like and does. Where the model disagrees with them, the model is wrong; estimates, clearance fits and earlier choices in the code give way. Say how sure each reading from a photograph or video is, and keep what is inferred or estimated in the model README's "Estimated, not from the manual" section.
- **Build from measured lines:** every outline, size and place you draw or change is measured first, on a photograph (`References/`), a video frame (`References/VIDEOS.md`, `tools/video.py frame`) or the manual's figure or text: traced on the image and put in millimetres through the tools (`video.py anchor`/`fit`/`unproj`, `rimfit.py`, `framecam.py`, `fcal.py`, `holes.py`), or read off a dimension the manual gives. Trace lines and profiles: every edge and every side profile (silhouettes, undersides, steps, wall profiles) is traced on the image as points and built from them, never a stock form (a quarter-circle cove, a straight bar, a plain cylinder) in place of a line a frame shows; before deciding a profile can't be read, sheet the video round the moment for a frame that shows it side-on. Never draw a shape by eye, from memory, from a generic chronometer or to suit clearance alone. The code comment beside it names the source (video ID and mm:ss, photograph, figure or Op.) and the measured numbers; where no source shows a line, say so, keep it an estimate in the README's "Estimated" section, and fit it as little as the measured lines around it allow. Measure with the `measure-trace` skill (`.claude/skills/measure-trace/`, its engine `tools/mfgfit.py`) for every refinement of a part's shape or place; Claude does the measuring itself and never hands the owner a tracing task (the owner's rule, 20 October 2026). Split each traced edge into runs: a run whose points fit a circle within the trace's own scatter is a circle, built as an exact arc (centre and radius), never a polyline through the points, and a run that fits a line is a line (the owner, 18 October 2026; the hook `trace_circles_reminder.py` reminds Claude when a trace comes in).
- `RESOLVED.md` lists bugs already found and fixed (from the commit history), grouped by area, each with what to keep true. Read the relevant section before changing a part, control or build step, and don't reintroduce anything listed. When a change fixes a bug, add an entry in the same commit, under its heading, with the hash (entries for uncommitted fixes go under "Fixed, not yet committed"). `OPEN-QUESTIONS.md` lists the readings no source has settled (what is known, what conflicts, what was searched, what would settle each); `REQUESTS.md` is the post to send owners and the NAWCC for them; `IDEAS.md` holds ideas, `Review-results.md` open review findings and `PERFORMANCE.md` the frame-time measurements, what was done and what's next; neither belongs in `RESOLVED.md`. `PROVENANCE.md` is the Hamilton's ledger of what each part's outline, sizes and place rest on (the frame or camera each reading went through, how sure, what is left, and the order of work; 18 October 2026, superseding `SHAPE-PASS.md`'s ledgers): update a part's row when it is re-measured.

## Build / run

From the repository root:

    python build.py                          # the model (with its Essay tab), the root copy, site/
    python build.py --site-url https://...   # also adds canonical + og:image tags to site/index.html (or set SITE_URL)
    python build.py --site-url https://... --keep-html   # for hosts that don't redirect /page.html to /page
    python build.py --release patch -m "what changed" [-m ...] --site-url https://...   # a release: the next version in CHANGELOG.md, then the build

The live site is https://www.marinechronometermodel.com, a Cloudflare Worker serving `site/` (`wrangler.jsonc`); `worker.js` redirects the bare domain, the old workers.dev address and plain http to it, and the old addresses of the Wempe and the essay (`/wempe/`, `/marine-chronometer`) to theirs (above). Pushing doesn't deploy it: release (below), commit, then `python deploy.py`.

Versions: the site's version (`M.mm.pp`, shown with the list of changes in the panel's last section, Version … · what's new, and in About's byline) is the top entry of the root `CHANGELOG.md`, which `changelog.py` reads, checks and renders into the page at build time. A plain build never changes it, so `ci.py`'s rebuild matches. Every deploy is a release: build with `--release patch` (fixes, small changes), `minor` (new features or parts, a merged branch) or `major`, one `-m` per line of what changed, written for visitors, then commit `CHANGELOG.md` with the builds and deploy with `python deploy.py`. It is enforced: `deploy.py --check`, which `wrangler.jsonc` runs before every `wrangler deploy`, rebuilds with the live address and refuses uncommitted or stale builds, a version not above the live page's (unless the page is byte for byte the same), and a version tag already on another commit; `deploy.py` then tags the commit `vM.mm.pp` (push it with `--follow-tags`). Release and deploy each finished change as soon as the checks it calls for pass, without asking first: choose the kind and the lines yourself, then say what went live (the owner's rule, 21 October 2026); a failing check stops the release and is reported. After each release, bring the root `README.md` up to date with what the release changed (the pages and what they offer, the layout, setup, the build, the checks, publishing), in the same commit as the release or the next, and never leave it describing what the repository no longer does (the owner's rule, 27 October 2026).

Merging a branch into `main`: only after the checks its change calls for pass on it, chosen by judgment, not by habit (below, Verification tools); always `git merge --no-ff`, so every branch lands as its own merge commit (never a fast-forward or a squash). Resolve conflicts in the built copies and `site/` by rebuilding, not by hand.

Every local branch lives on GitHub too, merged or not: push a new branch with `git push -u origin BRANCH` as soon as it has its first commit, and push again after every commit on it (and `main` after every merge, with `--follow-tags`). Never leave a local branch without its `origin` copy or behind it; `git for-each-ref --format='%(refname:short) %(upstream:short) %(upstream:track)' refs/heads` should list every branch with an upstream and no `[ahead N]`.

Open `marine-chronometer-source/chronometer-working-model/index.html` directly in a browser for development. URL flags:

## Working-model architecture

- The scripts are classic (non-module) scripts sharing globals, loaded in order `report.js` → `core.js` → `mainspring.js` → `../shared/escapement.js` → `../shared/escplan.js` → `../shared/almanac.js` → `../shared/hairspring.js` → `../shared/contacts.js` → `../shared/select.js` → `movement.js` → `contactdata.js` → `box.js` → `makers.js` → `maker.js` → `overhaul.js` → `gltf.js` → `rigpy.js` → `blender.js` → `app.js`. `mainspring.js` declares only `MSHAPE` (the mainspring's solved states, generated by `tools/mainspring.py`: run it after changing the spring's length, thickness, core, barrel or its measured free curve; `--write` rewrites it from the last solve); `shared/escapement.js` declares only `makeEsc`, `escplan.js` only `drawEscPlan` and `escStage`, `almanac.js` only `ALM`, `hairspring.js` only `HSPR` and `contacts.js` only `CONTACTS` (each keeps its own `TAU`/`D2R` inside); `contactdata.js` declares only `HCONTACT` (generated by `tools/contacts.py --write` from `fine.py`'s tables: run it after changing them), `makers.js` only `MAKERS` (generated by `tools/makers.py`: run it after changing `bom.json` or `BOM.md`) and `maker.js` only `MAKER` (the maker's sheets, drawings, STL, Measure, build book; app.js calls `MAKER.bind()`). `overhaul.js` declares only `OVH` (the overhaul: Sec. V's and Sec. VIII's operations, each part moved off or on in its turn; app.js calls `OVH.bind()` and `OVH.frame()` each frame after the Exploded view's offsets; the model README's "The overhaul"). `report.js` declares only `REPORT` (Report a bug or inaccuracy: the dialog, and the page's last errors and warnings, which is why it loads first; app.js calls `REPORT.bind()`; nothing leaves the page but through the visitor's own email). `gltf.js` declares only `GLTF` (a glTF 2.0 writer), `blender.js` only `BLENDER` (the export to Blender: its own copy of the model, every motion played through it; app.js calls `BLENDER.bind()` with `config`, which sets the copy as the page is) and `rigpy.js` only `RIG_PY` (generated by `tools/rigpy.py` from `blender/rig.py`, the Blender rig: run it after changing the rig; the model README's "The model in Blender"). `core.js` defines shared helpers (`TAU`, `clamp`, `lerp`, `smooth`, `$`, `sc`, materials, geometry builders) used by the later files; load order matters.
- `essay/js/essay.js` (the essay's own page) is one IIFE that declares only `ESSAY` (`on`, `section`, `model`, `edges`): everything else in it is private. The page loads the Hamilton's `report.js` and `core.js` and the Wempe's model's scripts (`shared/kit.js`, `wempe/js/` from `dims.js` to `movement.js`, `essaydata.js`), not the Hamilton's `movement.js` (both declare `ESC`); it builds the Wempe's movement once after its first frame and lends it to each 3D figure as it draws (`lend`: shown in the figure's scene, only its parts visible, posed by `update(s)`, colours swapped and put back). Its 3D figures share one WebGL renderer off screen, copied onto each figure's 2D canvas, so the page has one context; smoke.py checks it.
- Units are millimetres. Movement frame: dial side +y, 12 o'clock −z, 3 o'clock +x. Arbor positions live in the `L` constant at the top of `movement.js`; they come from the photo-fitting pipeline, so don't move them casually. The photographed group (balance, fusee, barrel, and everything traced on the top-view photograph with them: pillars, bridges, cock, screws, engraving, damascening) is placed against the dial and train by one similarity, `PT()` (×`PHOTO_K` 1.039, turned `PHOTO_TURN` 19.42°, shifted `PHOTO_T`; `PTi()` back), fitted so the balance, fusee and barrel land where the real movements measure them (IDEAS.md 1.12): `L` holds the results, a new constant traced on that photograph goes through `PT()` (outlines running to the rim through `PTr()`), one measured on the video in the train bridge's own frame through `PR()` (the 14° turn alone), and sizes read against the fusee wheel scale by `FK`. The main heights are the constants beside it (`TB_U`, `TB_T`, `BB_T`, `CK_T`, `EY`, `LB_T`, `BAL_Y`), measured on a side photograph; the README's layout section says which heights are measured and which are fitted for clearance.
- Single model clock `tSim`. Balance phase `p = frac(tSim/0.5)`; `ESC.state(p)` returns balance angle, detent lift, trip-spring deflection and escape-wheel tooth progress. Escape-wheel position `E` (in teeth) drives every other arbor by a fixed ratio, so hands step in half-seconds. The movement's per-frame `mv.userData.update({E, th, lift, psDef, ...})` applies this state.
- `app.js` rebuilds the environment map on `webglcontextrestored`: a restored context loses PMREM render targets, which leaves every metal flat and dark.
- Screws: `screw(parent,x,z,y,r,h,len,thread)` draws head and threaded shank; each part the shank passes through gets a clearance hole (`hC`), the part it holds a tapped hole (`hT`), from the positions in the `S` table at the top of `buildMovement`. A shank without its hole shows in `fine.py` as a new overlap.
- The balance's amplitude is state (`H.amp` in `app.js`): below `ESC.AMIN` the escapement can't unlock and the train holds, as it does at run down, with the balance locking arm locked or with the train-blocking screw's dog point against a spoke; a stopped chronometer needs Twist to start (Sec. III). It follows the balance's equation of motion averaged over a swing: A² relaxes to `ESC.ampAt(drive)²` as exp(−2t/TF), the drive being the fusee's torque, the sustaining spring's while winding, or none; the escapement's rate at that swing (`ESC.rateAt`) is `escK` in the running rate (model README, "The balance's dynamics").
- Every part is a closed solid facing out (like a CAD model): build extrusions with `extrude()` (never `THREE.ExtrudeGeometry` directly: r128 winds a hole's wall into the metal under a clockwise outline), close lathe profiles or run them to the axis at r 0, and cap anything else left open (tubes, part-turned lathes and tori) with `closeGeo()` (or, for a tube rebuilt as the model moves, `reclose(old, g)`, which rewrites the old closed geometry in place), all in `core.js`; no `DoubleSide` or `noCap` on a new part. The only surfaces are flagged `userData.decal` (engravings) or `userData.surface` (the dial's printed face, the floor's shadow). A section's cut faces are the solids' back faces, hatched by `patchSection` and drawn 0.015 mm nearer (`SEC_DEPTH`) so a part lying on the cut one doesn't show through.
- Static pieces are drawn merged (`drawMerge` in `core.js`, synced each `paint()`; Display's Performance mode, on by default, `merge=0` in the hash when off): pieces under one group sharing a material draw as one copy kept outside `__mv`, while they're all shown and wear `mat0`; a piece that moves against its group or is rebuilt leaves its batch. Merged pieces sit on layer 2, so a new raycaster over the model enables layer 2 (or all); r128's shadow pass tests layers against the main camera, so the copy casts for its pieces. The model README ("Static pieces drawn merged") has the rest.
- Parts are tagged with `userData.partName` (used for picking/descriptions and by the interference check); a part that is several things has pieces (`PIECES` in `app.js`, keys `part.k`, found from each mesh's `userData.hn` or a `pc()` tag), which the page picks, describes, colours and hides on their own while every tool still works by part; `userData.noCap` / `noShadow` / `mat0` affect cross-sections and collision filtering.
- The code is written in a dense, compact style (long one-line statements, short names). Match it when editing.
- Types without npm: `jsconfig.json` (the scripts in load order, for VS Code and `ci.py`) and `js/globals.d.ts` (three.js as `any`, `makeEsc`). `core.js`, `movement.js`, `box.js` and the two shared scripts start with `// @ts-check` and must type-check (`npx -p typescript@5.9.3 tsc -p marine-chronometer-source/chronometer-working-model/jsconfig.json`, about 10 s; `ci.py` runs it); `app.js` and `essay.js` aren't checked yet. The main surfaces carry JSDoc types: `makeEsc`'s settings (`EscSettings`, so a misspelt setting is an error), `ESC.state`'s result (`EscState`), `update()`'s argument (`MoveState`), `arbor`'s options, `part` and `screw`. Where inference fails in the dense code, a `/** @type {…} */(…)` cast says what a value is; never change the code to please the checker.

- `mfgfit.py fit TRACE.json [--map MAP.json | --calib HOLES.json --link a=h1,...] [--image IMG] [--centres DATUMS.json|L] [--holes HOLES.json] [--floor PX]` (no browser; numpy, scipy, opencv, matplotlib): a traced part measured as machined, the engine of the `measure-trace` skill (`.claude/skills/measure-trace/SKILL.md`, the method). It calibrates on holes by their circles (never the ellipse centres), splits each edge into lines and arcs by dynamic programming on BIC (hyper-accurate circle fits) under machining priors (a flat arc only about a datum, nothing under two blurs), fits everything at once by geometric least squares, and tests the machining's relations, keeping each only where BIC allows: tangency, fillets, concentric with a datum or each other, equal radii, parallel, perpendicular, radial, a standard drill (fractional, number, letter, metric) when told apart.
